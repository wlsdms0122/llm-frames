# FRAMES-11: Titles, shapes, media, adjustment and null clips

Status: accepted

## Summary

The clip kinds that draw on a sequence: titles with styled runs, the three shapes (rect, ellipse and path, whose segments are cubic Bézier curves) with fill, stroke, gradients, dashes, arrowheads and trim, media clips (picture in picture and graphics made elsewhere), adjustment clips, null clips, which draw nothing and carry a transform for other clips, the masks and track mattes any picture takes, and blend modes.

## Motivation

Titles and shapes are the basics of any NLE. A lower third is a name and a role in two styles over a bar, an end card is a title in a box, a diagram is lines and arrows drawn on one by one, a spotlight darkens everything but a region. Each of these is made from a few general tools: text in runs, a rect, an ellipse and a path of straight and curved segments with a stroke model, and a mask. Their geometry belongs to frames, curves included: a curved arrow that draws on along a diagram, a dashed route on a map and a mask that hugs a face are one path each, and its stroke's joins, its trim by length, its arrowheads, its dashes, its keyframed shape, its box and the masks it cuts all need the curve itself, which no tool can give a path of points without flattening it once at one scale, how they move is a preset, and how they look is a theme style. Masks and track mattes cut any picture to a shape or to another clip, as they do in After Effects and Premiere Pro, which is how a logo is cut to a circle, a filter is kept to one region, or text is filled with video.

## Rules

### Titles

- A title's `text` is a string or a list of runs `{text, style}`. A string is one run in the clip's style. Its line endings, CR LF, CR and the other line endings of UAX #14, are read as `\n` before anything else reads its text ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#runs)). A run's `style` names a theme text style whose character attributes (font, size, weight, italic, tracking, kerning, features, baseline shift, colour, stroke, highlight, underline, text orientation and horizontal-in-vertical composition) apply to that run, with the clip's `character` merged over them, and the paragraph attributes (placement, offset, writing mode, direction, align, line height, lines, background) come from the clip's `style`, with the clip's `paragraph` merged over them. Runs are laid out as [FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md) says.
- `style` names a theme text style ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)) and is required, since which kind of text a title is belongs to the document.
- `character` is an object of character attributes, the character keys of a text style (`font`, `size`, `weight`, `italic`, `tracking`, `kerning`, `features`, `baselineShift`, `color`, `stroke`, `highlight`, `underline`, `textOrientation`, `textCombineUpright`, [FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)), as After Effects' Character panel sets them on a text layer. It merges over every run's style leaf by leaf, as every layer of a look merges ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)), so `character: {"color": "accent"}` colours every run and keeps each run's size and weight. Its lengths are theme lengths, as a style's are ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), and the clip's line height, when its style and its `paragraph` leave it to its default, follows the size of the clip's style with `character` merged over it.
- The attributes that decide the text's faces, shaping and line breaks (`font`, `size`, `weight`, `italic`, `kerning`, `features`, `textOrientation`, `textCombineUpright`) are static, since resolve makes those decisions once ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), and a keyframe's `character` that holds one stops. The others take keyframes ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)): `color`, `stroke`, `baselineShift`, which raises only its own run's glyphs, and `highlight` and `underline`, which take no part in layout, so a title's colour, a highlight's padding or an underline's offset changes as one value on one clip. `tracking` takes keyframes too, with its lines broken once at its largest value and its glyphs placed along them at each instant ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)). A title's size changes through its `scale` ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)).
- `paragraph` is an object of paragraph attributes, `writingMode`, `direction`, `align`, `lineHeight` and `background` as a text style takes them ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)), as After Effects' Paragraph panel sets them on a text layer. It merges over the paragraph attributes of the clip's style leaf by leaf, as `character` merges over the runs' styles, and its lengths are theme lengths. `writingMode`, `direction` and `align` are names and static, since resolve sets, orders and places the lines once ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#direction), [FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#writing-mode)), and a title turns from horizontal to vertical with `paragraph: {"writingMode": "vertical-rl"}`, as After Effects turns a text layer between horizontal and vertical. `lineHeight` and the background's `color`, `padding` and `radius` take keyframes ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), since no line break depends on them: evaluation places the lines at the line height of each instant and draws the background with its values then ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), so leading that opens as a title enters, or a box whose padding grows around a name, is one value on one clip. `placement`, `offset` and `lines` stay the style's: a title is placed by its own `placement` or `position`, and `lines` is the limit its style sets.
- `width`, in output pixels, is the width of the title's text box, and the text wraps at it. Each line is placed inside that width by its `align`, and its background fills it. It is the title's inline size, the length of its lines, so in a vertical writing mode it is the height of its text box and its lines wrap at it ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#writing-mode)), and a document keeps its `width` when its theme turns its text. A title without `width` does not wrap: its lines break only at `\n`, and its text box is as wide as its widest line ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)).
- A title's box is its text box, grown by its background padding ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), each at the instant it is read. It is placed by `placement` or `position` about its anchor point ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), or by `cornerPin`, the four points its box's corners go to ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)).

### Shapes

A shape is a clip of kind `rect`, `ellipse` or `path`.

| shape | Geometry | Keys of its own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rect` | a box: `area`, or `width` and `height` placed as a box | `radius`, `outset` |
| `ellipse` | a box, as for `rect`: the ellipse inscribed in it | `outset` |
| `path` | `points`, a list of at least two vertices ([Paths](#shapes)), joined in order by cubic Bézier segments, straight where a segment's tangents are 0 | `closed` (default `false`), which joins the last vertex to the first |

- A rect or ellipse takes exactly one of `area` (an area in its coordinate space, [FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)) and the pair `width` and `height` (output pixels, both needed). Given `width` and `height`, the box is placed by `placement` or `position` about its `anchor`, as a media clip's box is ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), or by `cornerPin` ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)). A shape given by `area` or `points` is placed by its geometry ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)).
- `radius` rounds a rect's corners with arcs, circular in the shape's own coordinates and carried with the outline, so a per-axis scale draws them elliptical ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). It is a length, the radius of every corner, or a list of four, `[topLeft, topRight, bottomRight, bottomLeft]`, the order CSS `border-radius` writes them, so each corner takes its own radius as a design tool rounds corners one by one; a list of another length stops. The corners are those of the box in the shape's own coordinates, its top left at its least x and y, so a mirroring scale carries each corner with its sides. **Where the radii of a side's two corners add up to more than the side, every radius is scaled by one factor**, the least over the four sides of the side's length over the sum of its two radii, as CSS keeps corner curves from overlapping, so the corners keep their proportions and one radius for every corner is cut to half the box's shorter side. A rect's `radius` is the one meaning of `radius` wherever a box takes it: a shape style's, a media clip's and a storyline picture's box, a rect mask, and a text background and highlight ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)). `outset` grows the outline a rect or an ellipse draws on every side, before the corners are rounded, and takes no part in placing it. Both are theme lengths ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), so they keep their size under a parent's zoom.
- A key a shape kind does not take stops: `radius` on an ellipse or a path, `outset`, `area`, `width` or `height` on a path, `points` or `closed` on a rect or an ellipse.
- A shape's box ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)) is its box as written for a rect or an ellipse, `width` by `height` or its `area`, and for a path the bounding box of its outline, the curve its segments draw and not their control points, the box SVG gives a curve and Core Graphics' `boundingBoxOfPath`. Each coordinate's extremes on a segment lie at its ends or where its derivative, a quadratic, is 0, so the box is exact. `placement`, `position`, `anchor`, the space its children see ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), an animation's `x` and `y` ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)) and a gradient's fractions all take that box, so they follow what is drawn. `outset` grows only the outline it draws, and so what it draws: its receiver box and its content-fit extent measure the grown outline.

**Paths.** A path's `points` are its vertices, and each vertex carries an in tangent and an out tangent, as After Effects and Lottie write a path (vertices, in tangents and out tangents):

| Vertex form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[x, y]` | a vertex at that point, both its tangents `[0, 0]` |
| `{"vertex": p, "inTangent": [dx, dy], "outTangent": [dx, dy]}` | a vertex at the point `p`, in any form a point takes where it is written ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), a point of an area included. `inTangent` and `outTangent` default to `[0, 0]` |

- An object in `points` is a vertex object, so a vertex at a point of an area writes that point as its `vertex`: `{"vertex": {"area": {"clip": "name"}, "point": "right"}}` is the vertex at the right edge of the clip `name`'s box. A key a vertex object does not take stops, `area` and `point` written in place of `vertex` included.
- The segment from a vertex A to the next vertex B is the cubic Bézier curve from A's point to B's, its control points A's point plus A's `outTangent` and B's point plus B's `inTangent`, as After Effects' and Lottie's are. A tangent of `[0, 0]` puts its control point on its vertex, so a segment whose two tangents are 0 is the straight segment between its vertices, and a path of points is joined by straight segments.
- A closed path's closing segment, from the last vertex to the first, takes the last vertex's `outTangent` and the first vertex's `inTangent`. An open path has no segment into its first vertex or out of its last, and its fill closes it with a straight segment ([Appearance](#shapes)), so the `inTangent` of its first vertex and the `outTangent` of its last shape nothing it draws. They are values of their vertices all the same, as After Effects keeps them on an open path, so a path means the same vertices whether `closed` is `true` or `false`, and a template whose `closed` is a choice writes one list of vertices for both.
- A tangent is an offset from its vertex, two numbers in the coordinate space its points are in. It is part of the outline, as a point is, and no theme length, so it follows the shape's scale and its parent's map with the points ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)). It is a direction and a length, not a place, so it takes no form of [FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md): an area or a clip area in a tangent stops. A point that carries tangents is a path's vertex, and a vertex object where a key takes a point (a `position`, a point of a `cornerPin`) stops.
- Segments are cubic Béziers alone. A quadratic Bézier with control point Q is the cubic with control points at two thirds of the way from each end to Q, exactly, and a circular arc of a quarter turn or less is the cubic whose tangents are tangent to the circle and (4/3) × tan(a / 4) × r long, for a turn of a and a radius r, within 2.73e-4 × r of it, so an author writes both as tangents. A circle is an ellipse ([Shapes](#shapes)).
- A vertex where the outline arrives and leaves in one direction ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#paths)) is smooth, as Illustrator's smooth point is, and the stroke turns through it without a corner: two tangents that point in opposite directions make one. Every other vertex is a corner, and takes the stroke's `join`, as does a cusp inside a segment.
- `points` keyframes ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)) time each vertex's point and each of its tangents as leaves of their own: a point as a point and a tangent per component. A vertex written as `[x, y]` names its point and nothing more, so its tangents come from below: statically `[0, 0]`, and in a keyframe the clip's static value or its other keyframes. A vertex object in a keyframe may leave out any of its keys, its `vertex` included, which then come from below as well, so `{}` there keeps the vertex below, while a static one writes its `vertex`. So a path of points eases into a curve with keyframes that write only tangents, and keyframes that move only the points keep its tangents, as After Effects keeps a vertex's handles when the vertex is moved.

**Appearance.**

| Key | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fill` | a colour, a gradient ([Gradients](#shapes)), or `false` | the inside of the outline painted with that colour or gradient, as SVG's `fill` takes a paint. A path is filled as if it were closed, an open one by a straight segment from its last vertex to its first, by the nonzero rule, as SVG fills it. `false` turns off a style's fill |
| `stroke` | `{paint, width, align, join, cap, start, end, dash, dashOffset}`, or `false` | the outline drawn as a line on the side of it `align` names. `false` turns off a style's stroke |
| `trim` | `[a, b]`, each from 0 to 1, default `[0, 1]` | the part of the outline the stroke draws, as After Effects' Trim Paths |
| `shadow` | `true` (on, with its style's shadow, or the theme `shadow` where the style gives none), `false`, or `{radius, offset, color}` whose missing keys come from the theme `shadow` | a drop shadow of the shape's drawn picture ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)) |
| `style` | a shape style's name | defaults for `fill`, `stroke`, `radius`, `outset` and `shadow` ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)) |

| Stroke key | Form | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `paint` | a colour or a gradient ([Gradients](#shapes)), as `fill` takes them: what the stroke and its arrowheads are painted with | the theme `accent` colour |
| `width` | a theme length above 0 ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), 0 or more where two keyframes or more write it ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), where a width of 0 draws no stroke and no arrowhead | required |
| `align` | `inside`, `center` or `outside`: the side of the outline the stroke's width lies on, inside it, centred on it with half on each side, or outside it, as Illustrator's Align Stroke and Figma's `strokeAlign` place a stroke | `center` |
| `join` | `miter`, `round` or `bevel`, as SVG `stroke-linejoin`, with a miter limit of 4 as SVG's | `round` |
| `cap` | `butt`, `round` or `square`, as SVG `stroke-linecap` | `butt` |
| `start`, `end` | `none`, the name of a head preset ([Heads](#shapes)), or `{preset, scale}`: `preset` that name or `none`, default `none`, and `scale` a number above 0 (0 or more where two keyframes or more write it, [FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), default 1, the size of the head in times its size at 1. A name is `{"preset": name}`, naming the preset alone | `none` |
| `dash` | a list of theme lengths, 0 or more and not all 0: the lengths of the dashes and the gaps between them in turn, as SVG `stroke-dasharray` | none: the stroke is drawn whole |
| `dashOffset` | a theme length: how far into the pattern the stroke starts, as SVG `stroke-dashoffset` | 0 |

- A stroke's `paint` is one value of two forms, a colour and a gradient, as a fill is. A stroke whose layers together write no `paint` is drawn in the theme `accent` colour. `paint` is the key of a paint that may be a gradient, and `color` the key of one colour wherever it is written: a text stroke, a border, a shadow, a highlight and an underline each take a `color` and no gradient ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)).
- A `center` stroke is `width` wide, centred on the outline. An `inside` or an `outside` stroke is the `center` stroke of twice its width, cut to the inside of the outline or to its outside, the inside being the region a fill of the outline paints by the nonzero rule, whether or not the shape has a fill. So an `inside` stroke reaches nothing past the outline, an `outside` one covers nothing of the fill, and each lies `width` deep on its side. Its joins, caps and dashes are those of the wider stroke, cut with it: an `outside` stroke's `round` join rounds a corner of the outline to a radius of `width`, its `miter` join keeps a square corner square, as a design tool draws a stroke outside, and the miter limit is the same ratio of the wider stroke's width. A corner on the side the cut takes away keeps the sharp edge the cut leaves, and the caps of a trimmed piece and of each dash are the wider stroke's, cut. `inside` and `outside` need a closed outline and a stroke without arrowheads: on an open path, whose sides enclose nothing, or with an arrowhead at either end, which lies across the outline with its tip at the end while such a stroke lies on one side of it, so the head would end no line, either stops. `align` is a name, static as `join` and `cap` are, so a keyframe that writes it stops ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)).
- An end other than `none` is an arrowhead, the line ending of design tools (Figma's stroke ends, Illustrator's and PowerPoint's arrowheads), drawn in the stroke's paint in units of u = `width` × `scale`. On every end a head is its preset's outline moved, turned and grown by one factor along both axes, never stretched along one: the one such map that puts its tip at that end of the drawn stroke and its base, the point `length` back from the tip on its axis, on the drawn stroke `length` × u back along it. Its axis runs from its base to its tip, and its size follows the chord between them, so its shape is the same on every end, as it is at every scale and on a short stroke. On a straight end the chord is `length` × u long and the head is its outline at u to a unit. On a curved one the chord is shorter, its tip and its base both lie on the curve, and the head is smaller by the chord over `length` × u. Where its tip and its base are one point the head is of size 0 and draws nothing. Its tip is the foremost of what it draws, so no head reaches past the end of the outline along it. The line stops inside the head, `inset` back from the tip at the size the head is drawn, so `inset` × u on a straight end, with its cap there ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#paths)).
- **Heads.** A head is a preset of the kind `head`, one JSON file at `presets/head/<name>.json` in a pack, named as every preset is, `<pack>/<name>` or an absolute path ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-17](../FRAMES-17/SPEC.md)). frames ships six in the pack `frames`, `frames/arrow` among them ([FRAMES-16](../FRAMES-16/SPEC.md)). `none` names no preset: it is the end with no head. A name that finds no head preset stops at `resolve`, as an unknown preset does. A head preset is data in units of u, the head at scale 1 on a stroke 1 wide:

| Key | Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `description` | text | how the head looks, which `presets` and `frames packs` print. Required |
| `points` | a list of at least two vertices ([Paths](#shapes)), each `[x, y]` or a vertex object `{vertex, inTangent, outTangent}` whose `vertex` is `[x, y]`, all of plain numbers | the head's outline, always closed and filled by the nonzero rule, in the head's own coordinates: its tip at the origin, its axis along the x axis from its base at x = -`length` to its tip, and y across the axis, positive to the right of the direction the head points, as y lies below x in a picture. Required |
| `length` | a number above 0 | how far back along the stroke from the tip its base lies, which is how much of the stroke the head takes. Required |
| `inset` | a number from 0 to `length` | how far back from the tip, along the stroke, the stroke's line stops. Required |

- A head preset takes no other key, and so no `params`: a head's size is its stroke's width and its `scale`. Every point of its outline and every control point of its segments has x ≤ 0, so nothing of it lies past its tip, and one with x above 0 stops at `read`, as does a `length` of 0 or less or an `inset` outside 0 to `length`. A head's vertex is a point in u, not a place, so an area, a text form or a clip area in it stops.
- A head at scale s is the head at scale 1 grown by s about its tip, every part of it growing with it, as Illustrator scales an arrowhead. Where the drawn stroke is shorter than its heads' lengths together, both heads shrink by one factor, each keeping its shape, so the heads together take the stroke's length. Arrowheads sit on the ends of the stroke as drawn: an open path's stroke always has two ends, and a rect's, an ellipse's or a closed path's has them only when its `trim` leaves part of the outline undrawn. A whole closed outline has no ends and draws no arrowhead. An end's `preset` is a name, static as `join` and `cap` are, so a keyframe that writes it stops, and its `scale` is a number that keyframes as `width` does ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)): a head of scale 0 draws nothing and stops no line, so a head grows in from scale 0.
- `dash` lays dashes along the stroke as drawn, from its start after `trim`: a dash of the first length, a gap of the second, and so on, the list taken twice over when its length is odd, as SVG does, and the pattern repeated to the stroke's end. `dashOffset` moves the pattern back along the stroke by its length, so keyframing it moves the dashes along the outline. Each dash takes `cap` at both its ends and `join` at the corners inside it. Arrowheads are drawn whole over a dashed stroke, and the line stops where they stop it as an undashed one does. A `dash` that is empty, holds a negative length or holds only zeros stops, in a keyframe as in a static value: a pattern of zeros draws the stroke whole, as SVG draws one, while the patterns beside it draw fine dots, so it is not where a dashed stroke changes continuously into a whole one. A gap of 0 is: `[d, 0]` draws the stroke whole for any `d`, and a dashed stroke eases into a whole one by keyframing its gaps to 0. A sampled pattern an ease carries to all zeros draws the stroke whole.
- `trim` measures the outline as a fraction of its length: a rect from its top left corner (for a rounded rect, where the top edge leaves the corner's arc), clockwise; an ellipse from its top, clockwise; a path from its first vertex along its segments, the closing segment last when it is closed. Its length is the arc length of the outline, so a trim and a dash fall at a distance along the curve, as After Effects' Trim Paths measures a curved path. These directions are in the shape's own coordinates, so a mirrored shape draws on mirrored. The stroke is drawn from the smaller of `a` and `b` to the larger, and `a` equal to `b` draws no stroke. A trimmed outline has ends, which take `cap` and its arrowheads. `trim` acts on the stroke only, and the fill is drawn whole. It is keyframable, which draws a stroke on or off.
- A key written on the clip, its static value with the structure its keyframes give ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), merges over its style's value leaf by leaf, as every layer of a look merges ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)): the style gives defaults and the clip changes only what it writes. So a clip's `stroke: {"paint": "#FF0000"}` takes its style's `width`, `join` and `cap`, and a theme that thickens the style's stroke thickens this one too; a colour `fill` or stroke `paint`, written statically or only in keyframes, replaces a style's gradient there, and a gradient a style's colour; and `false` turns a style's value off. A stroke whose clip and style together give no `width` stops. A shape without `style` takes nothing from the theme but colour names and the theme `shadow` for `true`. A shape that, after its style, has neither a fill nor a stroke stops, since it would draw nothing.
- `scale` and `rotation` act about the shape's anchor point ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). The stroke width and the shadow scale with the shape's own and its animation's scale by its scale factor, the same in every direction, and `radius`, `outset` and the dash lengths are part of the outline and follow its scale along each axis ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). None of them grows with a parent's scale ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)).
- A shape's `points`, `area`, `width`, `height`, `radius`, `outset`, `fill`, `stroke` and `shadow` keyframe leaf by leaf as [FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md) says, each timed leaf sampled over the clip's static value merged over its style's, so keyframes that write only a stroke's colour move the colour of the style's stroke while its width, `join` and `cap` stay, and a gradient's kind and number of stops are static while its points, offsets and colours move. A `points` keyframe interpolates vertex by vertex ([Paths](#shapes)), so every `points` value of one shape has the same number of vertices, and a keyframe with another number stops.

**Gradients.** A gradient is a paint that changes colour across the shape, as CSS's and SVG's linear and radial gradients do:

| Form | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| `{"kind": "linear", "from": p, "to": p, "stops": [...]}` | `from` and `to`, points in fractions of the shape's box |
| `{"kind": "radial", "center": p, "radius": r, "stops": [...]}` | `center`, a point in fractions of the shape's box, and `radius`, a number above 0 (0 or more where two keyframes or more write it, [FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), default 1: a fraction of the distance from the centre to the farthest corner of the box, as CSS's `farthest-corner` |

- A gradient says which it is with `kind`, `linear` or `radial`, which every gradient writes, in a keyframe as in a static value, and its other keys are those of its kind. A gradient without `kind`, with a `kind` other than these, or with a key its kind does not take stops, `radius` on a linear gradient included.
- A point `[u, v]` of a gradient is `[x + u × w, y + v × h]` of the shape's box `[x, y, w, h]` ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)): `[0, 0]` is its top left corner and `[1, 1]` its bottom right. Fractions outside 0 to 1 lie outside the box. So the gradient follows the box as it is placed, keyframed and parented, and is carried by the shape's transform with its geometry: a radial gradient is a circle in the shape's own coordinates, `radius × D` in radius, where D is the distance from its centre to the box's farthest corner, and is drawn as an ellipse where the shape is scaled per axis. So at `radius` 1 the last stop's colour reaches every corner of the box, on a wide frame and on a tall one alike.
- `stops` is a list of at least two `[offset, colour]`, with offsets from 0 to 1 in order, each at least the one before it. A colour may be a theme colour's name. Fewer than two stops, an offset outside 0 to 1, or an offset below the one before it stops.
- At a point q of the shape's own coordinates the gradient's position is g = ((q - from) · (to - from)) / |to - from|² for a linear gradient, the projection onto the line from `from` to `to`, and g = |q - center| / (radius × D) for a radial one. Its colour at g is the first stop's colour where g is at or below the first offset, the last stop's at or past the last offset, and between two neighbouring stops their colours interpolated at g's place between their offsets with premultiplied alpha, on the encoded form whatever the sequence's `compositing` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing)): each colour's red, green and blue multiplied by its alpha, all four channels interpolated linearly, and red, green and blue divided by the alpha that results (0 where it is 0). A gradient lays colour over space, where alpha is coverage, so a transparent stop adds no colour of its own: a vignette from `#00000000` or from `#FFFFFF00` to black darkens alike, with no grey band. Two stops at one offset make a hard edge there.
- A linear gradient whose `from` and `to` are the same point, or a radial one of radius 0, paints its last stop's colour, as SVG does.
- A gradient in a stroke's `paint` is laid over the same box as a fill's and paints the stroke and its arrowheads wherever they lie, the colours past its ends held as above.
- A gradient is a value with leaves, so it is keyframable ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)): its points, radius, offsets and colours move, and its kind and its number of stops stay. A keyframe's gradient writes the `kind` of the gradient it moves, and one of another kind stops as a value of another form does. Where an ease carries the offsets out of order, each sampled offset is clamped to 0 to 1 and then raised to the one before it, as CSS fixes the positions of its colour stops.
- A shape's `fill` and its stroke's `paint`, and a shape style's, are the only places a gradient stands. Every other colour a clip draws with, a `color` wherever it is written, is one colour.

**Note.** A rect over the frame with a gradient fill is an NLE's gradient generator, and a frame-sized rect with a radial gradient from a clear centre to a dark edge, blended with `multiply`, is a vignette that a template can place and keyframe. frames has no generator clip.

**Note.** To darken everything but a region, draw a rect over the frame filled with a dark colour, with an inverted mask of the region ([Masks and track mattes](#masks-and-track-mattes)). To point at a region, draw a path with `"end": "frames/arrow"` whose last point is a point of that region ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)). A label is a title parented to its shape ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)). A bar that fits its title and moves with it is a rect parented to the title whose `area` is the title's box with padding ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)).

### Media clips

- `width` and `height` size the clip's box in output pixels. Given one, the other follows the aspect ratio of the clip's first crop, or of the media when there is no crop, and every crop conforms to that box, so the box holds still while the crop moves. Given both, the crop conforms to the box by the clip's `conform` (`fit` or `fill`, default `fit`), as a storyline crop conforms to the frame ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)). Given neither, the box is the first crop, or the media, fitted to the frame, as an NLE conforms a picture to its sequence. Under content fit the box given neither is measured as [FRAMES-6](../FRAMES-6/SPEC.md) says.
- `crop` and `filter` work as on a storyline clip, with the clip's own box in place of the frame. `crop` is in media pixels of the clip's own media.
- `radius` rounds the box's corners as a rect's are rounded ([Shapes](#shapes)), a theme length or a list of four, one for each corner, cut as a rect's are. `border` is `true` (the theme `border`, `{width, color, align}`), `false`, a colour, which replaces the theme border's colour, or `{width, color, align}` whose missing keys come from the theme `border`, and draws a border of its width along the edge of the box with its corners rounded, on the side its `align` names as a shape's stroke lies on its outline ([Appearance](#shapes)), with `miter` joins: inside the box by the base theme, its outer edge following the rounded corners, centred on the edge, or outside the box, its outer edge following each corner rounded by that corner's radius plus the border's width and square where that radius is 0. `false` draws none, as no `border` does. `shadow` is `true` (the theme `shadow`), `false`, or `{radius, offset, color}` whose missing keys come from the theme `shadow`, and draws a drop shadow of the box's picture below it. Each length is a theme length ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)). `radius`, `border` and `shadow` are keyframable ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)): a border's width and colour move and its `align` is static, a colour in a keyframe writing the colour alone, and `true` in a keyframe points every leaf at the merged value, the theme's with the clip's static value over it. A storyline clip with media takes the three keys the same way on its frame-sized box ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)).
- A media clip of video, image sequence or compound media takes `timeRemap` ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md)), and every media clip takes `mask` and `trackMatte` ([below](#masks-and-track-mattes)) and `blend` ([Blend modes](#blend-modes)).
- A video media clip is picture in picture, an image sequence or a video with alpha is how a motion graphic made elsewhere comes in, and a compound clip is a window with an edit of its own inside it ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)).

### Adjustment clips

An adjustment clip applies a filter choice (`adjust`, [FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md)) to everything drawn below it in its frame, as an NLE's adjustment layer: over the whole frame, or inside its `mask` when it has one. It takes `trackMatte` as every clip with a picture does ([below](#masks-and-track-mattes)), and its coverage, the part of the frame its filtered result is drawn over, is multiplied by its masks' result and by its matte's value, so an adjustment whose track matte is a clip with a corner pin filters a plane in perspective ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)). Its `opacity` blends the filtered result with what is below, and its `blend` composites the filtered result onto the unfiltered one. Inside a compound clip it reaches only the clips of that compound clip. It takes no keyframed transform: its mask is in the space its parent gives, or in output pixels without one ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), and it moves with its tracked motion and its animation.

### Null clips

A null clip, `{"kind": "null"}`, draws nothing. It is a box with a transform, which other clips follow as their parent ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)) and name in a clip area or a point ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), as an After Effects null object carries the layers parented to it.

- Its box is given as a rect's is ([Shapes](#shapes)): exactly one of `area` and the pair `width` and `height`. Given `width` and `height`, the box is placed by `placement` or `position` about its `anchor`; given `area`, it is placed by its area, and its `anchor` names a point of that area. The stops of a rect's geometry are the null's: both `area` and `width`, a `width` without a `height`, no box at all, and `placement` beside `area`. Its box is as written, with no outset.
- It takes the keys that place and move a clip: `at`, `parent`, `track` ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), `position`, `anchor`, `scale`, `rotation`, `animation`, `motionBlur` ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)), and keyframes of `area`, `width`, `height`, `position`, `scale` and `rotation`. Its transform is a rect's placed the same way ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), and the space it gives its children is a rect's: its box, origin at the box's top left, when placed by `width` and `height`, and the space its area is written in when placed by `area`.
- Its `animation` moves it by the transform part of its spans, `scale`, `rotation`, `x` and `y` on its box ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), which carries its children. The opacity and reveal of a span act on a picture, and a null has none, so they act on nothing. No theme default animation names a null, so an `animation` it does not write is `"none"`, and a choice written as an object without `preset` stops, as there is no default to merge over ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)).
- It takes no key of appearance or compositing: `lane`, `opacity`, `blend`, `mask`, `trackMatte`, `filter`, `style`, `fill`, `stroke`, `shadow`, `radius`, `outset` and `trim` stop as keys it does not take ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md#keys)), and so does `cornerPin`, since a corner pin places a picture on a plane and carries no children ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)).
- It is a connected or a sequence clip, and a storyline clip of kind `null` stops as a kind that place does not take: the storyline shows its clips' pictures one after another, and a null has none to show.
- It is not a clip with a picture ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md)). Where a clip's box and transform are read, a null serves as any clip: as a parent, and as the clip a clip area or a point names. Where a clip's picture is read, naming a null stops: a track matte's `clip` ([below](#masks-and-track-mattes)). It draws no item ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)), so nothing is under a point of it, and content fit does not measure it ([FRAMES-6](../FRAMES-6/SPEC.md)).

### Masks and track mattes

`mask` is taken by every clip with a picture: a storyline clip with media, a media clip, a title, a shape and an adjustment clip. Its value is a mask or a list of masks, combined in list order.

| Mask key | Form | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kind` | `rect`, `ellipse` or `path`, the kinds of a shape clip ([Shapes](#shapes)): which shape the mask's geometry is | required |
| `area` | an area ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), for a rect or an ellipse | |
| `width`, `height`, `position` | a box for a rect or an ellipse: `width` by `height`, its centre at the point `position` | |
| `points` | a list of at least two vertices ([Paths](#shapes)), for a path. The path is always closed | |
| `radius` | the corner radii of a rect, a number or a list of four as a rect shape takes them, in the mask's coordinate space, cut as a rect's are ([Shapes](#shapes)) | 0 |
| `feather` | a theme length, 0 or more: the standard deviation of a Gaussian blur of the mask's edge | 0 |
| `invert` | `true` or `false`: the mask covers everything outside its geometry | `false` |
| `mode` | `add`, `subtract` or `intersect`: how the mask combines with the masks before it | `add` |

- A mask's `kind` decides which geometry keys it takes, and a mask without `kind` stops.
- A rect or ellipse mask takes exactly one of `area` and the box `width`, `height` and `position`, which are all needed together. A path mask takes `points`. Any other geometry key on a mask stops.
- A mask's geometry is in the clip's own coordinate space: for a storyline clip or a media clip, the media pixels of its media; for a title, its text box; for a rect or ellipse placed by `width` and `height`, its box; for a shape placed by `area` or `points`, the space its geometry is written in; for an adjustment clip, its parent's space or output pixels. It is carried with the clip by its own transform, its parent's ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), and its tracked motion ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), so a mask stays on what it cuts. A clip with a corner pin keeps its masks in its own space, and they are carried with its picture onto its corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)). On a storyline clip it cuts the picture before the transition motion moves it ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)), and that motion then carries the cut picture. Its feather is a theme length and does not grow with a parent's scale.
- A mask's value at a point is the coverage of its geometry (1 inside, 0 outside), blurred by its feather, and 1 minus that when inverted. The masks of a clip combine in order, as After Effects combines a layer's masks: the result starts at 0 when the first mask's mode is `add` and at 1 when it is `subtract` or `intersect`, and each mask m then changes the result r to `r + m - r × m` (`add`), `r × (1 - m)` (`subtract`) or `r × m` (`intersect`). The clip's alpha is multiplied by the result.
- `mask` is keyframable leaf by leaf ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)). The clip's `mask` gives its masks in order, each with its `kind`, `invert` and `mode`, which are static, and its geometry, written there or only in keyframes, whose form and number of points are static all the same ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), and a keyframe's `mask` is a list of the same length whose masks write the geometry, `radius` and `feather` they move, merged over the static masks. Between keyframes each mask interpolates its geometry per component (an area per component, a box's `width`, `height` and `position`, a path vertex by vertex as a path shape's `points`) its `radius` per corner as a rect shape's ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md#interpolation)), and its `feather` as a number. A keyframe whose list has another length, or that writes a static leaf of a mask, stops.

`trackMatte` is taken by every clip with a picture, an adjustment clip included: `{clip: "<id>", mode: "alpha" | "luma", invert: false}`. `mode` is required and `invert` defaults to `false`.

- `clip` names, by its `id` ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md)), a connected or sequence media clip, title or shape in the same document. Any other clip named (a storyline clip, an adjustment clip, a null, an audio clip, a clip in another document), or an id that names no clip, stops.
- The named clip is the matte and is not drawn itself. Its picture at t is what it would draw at t (with its transform, parent, animation, opacity, mask and its own track matte, and without its blend), on a transparent frame, and nothing outside its visible window. A media clip's picture there is drawn with its own `crop`, `filter`, `radius`, `border` and `shadow`, so a matte clip graded by its `filter`, as by `levels`, gives the matte of the graded picture.
- `alpha` multiplies the clip's alpha by the matte's alpha. `luma` multiplies it by the matte's luma ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)) clamped to 0 to 1, times its alpha, so a transparent matte counts as black and a part brighter than reference white counts as white. `invert` uses 1 minus that value. The matte is a drawn picture, not an analysis picture, so a matte clip of HDR media weighs as its `toneMap` and the sequence bring its media in ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#notes)).
- The matte cuts the clip where its mask does: on a storyline clip, before the transition motion ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)).
- A clip used as a matte by two clips is fine. A matte that has a track matte of its own is followed, and a chain of track mattes that comes back to a clip already in it stops.

### Blend modes

`blend` composites a clip with a picture with what is below it: a storyline clip with media, a media clip, a title, a shape, or an adjustment clip's filtered result. The modes are those of W3C Compositing and Blending Level 1, `normal` (default), `multiply`, `screen`, `overlay`, `darken`, `lighten`, `color-dodge`, `color-burn`, `hard-light`, `soft-light`, `difference`, `exclusion`, `plus-lighter`, and the non-separable modes `hue`, `saturation`, `color` and `luminosity`, and `add`, the Linear Dodge (Add) of After Effects and Photoshop. A non-separable mode mixes the three channels of a colour together: it is computed with W3C's `Lum` (the weights 0.3, 0.59 and 0.11), `Sat`, `SetLum`, `SetSat` and `ClipColor`. Where `ClipColor` would divide by 0, for a colour whose three channels are equal and lie below 0 or above 1, it returns that colour as it is. `plus-lighter` is W3C's: it adds the two premultiplied colours, co = min(1, cs + cb) and αo = min(1, αs + αb). `add` blends by B(Cb, Cs) = Cb + Cs, without bound, so light adds above reference white: its composite is co = cs + cb, the premultiplied colours, with αo = αs + (1 - αs) × αb. How each mode's formula is read, and in which form, is [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing).

**Every mode is its formula exactly as written, on Cs and Cb as they are**, the bounds W3C writes inside a formula kept as part of it ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#working-values)), as After Effects computes its modes unclamped at 32 bits per channel. So a black source under `screen` or `add`, and a white one under `multiply`, leaves the backdrop as it is at any value.

- `normal` and `add` keep their character above 1 and below 0: Cs and Cb + Cs mean on every real number what they mean on 0 to 1.
- `multiply`, `darken`, `lighten` and `difference` keep their character above 1, where Cb × Cs, min(Cb, Cs), max(Cb, Cs) and |Cb - Cs| mean what they mean on 0 to 1. Below 0, where a wide gamut colour lies on the sRGB primaries of the working values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#working-values)), only `lighten` does. Two negative channels multiply to a positive one, so BT.2020's green, the linear (-0.5876, 1.1329, -0.1006) on the sRGB primaries, under `multiply` with itself gives (0.6397, 1.2042, 0.1277) on BT.2020's primaries under `linear` and (0.6418, 1.2048, 0.1312) under `encoded`: red from two greens. `darken` picks the negative channel of each colour, so BT.2020's green and red darken to the linear (-0.5876, -0.1246, -0.1006), whose luminance, -0.2213, is darker than black, in both forms. `difference` turns a negative channel positive, so black under it, which leaves every colour from 0 to 1 as it is, takes BT.2020's green to (0.7461, 1.0835, 0.1994) on BT.2020's primaries, in both forms. `lighten` is never below either colour in any channel, so it never darkens, but it picks sRGB channels: BT.2020's green and red lighten to (1.4141, 1.1563, 0.1107) on BT.2020's primaries, brighter than BT.2020's yellow, (1, 1, 0), which the same mode gives on those primaries.
- The other modes do not keep their character, as After Effects marks its modes that are not HDR compatible. `screen`, Cb + Cs - Cb × Cs, gives less than a value above 1 wherever the other value is above 0, so `screen` of 1.5 over 1.5 gives 0.75, darker than either, and `overlay`, `hard-light`, `soft-light` and `exclusion`, formulas written for 0 to 1, turn over past it as well. The bounded ones clip: `color-dodge` and `plus-lighter` at 1 and `color-burn` at 0, inside their `min`. A bound of 1 on a channel is reference white for a neutral colour and the edge of the sRGB gamut for a chromatic one, in both forms: P3's red, the encoded (1.0931, -0.2267, -0.1501), under `plus-lighter` over black is cut to (1, -0.2267, -0.1501), the linear (0.8150, -0.0075, -0.0038) on P3's primaries, a darker red towards sRGB's, and as a backdrop under `color-dodge` its red is cut at 1 by every source from 0 to 1. `color-burn`'s 0 lifts a negative channel to the gamut's edge in the same way under every source from 0 to 1. `hue`, `saturation`, `color` and `luminosity` end in `ClipColor`, which takes a colour whose luminosity lies from 0 to 1 into 0 to 1, clipping its HDR and wide gamut values, and turns over one whose luminosity lies outside.

Light leaks and film burns come in this way, as media with `screen`, `plus-lighter` or `add`, where `add` adds the leak's light to an HDR highlight under it, which `screen` would darken and `plus-lighter` clip at 1, and keeps a wide gamut leak's colour, which `plus-lighter` cuts towards sRGB even over black, so a wide gamut light leak uses `add`; and a colour wash as a rect over the frame filled with one colour and blended with `color`, which keeps the luminosity of what is below.

## Alternatives considered

A clip that only carries motion written as a shape: a rect at opacity 0, or a rect with neither a fill nor a stroke. The first says that a rectangle is drawn and hidden, and it is still composited, stacked in a lane and open to a mask and a track matte, all to no effect. The second is the shape that draws nothing, which stops because an unpainted shape is a mistake, and a reader could not tell one written on purpose from one whose style lost its paint. A null says outright that nothing is drawn, takes only the keys that place and move a box, and draws no item. It is a kind of its own, `"kind": "null"`, and not a shape, a clip that takes a style, a fill and a stroke.

Corners cut one by one, each to half the box's shorter side, as one radius is cut: two large corners on one side would still overlap, and a corner would keep its radius while its neighbour's ran past the middle of the side. One factor for every radius, as CSS scales them, keeps the corners from meeting and keeps their proportions, and with one radius for every corner it is the same cut.

Two radii for each corner, one along each axis, as CSS writes `border-radius` with a slash: a corner is part of the outline and is carried with it, so a per-axis scale already draws it elliptical, and a design tool rounds a corner by one radius.

The two- and three-value forms of CSS `border-radius`: each is another spelling of four values, and a list read by its length would give `[a, b]` a meaning other than its two numbers in order. One length or all four leaves one reading, and any other length stops.

A fixed set of annotation shapes (a box, a fill, a dim, an arrow) with labels: each is a rect, a path or a mask with a style, and a fixed set names purposes where frames gives tools. A label side chosen by the engine: a label is a title parented to its shape, placed where the document puts it.

Emphasis markup inside a title's text: a run names its style, so any style can be mixed into a line and no character has to be escaped.

Paths of straight segments only, with curves arriving as media or flattened into points by a tool: a curve drawn as media cannot be trimmed, dashed, given arrowheads, keyframed or used as a mask, and a curve flattened into points is fixed at the scale it was flattened for, so a zoom on its parent shows its facets, its joins fall at every point, and an editor moves hundreds of points where the curve has four control points. What a tool cannot build belongs in frames ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)), and the stroke model needs the curve itself.

SVG path data (`M`, `L`, `C`, `Q`, `A`, `Z`) as a path's form, as a string or as a list of commands: numbers inside a string are no leaves to keyframe ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), a control point written as a place of its own stays behind when its vertex moves by a keyframe or a clip area ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)) and bends the curve, a relative or a horizontal command holds a coordinate that a parent's rotation cannot map alone ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), and the kind of each command would be structure that every keyframe has to repeat. A vertex with its tangents as offsets keeps its handles with it wherever its point goes, and keeps every path of points what it was.

A path's tangents as two lists beside `points`, as After Effects' Shape object writes `vertices`, `inTangents` and `outTangents`: one vertex would be spread over three lists, an inserted vertex inserted at one index in each, and a path with one curved corner would write every other tangent as 0. The display list takes that form ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#paths)), since evaluation writes it whole, and a document keeps a vertex in one place.

Quadratic segments, arcs and smooth or automatic tangents (Illustrator's smooth point, After Effects' RotoBezier): a quadratic is exactly a cubic, an arc of a quarter turn or less is a cubic within 2.73e-4 of its radius, and a smooth vertex is two tangents written opposite, so each would be a second spelling of a cubic. Automatic tangents are a function of the points, which a tool computes and writes.

A path's box as the bounding box of its control points, Core Graphics' `boundingBox`: it holds points nothing is drawn at, so a centred anchor, a gradient's ends and a child's space would lie off the curve, and the box would grow when a tangent lengthens past where the curve reaches.

Arrowheads as any picture, as SVG's `marker` draws a picture at a vertex, placed by a reference point of its own: a picture has a box, an orientation and a point to place it by, and nothing that says where the line stops so that none of it shows past the head, or how much of the stroke the head takes, so frames could not stop the line under it nor shrink it to share a short stroke. A fixed set of heads drawn by frames, as Figma and PowerPoint offer theirs: a pack could not add a head, and a head is a look, which packs carry. A head preset is an outline with its `length` and its `inset`, in units of the stroke's width, and those are what every rule of a head needs: frames lays the outline along the stroke's end, shrinks heads by their lengths and stops the line at the inset without knowing the shape.

A head centred on the end of the stroke, as SVG centres a marker on its reference point and Illustrator extends an arrowhead past the end of its path: half of a circle or a square would lie past the end, so a pointer whose last point is a point of a region would cover what it points at, and a line drawn on by `trim` would show its head ahead of the line. Every head's tip is the end, as Illustrator's arrowheads lie when their tips are placed at the end of the path.

A head's bands and bars kept at the stroke's width at every scale, as Figma draws a line arrow at the stroke's weight: a head's shape would change with its scale, and a head shrunk on a short stroke could not keep its shape. A head's length and width given apart, as PowerPoint's line ends take three widths and three lengths: a head would stretch out of the shape its name gives, and a size not taken from the stroke would not follow a theme that thickens it. A head grown whole by one scale of the stroke's width, as Illustrator scales an arrowhead, keeps its shape and follows the stroke.

A name for an end as the whole end, its scale 1 whatever the style below gives: a shorthand names the leaves it names and nothing more ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)), as a border's colour keeps the width below, so a clip that changes its style's head to another preset keeps the style's size.

An arrowhead along the curve's direction at its tip, as SVG orients a marker with `orient="auto"`: the stroke stops at the head's base, which off a straight end lies beside the curve, so the line would show past one side of the base and leave a gap at the other. A head whose tip and base both lie on the stroke meets the line on a curve as it does on a straight end, as TikZ's flexed arrow tips sit on a bent path.

A head on a curved end stretched along its axis to reach its base and kept u across it: a circle would be drawn as an ellipse and a square as an oblong, so a head's shape would change with the curve under it, while it keeps its shape at every scale and on a short stroke. A head grown by one factor to its chord reaches its base on the curve and keeps its shape, and on a straight end, where the chord is its length, it is the same head.

A path's vertex as a point or one of two objects, a vertex object or a point of an area `{"area": ..., "point": ...}` written in its place, told apart by the keys each holds: a keyframe's vertex object may leave out every key, and `{}` holds none, so a rule would have to choose its form. The vertex object is the one object form in `points`, its `vertex` takes every form a point takes, and `[x, y]` is the vertex without tangents, so no object in `points` needs its form told.

A stroke's paint under two keys, `color` and `gradient`, at most one of them written: one fact would sit under two keys, a layer that writes one would have to drop the other below it, and a stroke that writes neither would take a third meaning from what it lacks. `paint` takes a colour or a gradient as `fill` does, a paint of another form replaces the one below by the look's one rule ([FRAMES-22](../FRAMES-22/SPEC.md)), and `color` means one colour wherever it is written. `paint` is the display list's word for the same value ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)).

A gradient's kind as the one key it holds, `{"linear": {"from": ..., "to": ..., "stops": ...}}`: one key would carry the kind and hold the content one level down, so a gradient would be two objects where it is one value, and its kind would be read by trying each key in turn. `kind` says which gradient it is with one value, as it says what a clip is ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md)), and the gradient's keys stand beside it.

A mask's kind under `shape`: `shape` names the family of the clip kinds `rect`, `ellipse` and `path`, so in a mask it would name one of them and elsewhere all three. A mask writes `kind` with the values a shape clip's `kind` takes, so a mask and the shape it cuts to say the same word.

A path mask of at least three points: two vertices enclose a region once a tangent bends one of the two segments between them, a lens or an eye, and whether a path encloses anything is a matter of its values, which keyframes move, as three points in a line enclose nothing. Two is the fewest that make a segment.

Masks as clips of their own: a mask belongs to the clip it cuts and moves with it, as in After Effects. A clip that shapes another clip's picture is a track matte, which names its matte by id.

Trimming the fill with the stroke, as After Effects trims a whole shape: drawing on is a stroke's, and a fill trimmed with it would change shape as the stroke draws on.

Gradient points in the shape's coordinate space, as SVG's `userSpaceOnUse`: a gradient would stay where it was written while its shape moves or grows, and a template's bar would need its gradient written again for every size. Fractions of the box, as SVG's `objectBoundingBox` and CSS's gradients on an element, follow the shape. A radial radius as a fraction of the distance to the farthest corner keeps the gradient round, where `objectBoundingBox` stretches it over a box that is not square, and reaches the whole box at 1 whatever its shape. A fraction of the box's width would leave the top and bottom of a tall box outside a vignette written for a wide one.

Gradients left to tools, as bands of flat colour stacked or an image made outside and laid in: one ramp would be spread over many clips or baked into media, and its colours, its direction and the box it follows would not be values of one shape ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)).

Inputs of the modes W3C defines on 0 to 1 clamped to 0 to 1: black under `screen`, the ground of a light leak, would press an HDR highlight to reference white over the whole frame the leak covers, and a P3 colour under it to the sRGB gamut, where black under `screen` should change nothing ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#alternatives-considered)). Each mode defined again above 1: formulas no other tool shares. Every mode as W3C writes it, unclamped as After Effects computes its modes at 32 bits per channel, keeps each identity, and the modes that lose their character are named. `plus-lighter` stays W3C's, bound at 1, and `add` is the unbounded sum, as After Effects' and Photoshop's Linear Dodge (Add), for light that should pass reference white.

Every blend function on the encoded form whatever `compositing` is, because W3C writes them on encoded values: `add` would follow `compositing` and `multiply` would not, with nothing in their formulas to tell them apart, and under `linear` the modes that filter and add light would not act on light. A mode whose formula holds a constant whose meaning depends on the form (`overlay`, `hard-light`, `soft-light`, `exclusion` and the non-separable modes) stays on the encoded form, and every other mode follows `compositing`, as After Effects computes its modes in linear light when it blends at gamma 1.0 ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing)).

A stroke's `align` drawn as the outline offset by half the width to one side and stroked centred there: the offset of a cubic is no cubic, and it folds over itself wherever the curve turns tighter than the offset, so it would be approximated and its folds cut away. The stroke twice the width cut by the outline is exact, keeps every join, cap and dash rule of a centred stroke, and is the way a picture's border is drawn inside its box.

An open path's inside taken from its fill, which closes it by a straight segment: the stroke would be cut along a segment it does not draw, ending in a slant at both ends, and which side it lies on would follow from the fill's closing rule and not from the path. A design tool offers the choice for a closed path only.

Arrowheads on an `inside` or `outside` stroke: a head's tip and base lie on the outline and the head lies across both its sides, while a stroke that lies on one side of its outline has no line on the outline for a head to end, so half the head would cover what the stroke leaves bare. A head moved to the middle of the stroke would sit off the outline `trim` measures.

A name shorthand for a border's side, `border: "outside"`: a clip's border already takes a colour, and a theme colour is a name, so `outside` would be a colour in a theme that names one so. An object whose missing keys come from the theme `border` is the form `shadow` takes, and it names the side without a second shorthand.

Dashes laid from the outline's start and uncovered by `trim`: a dashed line drawn on would show the same, but a trim whose start moves would cut a dash anywhere along its length. Laid along the stroke as drawn, as After Effects lays dashes on a trimmed path, the stroke always starts with a whole dash, and `dashOffset` moves the pattern where that is wanted.
