# FRAMES-23: Command line and machine output

Status: accepted

## Summary

The commands, their arguments and output, `loudness` among them, the fixed style of the time labels `still` and `sheet` draw, `transcode` for delivery files, and the machine output that programs outside frames build on.

## Motivation

An agent and the programs it runs drive frames only through commands. Editing is a loop: change the document, look at it with `still`, `sheet` and `timeline`, change it again, render, and use what was rendered as media in the next edit. So the commands that check must be cheap and must say exactly what they saw, and what each command prints and how it fails is the interface. Two implementations that differ in a flag, a label or a field would break every program written against one of them.

## Rules

```
frames [--theme <pack/name | /abs/theme.json>] [--decoder <name>] [--renderer <name>] [--mixer <name>] [--encoder <name>]
       [--shaper <name>] [--tracker <name>] [--ocr <name>] <command> ...

render    <document.json> -o <out.mov | out.mp4 | out.png> [--at <t>] [--deliver <out.mp4>] [--args <args.json>]
          [--report <report.json>] [--progress text|json|none]
still     <document.json> --at <t>[,<t>...] -o <out.png> [--args <args.json>]
resolve   <document.json> -o <resolved.json> [--args <args.json>]
timeline  <document.json> [--at <t>] [--args <args.json>] [--json]
evaluate  <document.json> --at <t> [--args <args.json>]
locate    <document.json> --at <t> --point <x,y> [--args <args.json>]
probe     <media> [--page <n>] [--json]
loudness  <media | document.json> [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--args <args.json>] [--json]
ocr       <still | video | image sequence> [--fps <rate>] [--page <n>] [--at <t> | --from <t> --to <t> --step <s>] [--languages <tag>[,<tag>...]] [--color-space <name>] [--json]
track     <video | image sequence> [--fps <rate>] --at <t> (--area <area> | --quad <quad>) [--to <t>] [--color-space <name>]
extract   <video | image sequence | document.json> <outdir> [--fps <rate>] [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--step 1]
sheet     <video | image sequence | document.json> -o <out.png> [--fps <rate>] [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--step 1] [--cols 6]
transcode <in> [--page <n>] -o <out> [--quality high|medium|low] [--max <px> | --scale <s>] [--codec h264|hevc] [--color-space <name>]
presets   [<kind>] [<name>]
packs     [<pack>]
theme
```

- Every command that takes a document (`render`, `still`, `timeline`, `evaluate`, `locate`, `loudness`, `extract`, `sheet`) also takes a resolved document, which it uses as it is. `render` takes a document, a template with `--args`, or a resolved document. A resolved document with `--theme` or `--args` stops, since both are already written in. A template renders with its defaults when every parameter has one. A template with `--theme` stops, since only its arguments change it, and `--args` with a document that is not a template stops, since it has no parameters to take them.
- `render` writes a sequence to a `.mov` master, to an `.mp4` delivery file alone, or one frame of it to a `.png`: the frame at sequence time `--at`, 0 when it is left out, at the frame's size and without a time label, in the sequence's colour space at 16 bits per channel ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). `--at` lies in `[0, duration)`. `--at` with a `.mov` or an `.mp4` stops, since a video holds every frame. `--deliver` names an `.mp4` and goes with a `.mov` only: with an `.mp4` or a `.png` it stops, since there is no master for it to stand beside. Any other extension stops. `resolve` writes to a `.json` file, compared ignoring case, and any other extension stops before the document is read.
- While `render` draws it writes its progress on standard error, and with `--report` it writes the report of the run to a `.json` file when its last file is closed ([Runs](#runs)). The report's extension is compared ignoring case, and any other extension stops before the document is read, as does a report path that is the output's or the delivery's.
- `still` draws sequence frames at the given sequence times without encoding a video. It is the cheap check while a document is being edited. Several times go side by side, left to right in the order given, each with its time label ([Time labels](#time-labels)).
- `timeline` resolves without rendering and prints, for every storyline clip, its edit range, drawn range, clip range (`[in, out)` on its clip time), its speed or that it is time-remapped, its incoming transition with its length, and its sound range, the range its sound plays over with its `split` applied, or that it plays no sound ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), and for every other clip its visible range, kind, lane, animation, place (`storyline[1].connected[0]`) and parent, and for a visual media clip its sound range as well. Everything resolve stops on while it plans (an unknown preset, a clip that is never visible, a parent chain with a cycle, a bad argument) stops here too. It reads media for its facts, but not a LUT, which resolve reads only when it pins the file and which has no say in time. It writes no file.
- `timeline --at <t>` also gives what every clip's time map gives at sequence time t ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md#the-time-map-at-every-instant)): a storyline clip's clip time, and the media time every clip with media shows there. It gives them whether or not the clip draws anything at t, outside its window, covered, masked away or at opacity 0, since a time map is a fact of a clip at a time and not of what is drawn. t is any sequence time, inside `[0, duration)` or outside it, since a clip's time map is defined at every sequence time and the instants a value takes effect at lie anywhere on it: a keyframe before the first clip's `in` lies before 0, and one past the last clip's `out` after the end.
- `evaluate` prints the display list at sequence time t as machine output, item by item, with the clip or op that drew each, and the text of every title drawn at t, and for a frame with motion blur the list it would have without motion blur, every value at t. `locate` maps a point of the output at t back to the document ([Machine output](#machine-output)). Both stop on `--at` outside `[0, duration)`, as `still` does.
- `probe` prints the kind, size, duration, frame rate, alpha, colour space, and the audio channels and sample rate of its sound ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#rules)). The kind is `image`, `page` (a page of a PDF, [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)), `video`, `audio` or `imageSequence`, which a directory probes as, and the frame rate is the nominal one. A fact the medium does not have is `null`: the size of sound, the duration and frame rate of a still and an image sequence, whose rate is the document's `fps`, and the bits per channel of a page. `alpha` says whether the picture carries an alpha channel. The colour space is, for every kind, the one a clip's `colorSpace` takes where it is not written ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)): for raster media the one the decoder takes the picture in by its tags ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces)), the name of the table row its tags match, `srgb` when it is untagged, and none when it is tagged outside the table. With it come the H.273 primaries, transfer and matrix the picture is tagged with, the bits per channel the file stores, whether it is HDR, which a transfer of PQ or HLG (16 or 18) makes it, and the conversion by which the decoder takes its pictures to working values where they are read by its tags, the value a run's report gives the medium ([Runs](#runs)). An image sequence reports its first frame's.
- `--page <n>` names a page of a PDF for `probe`, `ocr` and `transcode`, as a page's media form does ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)), a whole number from 1. Without it a PDF of one page is that page. `ocr` and `transcode` of a PDF of more than one page without it stop, naming the page count, since they would choose the picture. `probe` of a PDF of more than one page without it gives the file's page count and `null` for every fact of a page (its kind, size, alpha and colour space), since the file is no one medium and `probe` is how the page count is learned. A `--page` above the page count, and one on a file that is not a PDF, stop. `probe` of a page makes the checks its decoder makes of a page and refuses it the same way, before it starts, naming the file, the page and what the page lacks ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)), so a tool learns there whether a page is read. Of a page it reads it gives kind `page`, its size in its media pixels, which are points and may be fractions ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md#units)), the page count, alpha `true`, and the colour space of its untagged colour, its device colour that no default colour space (`DefaultRGB`, `DefaultGray`) redefines ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)): `srgb`, with the H.273 primaries 1 and transfer 13 and no matrix, no bits per channel, since a page has no pixels of its own, and `hdr` false, since a page is not HDR ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)). A page whose resources name a `DefaultRGB` reports `srgb` all the same, since the key takes it there too, and content drawn in a tagged colour space or under a default colour space is not described by it.
- `loudness` measures the loudness and peaks of one sound with the meter of [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#loudness-meter): integrated loudness, loudness range, the largest momentary and short-term loudness, true peak and sample peak. The sound is a mix, mixed with the mixer role as `render` mixes, without rendering pictures, before it is clipped and encoded ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#measuring-loudness)): for a document, which it resolves first, its mix, the outermost bus's output after the sequence's `audioFilter`, and for a medium the mix of the sequence that plays one clip of it from media time 0 at `volume` 1, `pan` 0 and `speed` 1 with no `audioFilter`, as long as the clip's sound. `--from` and `--to` are media time for a medium and sequence time for a document, and measure an interval of the whole sound from `--from`, 0 when it is left out, to `--to`, the end of the sound when it is left out: the medium's sound, or the mix plan's length ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#the-mix-plan)). `--from` lies in `[0, end)` and `--to` after `--from` and at most at the end, and any other time stops before anything is measured. A medium without sound (a still, an image sequence, a video without a sound track) stops, since it is asked for a sound it does not have. A document with no sound has a silent mix, and every measure of it is `null`. A `.json` file, a pack template or `-` is a document to it, and any other path a medium. `--args` with a medium stops, since a medium has no parameters to take them. It writes no file, and without `--json` it prints the measures for people.
- A command that reads a medium at media times takes both media that play in media time: `ocr`, `track`, `extract` and `sheet` take an image sequence wherever they take a video, and `probe` takes every medium ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md)). An image sequence is named by its directory, as its media form names it, and a directory is an image sequence to them. Its rate is not in its files, so `ocr`, `track`, `extract` and `sheet` take it as `--fps <rate>`, the `fps` its media form writes, a number above 0. An image sequence without `--fps` stops, naming `--fps`, and `--fps` with any other medium stops, since that medium's files give its rate. `probe` takes no `--fps`, since it reports what the files hold, and gives an image sequence's `duration` and `fps` as `null`. Read with `--fps r`, an image sequence is read as a video is: its media time is frame index / r, its duration its frame count / r, and the frame shown at a media time is the one [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#rules) picks, so `--at`, `--from`, `--to` and `--step` are media times on it as on a video, and the frames `track` prints are its frames, at their media times.
- `ocr` prints `size WxH` on the first line, then one line per recognised line, `[x, y, w, h]  text`, in media pixels, for a page in points as for an image in its pixels, a fraction written rounded as machine output writes sizes and positions ([Machine output](#machine-output)). Over a range it does this for each frame, under a line with its media time. Lines come top to bottom by row and left to right within a row, where lines whose vertical centres lie within half the shorter one's height of each other are one row. Without `--at` or a range a video or an image sequence is read at time 0. `--at` and `--from` lie in `[0, duration)`, `--to` at most at the duration, after `--from`, and `--step` is at least a microsecond. A still takes neither `--at` nor a range. A range is any of `--from`, `--to` and `--step`, and it needs `--step`, and `--at` with any of them stops. A frame's `time` is the media time asked, and the frame read is the one shown at it. `--languages` gives the recognition languages ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)).
- `track` follows a target on the frame at media time `--at`, backwards and forwards with the tracker provider, and prints the track data a clip's `track` takes ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#track-data)). The target is an area or a quadrilateral, exactly one of `--area` and `--quad`, and neither or both stop. `--area` is the JSON text of a rect area `[x, y, w, h]` of [FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md) in the frame at `--at`, and its messages name `--area`. `--quad` is the JSON text of four points `[[x, y], [x, y], [x, y], [x, y]]` in the frame at `--at`, the target's top left, top right, bottom right and bottom left corners, the order a `cornerPin` writes its points in ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)), so a corner pin written on that frame is given as it is written. A quadrilateral that is not four points of two numbers, or that is not strictly convex ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-homography)), stops, and its messages name `--quad`. An area is the quadrilateral of its four corners, and the target's quadrilateral is the one the frames printed carry, so a quadrilateral followed by a tracker that follows perspective, the `planar` tracker ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)), gives the track data of a plane in perspective, which drives a corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#a-tracked-corner-pin)). The default `point` tracker follows a quadrilateral as it follows an area, by similarity images of it, so the quads it prints keep the shape the quadrilateral has on the frame at `--at` and carry no perspective. It follows over the whole video or image sequence, or with `--to` over the frames shown between `--at` and `--to`, which may lie on either side. A tracker reads only the media's pixels, so it is given the target's quadrilateral when it lies inside the picture, and otherwise the rectangle that bounds the part of it inside, as its four corners, and each quad it reports for what it was given, r for g, is carried to the whole target p by the homography between them, H(g → r) applied to each corner of p ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-homography)). The frames printed are walked out from the frame at `--at` in each direction up to where the tracker reports the target lost, or up to the first frame whose quad, as carried to the whole target, is not strictly convex or runs the other way round than the target: a plane folded over itself, or seen from its back, is not a plane followed. A target with no part inside the picture, or one the tracker follows on no frame, stops.
- `ocr` and `track` read the media in the colour space `--color-space` names, over what the file says, as a media clip's `colorSpace` corrects it ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), so they see the picture the renderer draws for a clip that writes that `colorSpace`. The name is one whose Media column says yes, and any other name stops. Without it the file's tags apply.
- `extract` writes frames as PNG files. `sheet` lays frames out in one picture, each with its time label. Given a document they take composite frames at sequence times, as `still` draws them, and given a video or an image sequence the frames shown at media times. A frame with motion blur is the frame as the renderer draws it, the mean of its samples ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)), in a sheet's cell as in `still` and `extract`. `sheet` scales each frame so its longer side is at most 480 pixels, never enlarging it, and lays the frames out left to right in `--cols` columns, or one column per frame when there are fewer frames, then top to bottom. `extract` writes each frame at full size and unlabelled, named by its index in time order with at least four digits (`0000.png`, `0001.png`), into the directory, which it makes when it is missing. A `.json` file, a pack template or `-` is a document to them, a directory an image sequence, and any other path a video.
- `still`, `sheet` and `extract` write analysis pictures ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), SDR previews for looking at and the same normalised picture the providers read: PNGs in sRGB at 8 bits per channel, with HDR tone mapped and wide gamut clipped as it gives, whatever the sequence's or the video's colour space. They are not the master: the master of a `display-p3`, `rec2020` or HDR sequence is checked with `render --at`, which writes the frame in the sequence's colour space.
- `still`, `extract` and `sheet` stop on a time outside the video or sequence, on `--to` past its end, on `--from` not before `--to` and on a `--step` under a microsecond, before they write anything.
- `presets` lists presets by kind: a line with the kind, then a line for each of its presets, indented, with the name a document gives it and its description. With a name it prints that preset's JSON, its `params` included. `packs` lists the packs, the one that ships with frames and the installed ones, or what one pack holds, each tool with its description. Under a preset or a template that publishes parameters, `packs <pack>` prints one line for each, indented, in the order they are declared: its name, its type, its default as JSON on one line (`-` when it has none) and its description. A template's lines come from its `params` alone, checked as they are where it is used, so a template whose media is not there still lists them, and a broken declaration stops the listing, naming its place.
- The analysis commands (`probe`, `loudness`, `ocr`, `track`, `extract`, `sheet`, `timeline`, `evaluate`, `locate`) report facts to write a document with. `ocr` and `track` use the providers, and `transcode` writes with the encoder role. All of them read media through the decoder role.
- Paths are absolute. A document argument may also name a pack template, `<pack>/<name>`, or be `-`, read from standard input.
- Times are seconds written as decimals, several separated by commas, a point is `x,y`, `--cols` and `--max` are whole numbers, `--scale` is a number above 0, fractions allowed, and languages are BCP 47 tags separated by commas. A value that is not one, or is not finite, stops naming its option.
- A command word with a `/`, `frames <pack>/<name> ...`, runs that pack's tool with the global options before the name and the arguments after it, unchanged, with standard input and output passed through and `FRAMES_EXECUTABLE` set to the frames that runs it ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md#tools)). Any other word that is not a command of frames stops: `unknown command "rendr"`.
- `frames --version` prints the version ([FRAMES-18](../FRAMES-18/SPEC.md)). A pack names the versions it is written for with `engine` in its `pack.json`, and its tools are checked with it before they run.
- `frames --help` prints the usage, then each global option with what it takes and its default, to standard output and exits with 0. `frames <command> --help` prints that command's help to standard output and exits with 0, running nothing: its line of the usage, what it does, and each argument and option with what it takes and its default. An argument `--help` anywhere among a built-in command's arguments does this. A tool's arguments pass through unchanged, `--help` among them, so `frames <pack>/<name> --help` is the tool's own help.
- The global options before `--help` are checked as for any command, so `frames --renderer metal --help` stops. `--version` and `--help` stand alone after the global options, and an argument after either stops: `frames --version --help` and `frames --help render` stop, and a command's help is `frames <command> --help`.
- On success, stdout gets one line for each file written, with its path and size.
- On failure, stderr gets `frames: <reason>` and the exit code is 1. Without arguments, frames prints the usage to standard error and exits with 64, and so it does with global options and no command, since the command line is incomplete.
- The global options are checked before any command or tool runs, so an unknown role or provider name stops the same way for every command.
- Messages name the place, for example `format: unknown key "inset". Allowed: size, fps, background, conform, colorSpace, peak, compositing`. A place is a path of keys and indexes from the document, and the document itself is `top level`. A clip or op with an `id` is named with it after its index: `clips[1]#logo`.
- A message about a file other than the document (a theme, a preset, a template, a document named as media, an args file, a pack's `pack.json`) puts that file first, as it was referred to, then the place in that file: `looks/warm: defaults.transition (defaults): unknown transition preset "dissolve"`. A pack's `pack.json` is named by its path. What resolve finds inside a nested document or a template instance, once each is read and checked, is named by the place of the clip that holds it, continued as `timeline` continues it (`storyline[0].media.storyline[1]`).

### Time labels

`still` and `sheet` label each frame with its time in one fixed tool style. No theme changes it, so a check looks the same whatever the document's look, and it is laid out by frames itself, not by a document.

| Part | Value |
| --- | --- |
| text | the time in seconds, rounded to the microsecond in its shortest form, followed by `s`: `0s`, `2.5s`, `12.04s`. A video's frames are labelled with their media time, a document's with sequence time |
| size | S = max(14, round(L / 40)) pixels, where L is the longer side of the frame as it is drawn in the picture (48 for a 1920 by 1080 frame, 14 on a sheet frame 480 wide) |
| font | Helvetica Neue Medium (`HelveticaNeue-Medium` in `/System/Library/Fonts/HelveticaNeue.ttc`) at size S, shaped by the shaping provider ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) |
| colour | `#FFFFFFFF` text, no stroke |
| background | `#000000B3`, a rectangle around the text's line box (the font's ascent and descent at S), 0.25 S above and below it and 0.5 S left and right of it, with corners of radius 0.3 S |
| place | on the frame, the background's top left corner 0.5 S from the frame's top left corner, as an NLE burns a time into the picture |

- Frames lie S apart, with S around them, on an opaque `#202020FF` background, which their transparent parts show. A `still` of one time is that frame with its label and the same margin.

### transcode

`transcode` writes a delivery file from a still, an audio file or a video file: a format and a quality level for where the file goes. Whether it is smaller than its source depends on the source.

| Input (judged by contents) | Output extension | Written as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| still (an image or a page) | `.jpg`, `.heic`, `.png` | JPEG or HEIC at the quality level, HEIC at 10 bits per channel in an HDR colour space. PNG is lossless, and only `--max`, or `--scale` for a page, changes it, at the source's bit depth, and 16 bits per channel in an HDR colour space |
| audio | `.m4a` | AAC, up to 2 channels and 48 kHz |
| video | `.mp4`, `.mov`, `.m4v` | H.264 (the default in an SDR colour space) or HEVC (Main 10, the default in an HDR colour space), with AAC sound |
| video | `.m4a` | the sound only |

| Quality | Still (0 to 1) | Video | Audio (per channel) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `high` | 0.9 | high | 96 kbps |
| `medium` (default) | 0.75 | medium | 64 kbps |
| `low` | 0.55 | low | 32 kbps |

- `--max` caps the longer side of a source with pixels of its own, and such a source smaller than it is never enlarged. `--scale` is the pixels per point a page of a PDF is drawn at (below). A cap and a target are two things, so `--max` on a page and `--scale` on a source with pixels of its own stop, naming the other option. Video sizes round down to even numbers, so neither the source nor `--max` is exceeded. A video whose side would round below 2 pixels, from its source or under `--max`, stops before anything is written, naming the size it would give, and `--max` when it is what shrank it.
- The output is in the source's colour space when a sequence can be output in it ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces), the Sequence column), and in `srgb` otherwise, so a source tagged outside the table is written as `srgb`. `--color-space` names the output's colour space instead, a name a sequence takes, and any other name stops. The source is read as a sequence of the output's colour space reads a media clip's with `toneMap` true ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), so an HDR source is tone mapped to the output space's peak when its source peak is above it, 1000 cd/m² for `rec2100-pq`, the default `format.peak`, and written as that sequence's output is encoded and tagged ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)). A source the decoder stops on for its colour, an image whose ICC profile is LUT-based or a video whose code points no formula defines ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), is read by the platform's own colour management at relative intent instead: the one reading of frames that no fixed formula gives, made once, so the file it writes is read by the fixed formulas from then on.
- A page of a PDF ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)) is drawn whole at `--scale` pixels per point, and where it is not given at the scale a provider reads the page at, 4, or the largest at which the decoder holds the whole page where that is less ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)), since the file it writes is the master of a still from then on and a master never trades quality for size ([Master at full quality](../README.md#principles)). It is drawn as the decoder draws the whole page at that scale, each side rounded to the nearest whole number ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)). So only a `--scale` that is written can be too large: one whose picture the decoder cannot hold stops before anything is written, as the decoder refuses it, naming the size. A `.png` of a page is 16 bits per channel and keeps what the page paints nothing over transparent, and a `.jpg` of a page, a format without alpha, is the page over white, as a page read for its values is ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)). A page its decoder refuses for drawn text set in a font its file does not embed is drawn with the font the platform finds for it, by the platform's own colour management at relative intent, the one reading no fixed rule gives, made once, so the file it writes is read as pixels from then on. A PDF that opens only with a password is refused here as everywhere ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)).
- `--codec h264` with an HDR colour space stops, naming HEVC, since H.264 at 8 bits cannot carry HDR. A `.jpg` output in an HDR colour space stops, naming `.png` and `.heic`, since JPEG holds 8 bits.
- Metadata (EXIF, location) is not copied. Rotation is applied to the pixels. A PNG and a HEIC keep alpha. A JPEG gets transparent parts on white, and a video, written as H.264 or HEVC without alpha, gets them over black, as a delivery file does ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)).
- A video is written at its frame rate: frame k is the source frame shown at k / fps, for every k whose time is before the video's duration and frame 0 always, as a sequence's frames are, so a variable frame rate source keeps its length.
- The video levels are the encoder's: with `avfoundation` they are `AVVideoQualityKey` 0.7, 0.5 and 0.3, for H.264 and HEVC alike.
- Sound with more than 2 channels is downmixed to stereo by ITU-R BS.775, as the mixer downmixes it, and a rate above 48 kHz is converted to 48 kHz. A bit rate too high or low for the sample rate moves to the nearest one the AAC encoder takes.
- The output line gives size, format and the ratio to the source: `<path>  <size>  <format>  <percent> of <source size>`, the percent with one decimal.
- The same file as input and output stops, compared with symbolic links resolved and then as the file system names it, so a hard link stops too. An extension that does not fit the input stops, compared ignoring case, and so do `--codec` for an output that is not a video, `--color-space` for an output of sound only and an output that is a directory. An existing output file is removed only after every check passes.

What frames reports about a document or a medium is a contract, so programs outside frames can build on it. - The `kind` of a clip in `timeline`, `evaluate` and `locate` is the clip's kind: `video` (an image sequence included), `image`, `page`, `audio` (an audio clip, of a video's or a compound clip's sound included), `compound` (a sequence as media, a template instance included), `title`, `rect`, `ellipse`, `path`, `adjustment`, `solid` or `null`. It is the document's `kind` ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md)), with a `media` told apart by what its media is. - The display list and the mix plan are the roles' wire forms ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), not part of this output.

### Runs

**A run of `render` reports how it went**, since what each stage of the run cost, how each medium was read and whether a hardware encoder wrote the file are known to the engine alone ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md#layers)), and a program that finds a render slow would otherwise attach a profiler to the process and read frames's source.

A run is divided into stages, each the work named below, and every measure of a run is given by them: the report's `stages`, and the intervals Instruments shows.

| Stage | Work | Thread |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `resolve` | reading the document, the theme and the `--args` file, and resolving them ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), the providers resolve calls included | the render's |
| `check` | everything that refuses before the first frame: the pinned files' hashes, the decoder's check of every pinned medium, the mix plan made and the mixer's check of it, the renderer's check of every frame's display list and the encoder's check of every target ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)) | the render's |
| `evaluate` | evaluation of every frame's display list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)), for the checks and for drawing | the render's |
| `mix` | the mixer mixing the mix plan ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) | the render's |
| `decode` | the decoder reading pictures and sound out of their files, as the files store them | the render's, and the decoder's own for a picture it reads ahead |
| `workingConversion` | the decoder taking each picture's stored values to working values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), tone mapping included | the render's, and the decoder's own for a picture it reads ahead |
| `draw` | the renderer drawing display lists into frames of working values ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)), what it reads through the decoder left out | the render's |
| `outputConversion` | the encoder taking frames of working values to the output's encoded values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)) | each file's queue, and the render's for a PNG |
| `encode` | the encoder compressing the encoded frames and writing them with the sound into files | each file's queue, and the render's for a PNG and for finishing each file |

- A stage's time is the wall time its work runs, by a monotonic clock, summed over the threads that run it: the render's, the queue of each file and the threads the decoder reads ahead on. Work that one call spreads over several cores counts once, as the wall time of that call. Where the decoder reads inside another stage (the providers in `resolve`, the mixer in `mix`, the renderer in `draw`), that reading is `decode` and `workingConversion` and not the other stage, so no time is counted twice.
- A decoder may read a video's next picture ahead on a thread of its own while the render's thread works on the one it gave ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)). That reading is `decode` and `workingConversion`, and `readAhead` gives its part of each. It runs inside no stage of the render's thread, so it is taken from none. When the render's thread asks for a picture still being read ahead, or for another of the same file while one is, it waits for that reading, and the wait is `waiting.decoder`, not `decode`.
- The render's own thread runs the first seven stages one at a time, and between them waits on the encoder and on the decoder's reading ahead. So `seconds`, the wall time of the run, is the sum of those seven less the two of `readAhead`, with `waiting.encoder`, `waiting.decoder` and `other`: `waiting.encoder` is the time the render's thread spends in the encoder, making the writers, handing it a frame while two are still being written, finishing the files and writing a PNG whole, `waiting.decoder` is its time waiting on the decoder's reading ahead, and `other` is the time in none of them, which is what `render` does between the stages.
- A video's `outputConversion` and `encode` run on a serial queue for each file, alongside the render's thread, but for finishing each file, which is `encode` on the render's thread inside `waiting.encoder`. So they are not part of that sum either, and a run whose `waiting.encoder` is large is held up by its encoder. A PNG's run on the render's thread, inside `waiting.encoder`.
- A run begins when `render` starts reading its document, once its command line is checked, and ends when its last file is closed. A stage's time holds the work that ended within the run, and a tally the pictures whose reading did, so the part of a reading ahead that runs past the end of the run is in neither. A run that stops writes no report.

The report is one JSON object ([Machine output](#machine-output)). Every field is always there. A field is `null` only where it says so.

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `frames` | the frames written: the sequence's frame count for a video, and 1 for a PNG |
| `seconds` | the run's wall time |
| `cpuSeconds` | the processor time the process spent over the run, user and system, on every thread |
| `fps` | `frames` divided by `seconds` |
| `cores` | the average number of cores busy: `cpuSeconds` divided by `seconds` |
| `peakMemory` | the largest resident memory of the process over its life, in bytes |
| `machine` | `{chip, cores, os}`: the processor's name as the system gives it (`Apple M2 Pro`), the number of logical cores, and the operating system's name and version (`macOS 26.2.0`) |
| `roles` | `{decoder, renderer, mixer, encoder}`: the name of the implementation that filled each role ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) |
| `stages` | each stage's time, one key for each stage of the table above, in its order |
| `readAhead` | `{decode, workingConversion}`: the part of each of those stages the decoder spent reading ahead on threads of its own, above |
| `waiting` | `{encoder, decoder}`: the render's thread's time in the encoder and waiting on the decoder's reading ahead, above |
| `other` | the render's thread's time between stages, above |
| `media` | each medium whose pictures the decoder read, in the order it first read each: `{path, page, colorSpace, conversion, pixelFormat, framesRead, framesDecoded, reopened}` |
| `files` | each file written, the output first and then the delivery: `{path, codec, hardware}` |

- In `media`, `path` is the pinned absolute path of the medium, for an image sequence its directory, as `timeline` gives it, and `page` the number of a PDF's page, `null` for any other medium. `colorSpace` is the name of the space of the table its pictures are read in ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces)), `null` outside the table. `conversion` is how they are taken to working values, below. `pixelFormat` is how the decoder is given a picture's stored values by what it reads the file with, `<bits>-bit <integer | float> <channels>`, the channels `gray`, `gray alpha`, `RGB` or `RGBA` (`16-bit float RGBA`). `framesRead` is the pictures the decoder gave, `framesDecoded` the pictures it decoded, to give them and reading ahead, the frames it decoded past on its way to a later one included, so a picture read ahead and never asked for is decoded and not read, and `reopened` the times it opened a reader on the medium again after its first, reading ahead included: for a video a reader started again at a frame earlier than the last it read or too far after it to decode up to, and for a still or an image sequence's frame each read of a file it read before. A medium read by two conversions, a displacement map's file that a clip also shows in a `colorSpace` it writes ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), has an entry for each. Sound is read in `decode` and has no entry.
- In `files`, `path` is the file's absolute path, `codec` what its pictures are written as (`ProRes 422 HQ`, `ProRes 4444`, `H.264`, `HEVC Main 10`, `PNG`), and `hardware` whether a hardware encoder wrote them.
- Times are seconds, written as machine output writes times. `fps` and `cores` are written rounded to the nearest millionth, and `peakMemory` and the counts are whole numbers.

The conversion to working values is the form of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media) that converts a picture, by the colour space a place writes for it or by its tags:

| Conversion | Pictures |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | in `srgb`, whose stored values are working values as they are |
| `space` | in a space of the table other than `srgb`, by that space's formulas ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces)) |
| `transfer` | a video tagged outside the table, by the formulas of H.273: its transfer decoded, then the matrix of its primaries to linear sRGB |
| `pq` | a video tagged PQ on primaries other than BT.2020: its display light by the PQ EOTF on its own primaries, taken to BT.2020 primaries |
| `hlg` | a video tagged HLG on primaries other than BT.2020: its display light by the HLG EOTF and OOTF on its own primaries, taken to BT.2020 primaries |
| `profile` | an image whose matrix and tone-curve ICC profile describes no space of the table, by its tone curves and colorants |

- A page of a PDF is drawn in `srgb` and reads as `none`, or as `space` in a `colorSpace` written for it.

**Progress.** `render` writes on standard error how far it has drawn, in the form `--progress` names: `text`, the default, a line `frame <k>/<n>`, `json` the object `{"progress": "0.1.0", frame, frames, seconds}` on one line, and `none` nothing. k is the frames drawn and handed to the encoder, n the run's `frames` and `seconds` the wall time since the run began. A line is written for the first frame drawn a second or more after the last line, or after the run began, and for the last frame always, so a run writes at most one line a second and its last line says it drew every frame. Standard output is as for every command, a line for each file written, the report among them.

**Signposts.** On macOS every stage's work is an `os_signpost` interval named by the stage, in the subsystem `frames` and the category `stages`, so Instruments shows a run's stages by name on the thread that runs them.

### Item transforms

**Every item of `evaluate` reports `transform`, the map from its own coordinates to output pixels, and `rect`, where it lies in them, and under a `cornerPin` the corner pins that finish that map**, since where an item lies is a fact of the item, and `bounds`, the axis-aligned box of what shows of it, cannot say where a rotated, skewed or nested one lies, nor where one cut by a mask or a reveal lies whole.

- It is `[a, b, c, d, tx, ty]` in the matrix form of the display list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#fields-every-item-has)): a point (x, y) of the item's own coordinates is at (a x + c y + tx, b x + d y + ty) in the output.
- It is P_n ∘ R_n ∘ … ∘ P_1 ∘ R_1 ∘ T, applied from the right: T is the item's `transform`, and the pictures that hold the list the item is in are numbered from 1, the innermost, to n, the outermost, each with R_k the map from its list's pixels into the picture, (x, y) to (dx + (x - rx) × dw / rw, dy + (y - ry) × dh / rh) with `[rx, ry, rw, rh]` its `rect`, the list's whole frame where it is `null`, and `[dx, dy, dw, dh]` its `dest`, and P_k its `transform`. An item of the outermost list has n = 0. A picture whose source is a nested list is no item of `evaluate`: its list's background and items are, each carried by the maps of the pictures that hold it. The picture that draws a title by unit as a group maps its list onto itself with the identity, so a unit's `transform` is its item's, the title's with the unit's motion.
- An item's own coordinates are those `bounds` is the box of, carried by `transform`, then by its `cornerPins` under a corner pin, and cut by its reveal, its masks and the pictures that hold it. For a `picture` they are the coordinates its `dest` lies in. For a `path` they are its geometry's, which its stroke and arrowheads are drawn on. For a `glyphs` item they are its title's text box at t, of its `size` with its origin at the top left, where its glyphs, its `unitBox`, its characters' rects and its title's lines lie ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and not the title's box, which the background's padding grows from it: for a title whose text box is 200 by 56 and whose background `padding` is `[8, 12]`, the item's `size` is `[200, 56]`, its `rect` `[0, 0, 200, 56]` where no glyph it draws reaches outside the text box, and its background's `rect` `[-12, -8, 224, 72]`, and a padding that takes keyframes moves no character's rect. For an `adjust` item and the `background` they are the pixels of the list the item is in, whose whole frame it covers, and T is left out: an adjustment's own transform carries only its `reveal` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and the background has none.
- `rect` is `[x, y, w, h]`, the item's extent in its own coordinates as the display list gives the item, before its reveal, its masks and the pictures that hold it cut it: a `picture`'s `dest`, the box of a `path`'s geometry, the bounding box of its outline and not of its control points ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#shapes)), without its stroke, a `glyphs` item's `extent`, the drawn extent of its unit or its title ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units), [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and the whole frame of its list for an `adjust` item and the `background`. So a program finds where a rotated picture lies, whole, as `rect` carried by `transform`.
- **Under a `cornerPin`, an item's `transform` and `rect` give where it lies on its plane, the corner pins left out**, since a homography ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-homography)) has no form `[a, b, c, d, tx, ty]`: `transform` is P_n ∘ R_n ∘ … ∘ P_1 ∘ R_1 ∘ T as above, the map of each picture with a `cornerPin` taken as R_k, from its list's pixels to its `dest`. Such an item also reports `cornerPins`, the corner pins of the pictures that hold it, the innermost first and the outermost last, each `{dest, cornerPin}`: its picture's `dest`, as its four corners, and its picture's `cornerPin`, each a quadrilateral of four points `[x, y]` in the order of a corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-key)), carried by the picture's `transform` and the maps of the pictures that hold it, their corner pins left out, into the coordinates `transform` gives. A point of the item's own coordinates lies in the output where `transform` takes it and H(`dest` → `cornerPin`) of each entry, in order, then takes it, so a program finishes the map. An item no corner pin carries reports `cornerPins` as `null`, and its `transform` is the map to output pixels. So the item a clip with a corner pin draws in its group lies by `transform` in the clip's box B as on its plane ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-key)), carried by the group's `transform`, and its one entry is B and the corner pin Q(t), carried by the same `transform`.
- Items inside a track matte's source are not items of `evaluate`, since a clip used as a matte draws no item of its own, and a transition matte is a picture's `matte`, not an item, so neither reports a `transform` or text.

### The frame a picture draws

**A `picture` item of a media file names the frame it draws, in `evaluate` and `locate` alike**, since which file, frame and page a clip shows at t is a fact evaluation computes through the clip's time map, its `in`, `speed`, `loop` and `timeRemap` and a transition's handles, and a program that reads that frame (with `ocr`, `probe` or `extract`) would otherwise compute it again.

- `media` is the pinned absolute path of the file the frame is read from ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), an image sequence's frame file for one, since a resolved document keeps no written spelling.
- `mediaTime` is the frame's pinned media time, the timestamp of the frame [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#rules) picks at the media time the clip's time map gives at t ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md)), and 0 for an image and a page, which have one picture.
- `page` is the number of the page of a PDF the picture shows, and `null` for any other medium.
- A picture of a media file is a picture of a clip's media or of a transition's media cover ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)). A picture whose source is a nested display list is no item, so a compound clip's frames are named by the pictures inside it, and a transition matte is a picture's `matte`, not an item, so it names none. Every other item has none of the three keys.

### A clip's times and spaces

**`timeline` gives where and in what pixels every value a clip writes takes effect**, since the sequence time of a written time, the instant a value without keyframes takes effect at and the pixels a coordinate is written in are facts resolve computes from the clocks of [FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md) and the spaces of [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md), and a program that places a value on the frame it lies on (with `timeline --at` and `ocr`) would otherwise compute them again.

- `instant` is the clip's instant in sequence time, where a value it writes without keyframes takes effect ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md#a-clips-instant)): a storyline clip's `in`, and a connected or sequence clip's start, its storyline clip's `in` or 0 where it writes no `at`. A resolved document's timeline gives it as `null`, since resolve has found every value that takes effect at an instant and the resolved document keeps no instant.
- `clock` is `{clip, origin, speed}`, the map of the times the clip writes, its `at` and its keyframes' `at`, to sequence time: a time c written on the clip lies at sequence time `origin + c / speed`. `clip` is the place of the clip whose clip time they are on ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md)): the clip itself for a storyline clip, its storyline clip for a connected clip, and `null` where they are on sequence time, for a sequence clip and for every clip of a resolved document, which writes every instant in sequence time ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)). `origin` is the sequence time at which that clock reads 0, and `speed` is the storyline clip's `speed`, 1 on sequence time and on a time-remapped clip. A clock is that one line outside its clip as within it ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md#storyline-clips-on-the-sequence-clock)), so a keyframe before `in` or past `out` is carried by it too, and the frame a written time shows is what `timeline --at` gives at its sequence time. A connected or sequence media clip's `in` and `out` are media time and are not on its clock.
- `space` is the space the clip's `position`, `area`, `points` and `cornerPin` are written in, the space its parent gives ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md#the-space-a-parent-gives)), and `ownSpace` the space its `crop` and `mask` are written in, both `null` on an audio clip, which has no picture and writes no coordinates. Each is `{kind, clip}`. Kind `output` is output pixels of the frame, a nested sequence's inside a nested document, and its `clip` is `null`, since the frame is no clip's. Kind `media` is the media pixels of the clip at the place `clip`, which for a compound clip or a template instance are its nested sequence's output pixels. Kind `box` is the box of the clip at the place `clip`: a title's text box with its background at the instant evaluated ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), or the box of a rect or ellipse placed by `width` and `height`.
- `space` follows the parent chain as [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md#the-space-a-parent-gives) does. A storyline clip's and an unparented clip's are `output`. A parent that is a shape or a null placed by its geometry, an adjustment clip or a clip with a corner pin gives the space it is written in itself, a solid and a storyline clip with a corner pin give `output`, any other storyline clip and a media clip give its `media`, and a title and a rect, an ellipse or a null placed by `width` and `height` give its `box`.
- `ownSpace` is a storyline clip's and a media clip's own `media`, a solid's `output`, a title's and a rect's, an ellipse's or a null's placed by `width` and `height` own `box`, and for a shape or a null placed by its geometry and an adjustment clip its `space`, the space its geometry and its masks are written in.
- So a clip has its keys in two spaces at most, and has two exactly where its own content is placed in its parent: a media clip's `crop` and `mask` lie in its own media pixels and its `position` in its parent's space ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md#how-a-child-follows)), and a title's `mask` lies in its text box. A program reads the pixels a value lies in from the entry that writes it and follows no parent chain.
- `tracked` is `{clip, first, last}`, the media a `track` on the clip follows and the frames it is followed over ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#what-is-tracked)), given whether the clip writes `track` or not, since a program that follows a target for the clip reads them before it writes the track data. `clip` is the place of the clip whose video or image sequence the track follows, the clip itself or its parent, and the quads of the track data lie in that clip's media pixels. `first` and `last` are the pinned media times of the first and the last frame of that media its clip's time map shows while the clip is drawn, `speed`, `timeRemap`, `loop` and handles applied: a storyline clip over its `drawn`, a clip that follows a storyline clip's media over that storyline clip's `drawn`, and any other clip over its `visible`. So track data that names every frame from `first` to `last` keeps the clip drawn over its whole window, and a window narrowed by track data gives the frames of the narrowed window ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#where-the-target-is-lost)). `tracked` is `null` where there is no video or image sequence for the track to follow, where `track` stops ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#what-is-tracked)), on an audio clip, and in a resolved document, which holds every track as resolve fitted it and no track data to follow.

### Text placement

**`evaluate` reports how a title's text is split apart from where it lies**: `texts` gives each title's lines and words once, whole, whatever animates it, with the text box they lie in, and each character is reported on the item that draws it, so a program measures and fits text on the lines frames draws and lays out none again ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)). A line has one place in the text box and a word lies where its characters lie, so nothing depends on how a title's units fall and no entry is ever missing.

- `texts` holds one entry for each title drawn at t, in the order of its first item, each `{place, id, text, writingMode, direction, size, box, transform, cornerPins, lines, words}`: the title's place and `id` as its items name them, its `text`, its `writingMode`, `horizontal-tb`, `vertical-rl` or `vertical-lr`, the writing mode it was laid out in, and its `direction`, `ltr` or `rtl`, the paragraph direction it was laid out in ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), its text box's `size` at t as `[w, h]`, `box`, its title's box at t, the text box grown by its background's `padding` at t ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#titles)), as `[x, y, w, h]` in the text box, `[0, 0, w, h]` for a title without a background, `transform`, the map from its text box to output pixels without any unit's motion, and `cornerPins`, the corner pins that carry its text box, in the forms of an item's ([Item transforms](#item-transforms)), and its lines and its words in reading order ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)), each line `{text, range, baseline, ascent, descent}` and each word `{text, range}`, `range` its `[start, end)` in Unicode scalars over the title's text and `text` the title's text over it. A word a line breaks inside is one word, and a line with no character has an empty range. A line's `baseline` is the y of its baseline in the text box at t, its x in a vertical writing mode ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#writing-mode)), the line height at t applied and no `baselineShift`, which belongs to characters, and `ascent` and `descent` are its line's ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#lines)), so a line with no character has a place and a line whose characters are all shifted keeps the baseline they are shifted from. A word has no place of its own. Lines lie in the text box, which `transform` carries to the output, followed by `cornerPins` under a corner pin. The text box is a coordinate system of the title, not of an item, since every item of the title has it as its own coordinates, so its `size`, `box`, `transform` and `cornerPins` are the title's entry's: for a title not animated by unit `transform` is its one item's, and for a title animated by unit it is the title's, which each unit's item composes with the unit's motion, so a program places a title's lines without knowing which of its items is which. A title is drawn at t where it draws an item at t: outside its window it draws none, and a title used as a track matte draws none of its own, so neither has an entry. A time label is drawn by `still` and `sheet` only ([Time labels](#time-labels)), so `evaluate` reports none.
- An item's `characters` is the characters it reports, in reading order, each `{text, range, level, rect, baseline}`: `text` and `range` as above, `level` the embedding level it lies at ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#direction)), odd where it reads right to left, `rect` its glyph box at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) as `[x, y, w, h]` and `baseline` the y of the baseline it sits on, its x in a vertical writing mode, its line's baseline at t less its run's `baselineShift` at t and never a glyph's y, both in the item's own coordinates, the text box at t, which its `transform` carries to the output, followed by its `cornerPins` under a corner pin. It is a list on a `glyphs` item of `layer` `null` that holds runs and on a unit's item of `layer` `fills` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and `null` on every other item, a title's background drawn alone and the unit items of `highlights`, `underlines` and `strokes` included, so each character is reported once, with the item that draws its fill.
- A title not animated by unit reports every character on its one item. In a title animated by unit a unit's `fills` item reports the characters of its unit, those the unit holds and the spaces that go with it, as the resolved unit names them ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units), [FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), so every character of the title is reported once and `evaluate` assigns none. A space is drawn and reported with the unit it goes with, and a space that hangs past a line's end, which is no character, is drawn by a unit ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) and reported by none. `evaluate` tells which unit an item is by its `origin`, which names the title, and its `unit`, which names the unit ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)).
- `evaluate` places each character from the resolved title ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) and splits no text itself: its `rect` is its box at the static values, from its first glyph's pen x, its pen y in a vertical writing mode, and its cluster's `advance` and its line's `baseline`, `ascent` and `descent`, moved as its glyphs move at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) and taken into the text box at t as they are. A character with no glyph of its own has the `rect` and `baseline` of its group's first character ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)). So a ligature's characters share its box, a character of several scalars or glyphs is one box, a placement offset moves no box, the tracking beside a character lies between boxes and in none, and the same glyphs drawn at a static tracking or at a keyframed one of the same value give the same boxes.
- A word lies where its characters lie: the characters whose ranges are inside its range, on the items that report them, so a word a line breaks inside lies on both lines, and a word over several units lies on their items.
- Where text lies does not depend on what of it shows: `reveal`, masks, a track matte and `opacity` cut what is seen of an item and leave its `rect` and its `characters` as they are. `bounds` and `opacity` report what shows.

## Interactions

Machine output follows [FRAMES-18](../FRAMES-18/SPEC.md). Tools are [FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md). The display list `evaluate` and `locate` report is [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md), the providers `ocr` and `track` call and the master `render` writes are [FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md), and the checks made with `still` and `sheet` are [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md). The meter `loudness` measures with is [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md). The colour spaces `probe` reports and `transcode` writes, tone mapping and analysis pictures are [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md). The stages of a run are the work of the roles of [FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md), which report their facts of it, and its conversions to working values the forms of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media).

## Alternatives considered

A separate command or document kind for stills: a still is a frame of a sequence, so `render` writes it with `--at` and there is one way to draw a picture.

Time labels in the theme's text styles: a check would look different under every look, and a theme without such a style could not be checked at all. A fixed tool style keeps the check apart from the result.

Time labels under each frame on a band of their own: a label burnt into the corner, as NLEs overlay timecode, keeps a sheet as tight as its frames and reads the same at any size.

An API or a server in place of commands: commands with JSON output are what any agent, shell and language already drive, and frames keeps no state between them for a session to hold.

`transcode` defined as making a file smaller: a delivery file is written for where it goes at a quality level, and whether it is smaller depends on how its source was written.

`transcode` of a page of a PDF writing a PDF with its fonts embedded: it keeps the vectors, but only for a page refused for its fonts, and it is a decoder that writes PDF, a change of its own.

`transcode` refusing a source no fixed formula reads, as the decoder does: such a file could not enter an edit at all without another tool. Read once by the platform's colour management, it becomes a file of the table that every later reading converts by formula, and the resolved document pins that file.

Ink bounds of text, the box of its glyphs' outlines: evaluation would have to open font files, which it does not ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), and two shapers' outlines differ. A glyph box is made of advances, ascents and descents, which the resolved document already fixes, and a program that needs the drawn outline measures a frame from `extract` (README).

A separate command for text layout: it would be a second report of the same instant, where `evaluate` already names the clip and the time.

The media a clip shows at an instant as a command of its own, or on `evaluate`'s items alone: a clip outside its window, covered, masked away or at opacity 0 draws no item, while its time map is defined there, and a program placing a value on a frame needs that frame wherever the value takes effect. A second command would report a document's time beside `timeline`, which already holds every clip's ranges and speed, so `timeline` takes the instant.

The media a picture draws given by `locate` alone: a program that wants the frame a clip draws would have to find a point that lies on its picture, which a mask or a clip above may leave nowhere it can tell, and would test points until one names it. The frame is a fact of the item, so `evaluate` gives it on the item, by the same rule `locate` gives it.

`timeline --at`'s `mediaTime` as the media time the time map gives, before a frame is picked, beside `evaluate`'s frame time under the same name: one name would carry two values for one clip at one instant, which differ between two frames, and a program reading the frame with `ocr --at` or matching an item to its entry needs the frame's time. The time map's value between frames picks no other frame, so the frame's time is the fact both reports give.

`timeline`'s `media` as the document writes it (`broll/surf.mov`), the same on every machine: a program cannot open a pack name or probe it without looking it up as frames does, a resolved document keeps no written spelling, so a document and its resolved document would report two things for one clip, and the written spelling is in the document the program holds. The pinned path is what `locate` and `evaluate` give, so one clip's media is named one way in every report.

An image sequence's rate taken from the document's `fps` or a default, in `ocr`, `track`, `extract` and `sheet`: a command given a directory reads no document, and its media form requires `fps` because nothing in its files gives one, so `--fps` is the key of that form.

A written time carried to sequence time by `timeline --at`, given a clip and a time on its clock in place of a sequence time: every clip's map from its written times to sequence time is one line, so two numbers give the sequence time of every keyframe a clip writes from one report, while a second form of `--at` would run resolve once for each written time and give one option two meanings.

The space of each key of a clip reported apart, or a clip's space as the chain of its parents for a program to walk: a clip's keys lie in two spaces at most, where it is placed and where its own content is, and the walk is the rule of [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md), which a program would carry again with every row of its table.

Text placement under `locate`: a point names what lies under it, and cannot list a caption's words. A program hit tests a point against the characters' rects and their items' `transform` and `cornerPins` itself.

Entries for glyphs: a glyph id means nothing without its font, and a character's rect already places it.

Ranges counted in UTF-16 code units, as Core Text and JavaScript count them: the contract would follow one platform's string type. Unicode scalars are how Python indexes a string and how Swift's `unicodeScalars` and Rust's `chars` count it, and each entry's `text` spares a program counting at all.

A title by unit reporting its characters on its background item: the rects would lie where the text rests, not where its units are drawn. Each unit's item reports the characters that move with it.

A rect for each word, reported with the item that draws it: a word spread over several units has no one item to report it, so whether a word is reported would hang on how its title is animated, and a program would split the text again where it is not. The split is reported once for each title and holds under any animation. A line is reported with its place because every item of a title shares its text box, so a line has one baseline, ascent and descent there whatever animates its characters, and an empty line or the baseline under a shifted run is known only so.

A line that lies only where its characters lie: a line with no character would lie nowhere, though it takes its height in the text box, and a line whose characters are all shifted would lose the baseline they are shifted from.

A title's lines placed through one of its items, by a `layer` reported on every item or by a rule that a title's first item draws its background: a program would have to know an order or a field of the display list to find the coordinates its lines lie in, and the text box, which every item of the title shares, belongs to none of them. The title's entry carries the text box's `size` and `transform`, and the title's `box` in it, as an item carries its own.

A character's box from where its glyphs are drawn: a placement offset, which sets a combining mark over its base and with which some fonts kern, would move or narrow a box where the pen did not move, a mark drawn back over its base would cut its character's box to part of the base's width, and two shapers that split a position between advance and offset differently would give two boxes. The pen is where the line is laid out, so the resolved glyph writes its pen position apart from its placement offset ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)).

A `glyphs` item in the title's box, the text box grown by the background's padding: a padding that takes keyframes would move every character's rect while nothing moves on screen, and a title's mask, which lies in its text box ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)), would lie in other coordinates than its glyphs.

A unit read from the order of its title's `fills` items: which characters an item draws would hang on the items drawn around it, so a program that reads one item, or a list that items were cut from, could not tell its unit. Which characters an item draws is a fact of what it holds, so a unit's item names its unit as `unit` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and the order of a title's `fills` items follows from it.

Text placement carried on the display list's `glyphs` items: a renderer would carry a field it draws nothing from, and the display list, a renderer's contract, would hold the rules of a report. `evaluate` computes it from the resolved title with the moves that place the glyphs, which keeps it on the drawn glyphs as well.

`transform` only on items that draw text: the map from an item's coordinates to the output is a fact of every item, and a program that covers a region of a rotated picture needs it, with the picture's `rect`, as a caption fitter needs a title's lines with its `transform`.

`transform` without `rect`: a map with no domain, since `bounds` is cut by masks and reveal and cannot give back where a picture's `dest`, a path's geometry or a title's glyphs lie whole.

`transform` as a 3 × 3 matrix, the whole map under a corner pin: it changes the form of every item's `transform`, a major change ([FRAMES-18](../FRAMES-18/SPEC.md)), for the few items a corner pin carries, and it composes a picture's corner pin into one matrix with the affine maps around it, so a program could not tell where an item lies on its plane. The plane's map with the corner pins as `{dest, cornerPin}` gives both, each corner pin in the terms the display list writes it.

A title's box left out of `texts`: a boxed title covers its background's padding as well as its text box, and a program that keeps captions clear of other text would know only half of what it covers.

A character's box on its line's baseline, without `baselineShift`: a raised or bouncing character would be reported where it is not drawn, and the `baseline` it reports would not be the one it sits on.

A tracked frame's quad carried to the whole target by the similarity fitted to what the tracker reported: a perspective quad would lose its perspective wherever the target reaches past the picture. The homography of what was given to what was reported carries the whole plane, and for a tracker that reports similarity images it is that similarity.

The media a clip's track follows left to a program, from its `kind`, its corner pin and its parent: the rule is resolve's ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#what-is-tracked)), and a program that keeps it again follows a target in media resolve then refuses. The frames a track is followed over left out, so a program follows the target over the whole video: a long shot would be walked far past the frames the clip shows, and the frames its time map shows under a `timeRemap` or a `loop` are no range of the clip's `in` and `out` that a program could read without the time map. `timeline` gives both, since resolve computes them from the time map.

A `track` whose target is only a rectangle, with a corner pin followed by giving the tracker the rectangle that bounds it: the tracker would follow the pixels of a rectangle that is not the plane, a plane seen at an angle is no rectangle in the picture, and the quads printed would be the rectangle's, from which no homography gives back the corner pin's own corners on each frame. A quadrilateral in the corner pin's order is the target as the corner pin writes it.

A `track` that also prints the target and each frame's fitted motion: the target is the quad of the frame `time` names, and the motion is resolve's fit, which the resolved document writes ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), so what `track` prints would not be the track data a document takes.

Tracking inside a character's box, at the end of its advance: one shaper adds it to advances and another does not, and the same glyphs drawn at a static tracking and at a keyframed one of the same value would give different boxes.

A stage's time as processor time: a stage that waits on a GPU or on a hardware encoder would show none of the time it holds the run, and parallel work would show more time than the run took, so no sum would give `seconds`. Wall time on the thread that runs each stage adds up on the render's thread to the run, and `cpuSeconds` with `cores` gives the processor time beside it.

The decoder's reading ahead counted only as the time the render's thread waits for it, or that wait counted as `decode`: a run that reads ahead would show less `decode` and `workingConversion` than the work its media took, and a render held up by the decoder's thread would read as one decoding slowly on its own. `readAhead` gives the work done beside the render, and `waiting.decoder` the part of it the render's thread did not hide.
