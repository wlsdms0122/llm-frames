# FRAMES-23: Command line and machine output

Status: accepted

## Summary

The commands, their arguments and output, `loudness` among them, the fixed style of the time labels `still` and `sheet` draw, `transcode` for delivery files, and the machine output that programs outside frames build on.

## Motivation

An agent and the programs it runs drive frames only through commands. Editing is a loop: change the document, look at it with `still`, `sheet` and `timeline`, change it again, render, and use what was rendered as media in the next edit. So the commands that check must be cheap and must say exactly what they saw, and what each command prints and how it fails is the interface. Two implementations that differ in a flag, a label or a field would break every program written against one of them.

## Rules

```
frames [--theme <pack/name | /abs/theme.json>] [--decoder <name>] [--renderer <name>] [--mixer <name>] [--encoder <name>]
       [--shaper <name>] [--tracker <name>] [--ocr <name>] [--ocr-languages <tag>[,<tag>...]] <command> ...

render    <document.json> -o <out.mov | out.png> [--at <t>] [--deliver <out.mp4>] [--args <args.json>]
still     <document.json> --at <t>[,<t>...] -o <out.png> [--args <args.json>]
resolve   <document.json> -o <resolved.json> [--args <args.json>]
timeline  <document.json> [--args <args.json>] [--json]
evaluate  <document.json> --at <t> [--args <args.json>]
locate    <document.json> --at <t> --point <x,y> [--args <args.json>]
probe     <media> [--json]
loudness  <media | document.json> [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--args <args.json>] [--json]
ocr       <image | video> [--at <t> | --from <t> --to <t> --step <s>] [--languages <tag>[,<tag>...]] [--color-space <name>] [--json]
track     <video> --at <t> --area <area> [--to <t>] [--color-space <name>]
extract   <video | document.json> <outdir> [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--step 1]
sheet     <video | document.json> -o <out.png> [--from <t>] [--to <t>] [--step 1] [--cols 6]
transcode <in> -o <out> [--quality high|medium|low] [--max <px>] [--codec h264|hevc] [--color-space <name>]
presets   [<kind>] [<name>]
packs     [<pack>]
theme
```

- Every command that takes a document (`render`, `still`, `timeline`, `evaluate`, `locate`, `loudness`, `extract`, `sheet`) also takes a resolved document, which it uses as it is. `render` takes a document, a template with `--args`, or a resolved document. A resolved document with `--theme` or `--args` stops, since both are already written in. A template renders with its defaults when every parameter has one. A template with `--theme` stops, since only its arguments change it, and `--args` with a document that is not a template stops, since it has no parameters to take them.
- `render` writes a sequence to a `.mov`, or one frame of it to a `.png`: the frame at sequence time `--at`, 0 when it is left out, at the frame's size and without a time label, in the sequence's colour space at 16 bits per channel ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). `--at` lies in `[0, duration)`. `--at` with a `.mov` stops, since a master holds every frame, and so does `--deliver` with a `.png`, which has no frames to deliver. `--deliver` names an `.mp4`. Any other extension stops. `resolve` writes to a `.json` file, compared ignoring case, and any other extension stops before the document is read.
- `still` draws sequence frames at the given sequence times without encoding a video. It is the cheap check while a document is being edited. Several times go side by side, left to right in the order given, each with its time label ([Time labels](#time-labels)).
- `timeline` resolves without rendering and prints, for every storyline clip, its edit range, drawn range, clip range (`[in, out)` on its clip time), its speed or that it is time-remapped, its incoming transition with its length, and its sound range, the range its sound plays over with its `split` applied, or that it plays no sound ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), and for every other clip its visible range, kind, lane, animation, place (`storyline[1].connected[0]`) and parent, and for a visual media clip its sound range as well. Everything resolve stops on while it plans (an unknown preset, a clip that is never visible, a parent chain with a cycle, a bad argument) stops here too. It reads media for its facts, but not a LUT, which resolve reads only when it pins the file and which has no say in time. It writes no file.
- `evaluate` prints the display list at sequence time t as machine output, item by item, with the clip or op that drew each, and the text of every title drawn at t. `locate` maps a point of the output at t back to the document ([Machine output](#machine-output)). Both stop on `--at` outside `[0, duration)`, as `still` does.
- `probe` prints the kind, size, duration, frame rate, alpha, colour space, and audio channels and sample rate. The kind is `image`, `video`, `audio` or `imageSequence`, which a directory probes as, and the frame rate is the nominal one. A fact the medium does not have is `null`: the size of sound, and the duration and frame rate of an image and of an image sequence, whose rate is the document's `fps`. `alpha` says whether the picture carries an alpha channel. The colour space is the one the decoder takes the picture in ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces)): the name of the table row its tags match, `srgb` when it is untagged, and none when it is tagged outside the table. With it come the H.273 primaries, transfer and matrix the picture is tagged with, the bits per channel the file stores, and whether it is HDR, which a transfer of PQ or HLG (16 or 18) makes it. An image sequence reports its first frame's.
- `loudness` measures the loudness and peaks of one sound with the meter of [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#loudness-meter): integrated loudness, loudness range, the largest momentary and short-term loudness, true peak and sample peak. The sound is a mix, mixed with the mixer role as `render` mixes, without rendering pictures, before it is clipped and encoded ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#measuring-loudness)): for a document, which it resolves first, its mix, the outermost bus's output after the sequence's `audioFilter`, and for a medium the mix of the sequence that plays one clip of it from media time 0 at `volume` 1, `pan` 0 and `speed` 1 with no `audioFilter`, as long as the clip's sound. `--from` and `--to` are media time for a medium and sequence time for a document, and measure an interval of the whole sound from `--from`, 0 when it is left out, to `--to`, the end of the sound when it is left out: the medium's sound, or the mix plan's length ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#the-mix-plan)). `--from` lies in `[0, end)` and `--to` after `--from` and at most at the end, and any other time stops before anything is measured. A medium without sound (an image, an image sequence, a video without a sound track) stops, since it is asked for a sound it does not have. A document with no sound has a silent mix, and every measure of it is `null`. A `.json` file, a pack template or `-` is a document to it, and any other path a medium. `--args` with a medium stops, since a medium has no parameters to take them. It writes no file, and without `--json` it prints the measures for people.
- `ocr` prints `size WxH` on the first line, then one line per recognised line, `[x, y, w, h]  text`, in media pixels. Over a range it does this for each frame, under a line with its media time. Lines come in the order a text area's `nth` counts them ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)). Without `--at` or a range a video is read at time 0. `--at` and `--from` lie in `[0, duration)`, `--to` at most at the duration, after `--from`, and `--step` is at least a microsecond. An image takes neither `--at` nor a range. A range is any of `--from`, `--to` and `--step`, and it needs `--step`, and `--at` with any of them stops. A frame's `time` is the media time asked, and the frame read is the one shown at it. `--languages` gives the recognition languages for this command in place of the global `--ocr-languages` ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)).
- `track` follows an area found at media time `--at`, backwards and forwards with the tracker provider, as `track` on a clip does ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)). `--area` is the JSON text of any area form of [FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md), `[x, y, w, h]` or a text form found in the frame at `--at`, and its messages name `--area`. The tracker follows the area as found, before its padding. It follows over the whole video, or with `--to` over the frames shown between `--at` and `--to`, which may lie on either side.
- `ocr` and `track` read the media in the colour space `--color-space` names, over what the file says, as a media clip's `colorSpace` corrects it ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), so they see the picture resolve's providers see for a clip that writes that `colorSpace`. The name is one whose Media column says yes, and any other name stops. Without it the file's tags apply.
- `extract` writes frames as PNG files. `sheet` lays frames out in one picture, each with its time label. Given a document they take composite frames at sequence times, as `still` draws them, and given a video the frames shown at media times. `sheet` scales each frame so its longer side is at most 480 pixels, never enlarging it, and lays the frames out left to right in `--cols` columns, or one column per frame when there are fewer frames, then top to bottom. `extract` writes each frame at full size and unlabelled, named by its index in time order with at least four digits (`0000.png`, `0001.png`), into the directory, which it makes when it is missing. A `.json` file, a pack template or `-` is a document to them, and any other path a video.
- `still`, `sheet` and `extract` write analysis pictures ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), SDR previews for looking at and the same normalised picture the providers read: PNGs in sRGB at 8 bits per channel, with HDR tone mapped and wide gamut clipped as it gives, whatever the sequence's or the video's colour space. They are not the master: the master of a `display-p3`, `rec2020` or HDR sequence is checked with `render --at`, which writes the frame in the sequence's colour space.
- `still`, `extract` and `sheet` stop on a time outside the video or sequence, on `--to` past its end, on `--from` not before `--to` and on a `--step` under a microsecond, before they write anything.
- `presets` lists presets by kind: a line with the kind, then a line for each of its presets, indented, with the name a document gives it and its description. With a name it prints that preset's JSON, its `params` included. `packs` lists the packs, the one that ships with frames and the installed ones, or what one pack holds. Under a preset or a template that publishes parameters, `packs <pack>` prints one line for each, indented, in the order they are declared: its name, its type, its default as JSON on one line (`-` when it has none) and its description. A template's lines come from its `params` alone, checked as they are where it is used, so a template whose media is not there still lists them, and a broken declaration stops the listing, naming its place.
- The analysis commands (`probe`, `loudness`, `ocr`, `track`, `extract`, `sheet`, `timeline`, `evaluate`, `locate`) report facts to write a document with. `ocr` and `track` use the providers, and `transcode` writes with the encoder role. All of them read media through the decoder role.
- Paths are absolute. A document argument may also name a pack template, `<pack>/<name>`, or be `-`, read from standard input.
- Times are seconds written as decimals, several separated by commas, a point is `x,y`, `--cols` and `--max` are whole numbers, and languages are BCP 47 tags separated by commas. A value that is not one, or is not finite, stops naming its option.
- A command frames does not have, `frames <name> ...`, runs `frames-<name>` from `PATH` with the global options before the name and the arguments after it, unchanged, and with standard input and output passed through ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)).
- `frames --version` prints the version ([FRAMES-18](../FRAMES-18/SPEC.md)). An extension checks it against the versions it is written for, as a pack does with `engine` in its `pack.json`.
- `frames --help` prints the usage, then each global option with what it takes and its default, to standard output and exits with 0. `frames <command> --help` prints that command's help to standard output and exits with 0, running nothing: its line of the usage, what it does, and each argument and option with what it takes and its default. An argument `--help` anywhere among a built-in command's arguments does this. An extension's arguments pass through unchanged, `--help` among them, so `frames <extension> --help` is the extension's own help.
- The global options before `--help` are checked as for any command, so `frames --renderer metal --help` stops. `--version` and `--help` stand alone after the global options, and an argument after either stops: `frames --version --help` and `frames --help render` stop, and a command's help is `frames <command> --help`.
- On success, stdout gets one line for each file written, with its path and size.
- On failure, stderr gets `frames: <reason>` and the exit code is 1. Without arguments, frames prints the usage to standard error and exits with 64, and so it does with global options and no command, since the command line is incomplete.
- The global options are checked before any command or extension runs, so an unknown role or provider name stops the same way for every command.
- Messages name the place, for example `format: unknown key "inset". Allowed: size, fps, background, conform, colorSpace, peak, compositing`. A place is a path of keys and indexes from the document, and the document itself is `top level`. A clip or op with an `id` is named with it after its index: `clips[1]#logo`.
- A message about a file other than the document (a theme, a preset, a template, a document named as media, an args file, a pack's `pack.json`) puts that file first, as it was referred to, then the place in that file: `looks/warm: defaults.transition (defaults): unknown transition preset "dissolve"`. A pack's `pack.json` is named by its path. What resolve finds inside a nested document or a template instance, once each is read and checked, is named by the place of the clip that holds it, continued as `timeline` continues it (`storyline[0].media.storyline[1]`).

### Time labels

`still` and `sheet` label each frame with its time in one fixed tool style. No theme changes it, so a check looks the same whatever the document's look, and it is laid out by frames itself, not by a document.

| Part | Value |
| --- | --- |
| text | the time in seconds, rounded to the microsecond in its shortest form, followed by `s`: `0s`, `2.5s`, `12.04s`. A video's frames are labelled with their media time, a document's with sequence time |
| size | S = max(14, round(L / 40)) pixels, where L is the longer side of the frame as it is drawn in the picture (48 for a 1920 by 1080 frame, 14 on a sheet frame 480 wide) |
| font | Helvetica Neue Medium (`HelveticaNeue-Medium` in `/System/Library/Fonts/HelveticaNeue.ttc`) at size S, shaped by the shaping provider ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) |
| colour | `#FFFFFFFF` text, no stroke |
| background | `#000000B3`, a rectangle around the text's line box (the font's ascent and descent at S), 0.25 S above and below it and 0.5 S left and right of it, with corners of radius 0.3 S |
| place | on the frame, the background's top left corner 0.5 S from the frame's top left corner, as an NLE burns a time into the picture |

- Frames lie S apart, with S around them, on an opaque `#202020FF` background, which their transparent parts show. A `still` of one time is that frame with its label and the same margin.

### transcode

`transcode` writes a delivery file from an image, audio or video file: a format and a quality level for where the file goes. Whether it is smaller than its source depends on the source.

| Input (judged by contents) | Output extension | Written as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| image | `.jpg`, `.heic`, `.png` | JPEG or HEIC at the quality level, HEIC at 10 bits per channel in an HDR colour space. PNG is lossless, and only `--max` changes it, at the source's bit depth, and 16 bits per channel in an HDR colour space |
| audio | `.m4a` | AAC, up to 2 channels and 48 kHz |
| video | `.mp4`, `.mov`, `.m4v` | H.264 (the default in an SDR colour space) or HEVC (Main 10, the default in an HDR colour space), with AAC sound |
| video | `.m4a` | the sound only |

| Quality | Image (0 to 1) | Video | Audio (per channel) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `high` | 0.9 | high | 96 kbps |
| `medium` (default) | 0.75 | medium | 64 kbps |
| `low` | 0.55 | low | 32 kbps |

- `--max` caps the longer side. A smaller source is never enlarged. Video sizes round down to even numbers, so neither the source nor `--max` is exceeded. A video whose side would round below 2 pixels, from its source or under `--max`, stops before anything is written, naming the size it would give, and `--max` when it is what shrank it.
- The output is in the source's colour space when a sequence can be output in it ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces), the Sequence column), and in `srgb` otherwise, so a source tagged outside the table is written as `srgb`. `--color-space` names the output's colour space instead, a name a sequence takes, and any other name stops. The source is read as a sequence of the output's colour space reads a media clip's with `toneMap` true ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), so an HDR source is tone mapped to the output space's peak when its source peak is above it, 1000 cd/m² for `rec2100-pq`, the default `format.peak`, and written as that sequence's output is encoded and tagged ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)). A source the decoder stops on for its colour, an image whose ICC profile is LUT-based or a video whose code points no formula defines ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)), is read by the platform's own colour management at relative intent instead: the one reading of frames that no fixed formula gives, made once, so the file it writes is read by the fixed formulas from then on.
- `--codec h264` with an HDR colour space stops, naming HEVC, since H.264 at 8 bits cannot carry HDR. A `.jpg` output in an HDR colour space stops, naming `.png` and `.heic`, since JPEG holds 8 bits.
- Metadata (EXIF, location) is not copied. Rotation is applied to the pixels. A JPEG gets transparent parts on white.
- A video is written at its frame rate: frame k is the source frame shown at k / fps, for every k whose time is before the video's duration and frame 0 always, as a sequence's frames are, so a variable frame rate source keeps its length.
- The video levels are the encoder's: with `avfoundation` they are `AVVideoQualityKey` 0.7, 0.5 and 0.3, for H.264 and HEVC alike.
- Sound with more than 2 channels is downmixed to stereo by ITU-R BS.775, as the mixer downmixes it, and a rate above 48 kHz is converted to 48 kHz. A bit rate too high or low for the sample rate moves to the nearest one the AAC encoder takes.
- The output line gives size, format and the ratio to the source: `<path>  <size>  <format>  <percent> of <source size>`, the percent with one decimal.
- The same file as input and output stops, compared with symbolic links resolved and then as the file system names it, so a hard link stops too. An extension that does not fit the input stops, compared ignoring case, and so do `--codec` for an output that is not a video, `--color-space` for an output of sound only and an output that is a directory. An existing output file is removed only after every check passes.

### Machine output

What frames reports about a document or a medium is a contract, so programs outside frames can build on it. With `--json`, and always for `evaluate`, `locate` and `track`, a command prints one JSON object whose first key is the command's name, set to the version of the frames that printed it. The text output is for people and is not part of the contract.

| Command | Object |
| --- | --- |
| `probe` | `{"probe": "1.0.0", kind, size, duration, fps, alpha, colorSpace, audio}`, `size` as `[w, h]` in pixels or `null` for sound, `colorSpace` as `{name, primaries, transfer, matrix, bitDepth, hdr}` or `null` for sound, `audio` as `{channels, sampleRate}` or `null`. In `colorSpace`, `name` is the table name or `null` outside the table, `primaries`, `transfer` and `matrix` are H.273 code points as the file is tagged, `null` for one it leaves out (an image tagged by an ICC profile gives those of the table row the profile describes, `null` outside the table, and a `matrix` of `null`), `bitDepth` is a whole number and `hdr` a boolean |
| `loudness` | `{"loudness": "1.0.0", integrated, range, momentaryMax, shortTermMax, truePeak, samplePeak}`, the measures of one sound: `integrated`, `momentaryMax` and `shortTermMax` in LUFS, `range` in LU, `truePeak` in dBTP and `samplePeak` in dBFS, each rounded to the nearest hundredth, halves rounded up, and `null` where [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#loudness-meter) gives none. For a document the sound is its mix, after the sequence's `audioFilter`, and for a medium the mix of one clip of it |
| `ocr` | `{"ocr": "1.0.0", size, languages, frames}`, `languages` the tags asked for or `null` where the provider detected them, each frame `{time, lines}` and each line `{text, rect}`. An image has one frame at time 0 |
| `track` | `{"track": "1.0.0", time, target, frames}`: the media time of `--at`, the target's quadrilateral there as found, and each frame followed as `{time, quad, motion}` in time order, a quadrilateral and a motion as the resolved document writes them ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) |
| `timeline` | `{"timeline": "1.0.0", duration, storyline, clips}`. A storyline entry has `place, id, media` (the file as the document wrote it, such as `broll/surf.mov`, the same on every machine, or `null` for a solid, a document written in place and a template instance), `edit`, `drawn`, `clip` (each `[start, end)`), `sound` (`[start, end)`, the range its sound plays over, or `null` for a clip that plays no sound), `speed`, `timeRemap` (whether the clip is time-remapped, when `speed` is 1) and `transition` (`{preset, seconds}` or `null`). A clips entry has `place, id, kind, visible, sound` (for a visual media clip the range its sound plays over, its `split` applied, `null` for every other clip and for one that plays no sound), `lane, storyline` (the place of the storyline clip it is connected to, or `null`), `parent` (the place of its parent clip, or `null`) and `animation` (`{entrance, loop, exit, by, stagger}` or `null`: each span `{preset, seconds}` for the entrance and exit and `{preset, period}` for the loop, or `null`, with the lengths that apply after theme defaults, and `by` and `stagger` the unit and the seconds between units of a title animated by unit, with its default `stagger` applied, both `null` otherwise). An entry whose media is a compound clip or a template instance also has `nested`, the timeline object of the document inside, with places continued from the entry's. [FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md) maps sequence time to clip time and media time from these values. A resolved document keeps no names ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), so its timeline gives each `preset` as `null`, `media` as the pinned file (an image sequence as the directory of its frames), and no audio clips, which it holds only as sound |
| `evaluate` | `{"evaluate": "1.0.0", time, size, items, texts}`, `items` bottom first, each `{place, id, kind, item, bounds, opacity, transform, rect, characters}`: the clip or op that drew it (`null` for the background and a transition cover; inside a nested list an item with none takes the place of the clip that holds the list), the item kind of [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md) (`picture`, `path`, `glyphs` or `adjust`, and `background` for the background), the output rectangle it covers after its transform, reveal and masks, `null` when it covers nothing, the map from its own coordinates to output pixels and its extent in them, whatever of it shows ([below](#item-transforms)), and where the characters it draws lie, `null` on an item that reports none ([below](#text-placement)). `texts` is the text of every title drawn at t ([below](#text-placement)). The background is an item only when it is not fully transparent. A clip used as a track matte draws no item of its own. Inside a nested document a place continues the place of the clip that holds it (`storyline[0].media.storyline[2]`) |
| `locate` | `{"locate": "1.0.0", hits}`, topmost first: every item under the point, each `{place, id, kind, item}`, and for a picture of a media file also `{media, mediaTime, point}`: the pinned absolute path of the file the frame is read from (an image sequence's frame file), since a resolved document keeps no written spelling, the frame's pinned media time, and the point in that frame's pixels, `null` for a picture that shows its whole frame (a transition cover), whose pixel size evaluation does not know since it opens no file. An item is under the point when its shape holds it: a picture's `dest`, a path's fill (its geometry, closed) or the band of its stroke as drawn with its arrowheads, what a glyphs item draws ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)) and an adjustment's whole frame, carried by its transforms and cut by its reveal, its masks (their geometry without feather, inverted and combined by their modes) and the pictures that hold it. What a glyphs item draws is its drawn extent, its unit's for the item of one unit of a title animated by unit, grown by its stroke and joined with its background and the highlights, underlines or parts it draws, and for a title's background drawn alone under its units its background, nothing when the title has none. Rounded corners, joins, caps, feather, transition mattes, track mattes and transparent pixels are not told apart |

- Fields follow [FRAMES-17](../FRAMES-17/SPEC.md): a new field is an addition, and a removed or changed one a major change. The old form is no longer printed ([the principles](../README.md#principles), no legacy keys).
- Places are those of the messages above.
- The `kind` of a clip in `timeline`, `evaluate` and `locate` is the clip's kind: `video` (an image sequence included), `image`, `audio` (a clip written with `"picture": false` included), `compound` (a sequence as media, a template instance included), `title`, `rect`, `ellipse`, `path`, `adjustment` or `solid`. It names what the document wrote, and `item` names what the clip drew ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)), so a `title` draws `glyphs`, a `rect` a `path` and an `adjustment` an `adjust` item.
- Times and lengths are written rounded to the nearest microsecond, in their shortest form (`2.85`, not `2.850000`), so floating point noise is not reported as a fact. A microsecond is far below a frame at any rate, so no frame changes.
- Positions, sizes, opacities and the numbers of a `transform` are written rounded to the nearest millionth, for the same reason. A range is whole numbers.
- The display list and the mix plan are the roles' wire forms ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), not part of this output.

### Item transforms

**Every item of `evaluate` reports `transform`, the map from its own coordinates to output pixels, and `rect`, where it lies in them**, since where an item lies is a fact of the item, and `bounds`, the axis-aligned box of what shows of it, cannot say where a rotated, skewed or nested one lies, nor where one cut by a mask or a reveal lies whole.

- It is `[a, b, c, d, tx, ty]` in the matrix form of the display list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#fields-every-item-has)): a point (x, y) of the item's own coordinates is at (a x + c y + tx, b x + d y + ty) in the output.
- It is P_n ∘ R_n ∘ … ∘ P_1 ∘ R_1 ∘ T, applied from the right: T is the item's `transform`, and the pictures that hold the list the item is in are numbered from 1, the innermost, to n, the outermost, each with R_k the map from its list's pixels into the picture, (x, y) to (dx + (x - rx) × dw / rw, dy + (y - ry) × dh / rh) with `[rx, ry, rw, rh]` its `rect`, the list's whole frame where it is `null`, and `[dx, dy, dw, dh]` its `dest`, and P_k its `transform`. An item of the outermost list has n = 0. A picture whose source is a nested list is no item of `evaluate`: its list's background and items are, each carried by the maps of the pictures that hold it. The picture that draws a title by unit as a group maps its list onto itself with the identity, so a unit's `transform` is its item's, the title's with the unit's motion.
- An item's own coordinates are those `bounds` is the box of, carried by `transform` and cut by its reveal, its masks and the pictures that hold it. For a `picture` they are the coordinates its `dest` lies in. For a `path` they are its geometry's, which its stroke and arrowheads are drawn on. For a `glyphs` item they are its title's text box at t, of its `size` with its origin at the top left, where its glyphs, its `unitBox`, its characters' rects and its title's lines lie ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and not the title's box, which the background's padding grows from it: for a title whose text box is 200 by 56 and whose background `padding` is `[8, 12]`, the item's `size` is `[200, 56]`, its `rect` `[0, 0, 200, 56]` where no glyph it draws reaches outside the text box, and its background's `rect` `[-12, -8, 224, 72]`, and a padding that takes keyframes moves no character's rect. For an `adjust` item and the `background` they are the pixels of the list the item is in, whose whole frame it covers, and T is left out: an adjustment's own transform carries only its `reveal` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and the background has none.
- `rect` is `[x, y, w, h]`, the item's extent in its own coordinates as the display list gives the item, before its reveal, its masks and the pictures that hold it cut it: a `picture`'s `dest`, the box of a `path`'s geometry, without its stroke, a `glyphs` item's `extent`, the drawn extent of its unit or its title ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units), [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and the whole frame of its list for an `adjust` item and the `background`. So a program finds where a rotated picture lies, whole, as `rect` carried by `transform`.
- Items inside a track matte's source are not items of `evaluate`, since a clip used as a matte draws no item of its own, and a transition matte is a picture's `matte`, not an item, so neither reports a `transform` or text.

### Text placement

**`evaluate` reports how a title's text is split apart from where it lies**: `texts` gives each title's lines and words once, whole, whatever animates it, with the text box they lie in, and each character is reported on the item that draws it, so a program measures and fits text on the lines frames draws and lays out none again ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)). A line has one place in the text box and a word lies where its characters lie, so nothing depends on how a title's units fall and no entry is ever missing.

- `texts` holds one entry for each title drawn at t, in the order of its first item, each `{place, id, text, size, box, transform, lines, words}`: the title's place and `id` as its items name them, its `text` ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), its text box's `size` at t as `[w, h]`, `box`, its title's box at t, the text box grown by its background's `padding` at t ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#titles)), as `[x, y, w, h]` in the text box, `[0, 0, w, h]` for a title without a background, `transform`, the map from its text box to output pixels without any unit's motion, in the form of an item's ([Item transforms](#item-transforms)), and its lines and its words in reading order ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)), each line `{text, range, baseline, ascent, descent}` and each word `{text, range}`, `range` its `[start, end)` in Unicode scalars over the title's text and `text` the title's text over it. A word a line breaks inside is one word, and a line with no character has an empty range. A line's `baseline` is the y of its baseline in the text box at t, the line height at t applied and no `baselineShift`, which belongs to characters, and `ascent` and `descent` are its line's ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#lines)), so a line with no character has a place and a line whose characters are all shifted keeps the baseline they are shifted from. A word has no place of its own. Lines lie in the text box, which `transform` carries to the output. The text box is a coordinate system of the title, not of an item, since every item of the title has it as its own coordinates, so its `size`, `box` and `transform` are the title's entry's: for a title not animated by unit `transform` is its one item's, and for a title animated by unit it is the title's, which each unit's item composes with the unit's motion, so a program places a title's lines without knowing which of its items is which. A title is drawn at t where it draws an item at t: outside its window it draws none, and a title used as a track matte draws none of its own, so neither has an entry. A time label is drawn by `still` and `sheet` only ([Time labels](#time-labels)), so `evaluate` reports none.
- An item's `characters` is the characters it reports, in reading order, each `{text, range, rect, baseline}`: `text` and `range` as above, `rect` its glyph box at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) as `[x, y, w, h]` and `baseline` the y of the baseline it sits on, its line's baseline at t less its run's `baselineShift` at t and never a glyph's y, both in the item's own coordinates, the text box at t, which its `transform` carries to the output. It is a list on a `glyphs` item of `layer` `null` that holds runs and on a unit's item of `layer` `fills` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and `null` on every other item, a title's background drawn alone and the unit items of `highlights`, `underlines` and `strokes` included, so each character is reported once, with the item that draws its fill.
- A title not animated by unit reports every character on its one item. In a title animated by unit a unit's `fills` item reports the characters of its unit, those the unit holds and the spaces that go with it, as the resolved unit names them ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units), [FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), so every character of the title is reported once and `evaluate` assigns none. A space is drawn and reported with the unit it goes with, and a space that hangs past a line's end, which is no character, is drawn by a unit ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) and reported by none. `evaluate` tells which unit an item is by its `origin`, which names the title, and its `unit`, which names the unit ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)).
- `evaluate` places each character from the resolved title ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) and splits no text itself: its `rect` is its box at the static values, from its first glyph's pen x and its cluster's `advance` and its line's `baseline`, `ascent` and `descent`, moved as its glyphs move at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) and taken into the text box at t as they are. A character with no glyph of its own has the `rect` and `baseline` of its group's first character ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)). So a ligature's characters share its box, a character of several scalars or glyphs is one box, a placement offset moves no box, the tracking after a character lies between boxes and in none, and the same glyphs drawn at a static tracking or at a keyframed one of the same value give the same boxes.
- A word lies where its characters lie: the characters whose ranges are inside its range, on the items that report them, so a word a line breaks inside lies on both lines, and a word over several units lies on their items.
- Where text lies does not depend on what of it shows: `reveal`, masks, a track matte and `opacity` cut what is seen of an item and leave its `rect` and its `characters` as they are. `bounds` and `opacity` report what shows.

## Alternatives considered

A separate command or document kind for stills: a still is a frame of a sequence, so `render` writes it with `--at` and there is one way to draw a picture.

Time labels in the theme's text styles: a check would look different under every look, and a theme without such a style could not be checked at all. A fixed tool style keeps the check apart from the result.

Time labels under each frame on a band of their own: a label burnt into the corner, as NLEs overlay timecode, keeps a sheet as tight as its frames and reads the same at any size.

An API or a server in place of commands: commands with JSON output are what any agent, shell and language already drive, and frames keeps no state between them for a session to hold.

`transcode` defined as making a file smaller: a delivery file is written for where it goes at a quality level, and whether it is smaller depends on how its source was written.

`transcode` refusing a source no fixed formula reads, as the decoder does: such a file could not enter an edit at all without another tool. Read once by the platform's colour management, it becomes a file of the table that every later reading converts by formula, and the resolved document pins that file.

Ink bounds of text, the box of its glyphs' outlines: evaluation would have to open font files, which it does not ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), and two shapers' outlines differ. A glyph box is made of advances, ascents and descents, which the resolved document already fixes, and a program that needs the drawn outline measures a frame from `extract` (README).

A separate command for text layout: it would be a second report of the same instant, where `evaluate` already names the clip and the time.

Text placement under `locate`: a point names what lies under it, and cannot list a caption's words. A program hit tests a point against the characters' rects and their items' `transform` itself.

Entries for glyphs: a glyph id means nothing without its font, and a character's rect already places it.

Ranges counted in UTF-16 code units, as Core Text and JavaScript count them: the contract would follow one platform's string type. Unicode scalars are how Python indexes a string and how Swift's `unicodeScalars` and Rust's `chars` count it, and each entry's `text` spares a program counting at all.

A title by unit reporting its characters on its background item: the rects would lie where the text rests, not where its units are drawn. Each unit's item reports the characters that move with it.

A rect for each word, reported with the item that draws it: a word spread over several units has no one item to report it, so whether a word is reported would hang on how its title is animated, and a program would split the text again where it is not. The split is reported once for each title and holds under any animation. A line is reported with its place because every item of a title shares its text box, so a line has one baseline, ascent and descent there whatever animates its characters, and an empty line or the baseline under a shifted run is known only so.

A line that lies only where its characters lie: a line with no character would lie nowhere, though it takes its height in the text box, and a line whose characters are all shifted would lose the baseline they are shifted from.

A title's lines placed through one of its items, by a `layer` reported on every item or by a rule that a title's first item draws its background: a program would have to know an order or a field of the display list to find the coordinates its lines lie in, and the text box, which every item of the title shares, belongs to none of them. The title's entry carries the text box's `size` and `transform`, and the title's `box` in it, as an item carries its own.

A character's box from where its glyphs are drawn: a placement offset, which sets a combining mark over its base and with which some fonts kern, would move or narrow a box where the pen did not move, a mark drawn back over its base would cut its character's box to part of the base's width, and two shapers that split a position between advance and offset differently would give two boxes. The pen is where the line is laid out, so the resolved glyph writes its pen position apart from its placement offset ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)).

A `glyphs` item in the title's box, the text box grown by the background's padding: a padding that takes keyframes would move every character's rect while nothing moves on screen, and a title's mask, which lies in its text box ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)), would lie in other coordinates than its glyphs.

A unit's index written on its item: a renderer would carry a field it draws nothing from, as with text placement on the display list. The order a title by unit draws its `fills` items in is a rule of the display list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#glyphs-and-adjustments)), and `evaluate` reads it.

Text placement carried on the display list's `glyphs` items: a renderer would carry a field it draws nothing from, and the display list, a renderer's contract, would hold the rules of a report. `evaluate` computes it from the resolved title with the moves that place the glyphs, which keeps it on the drawn glyphs as well.

`transform` only on items that draw text: the map from an item's coordinates to the output is a fact of every item, and a program that covers a region of a rotated picture needs it, with the picture's `rect`, as a caption fitter needs a title's lines with its `transform`.

`transform` without `rect`: a map with no domain, since `bounds` is cut by masks and reveal and cannot give back where a picture's `dest`, a path's geometry or a title's glyphs lie whole.

A title's box left out of `texts`: a boxed title covers its background's padding as well as its text box, and a program that keeps captions clear of other text would know only half of what it covers.

A character's box on its line's baseline, without `baselineShift`: a raised or bouncing character would be reported where it is not drawn, and the `baseline` it reports would not be the one it sits on.

Tracking inside a character's box, at the end of its advance: one shaper adds it to advances and another does not, and the same glyphs drawn at a static tracking and at a keyframed one of the same value would give different boxes.
