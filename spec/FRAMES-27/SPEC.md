# FRAMES-27: Corner pin

Status: draft

## Summary

`cornerPin` places a clip's picture, drawn flat in its own box, by the projective map (homography) that takes the four corners of its box to four points in the clip's coordinate space, as the Corner Pin of After Effects and Premiere Pro and the Distort of Final Cut Pro move a picture's four corners. This spec gives the key and the clips that take it, the homography of four point pairs and its closed form, which corner pins stop and which draw nothing, and how a tracked corner pin rides the homography from the target's quadrilateral to its quadrilateral on each frame, so a perspective track, from a tracker provider or written in the document as track data ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), keeps a picture on a moving plane. Evaluation draws a clip with a corner pin as a group whose `picture` item carries the corner pin ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)).

## Motivation

A screen replaced in a shot, a poster put on a wall, a sign given new words, a title laid on a table top: each puts a picture on a plane seen in perspective, where the far edge is shorter than the near one. The live set maps a picture by affine maps only. A compound clip turned, scaled per axis and turned again gives any parallelogram ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), and no parallelogram has edges that converge. Outside frames, such a picture would be baked into media, warped frame by frame by another tool and brought back, and every change of its content would bake it again. A tracked one would be worse: After Effects' tracker writes the four points of a Corner Pin as keyframes on every frame, one fact spread over a value per frame, and those keyframes would sit on clip time, where a hold, a reverse or a `timeRemap` must play the track by media time ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)). With one key, the picture stays a clip whose content is edited as any other, and a tracked corner pin is one corner pin and one track.

## Rules

### The key

`cornerPin` is a list of four points ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), in any form a point takes where it is written, in the clip's coordinate space ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)): where the top left, top right, bottom right and bottom left corners of the clip's box go, in that order, the order of a quadrilateral ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). The four points make the clip's **corner pin**. It is keyframable, its points interpolated point by point as a list of points ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)). It has no default, and a clip without it is placed as [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) says.

| Clip | `cornerPin` |
| --- | --- |
| a title, a media clip with a picture, a rect or ellipse given by `width` and `height` (a clip with a box of its own, [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)) | taken: the corners of its box ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)) |
| a storyline clip with media | taken: the corners of its storyline picture's box ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)) |
| a shape placed by `area` or `points` | stops: its outline is points in its space already. A shape that must be drawn in perspective is put in a compound clip with a corner pin |
| a solid, an adjustment clip, an audio clip | not a key of theirs ([FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md)): a solid is the frame, an adjustment draws no picture of its own, and an audio clip draws none. A filter that must follow a plane, a number plate blurred in a shot, is an adjustment clip whose track matte is a shape with a corner pin ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#adjustment-clips)) |

- **A clip with a corner pin is placed by it**, as a shape is placed by its `area` ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). A clip that writes `cornerPin` and any of `placement`, `position`, `anchor`, `scale` and `rotation`, on the clip or in a keyframe, stops, naming both keys. It takes none of the placement defaults of [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), a style's placement included, so a title whose style gives a placement is placed by its corner pin.
- Its box is what it draws, at the size [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) gives it: a title's text box with its background, a media clip's box by its `width`, `height` and crop, a storyline picture's box, a rect's or ellipse's `width` by `height`. The corner pin decides only where that box's corners go, the box as it is at t.
- **Its own space is its plane.** The clip is drawn flat in its box B, `[0, 0, w, h]` with its origin at the box's top left, with everything it does: the transform part of its animation, whose scale and rotation act about the centre of B and whose `x` and `y` are multiples of B's size ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), its reveal, the animations of its units, its masks ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)), its strokes, its shadow and its filter. The corner pin's map K ([What stops and what draws nothing](#what-stops-and-what-draws-nothing)) carries that flat picture to the screen. So an entrance that slides or scales does so on the plane, and a scale grows from the point K takes B's centre to, where the corner pin's diagonals cross. Nothing of the clip's animation acts in its parent's space. A storyline clip's transition motion is not its animation: it belongs to the storyline's outer item ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)), so it moves the pinned picture in the frame's space, while a connected clip's entrance animation moves it on its plane. Its own size ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)) is B's size, with no other meaning.
- **Its corner pin bounds** at t, a name this spec gives, are the axis-aligned rectangle that bounds its corner pin as written at t, before its tracked motion, as any clip gives its box before its own transform ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md#clip-areas)): Q(t) for an untracked corner pin, and Q'(t), its keyframes brought back and interpolated, for a tracked one ([A tracked corner pin](#a-tracked-corner-pin)). They are one rectangle, which a clip area that names the clip takes, from a sibling and from a child alike, and they are defined at every instant, also where the corner pin draws nothing. `evaluate` reports for the clip's group the `bounds` it reports for every item, what it covers in output pixels ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)), which is not its corner pin bounds.
- **A corner pin carries no children.** Transforms stay affine ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), so a child of a clip with a corner pin cannot lie on its plane. A child of a clip with a corner pin is placed in the space the corner pin is written in and follows the clip's tracked motion M, a similarity ([A tracked corner pin](#a-tracked-corner-pin)), and not the plane. What should lie on the plane goes inside the clip with the corner pin: a compound clip with a corner pin holds it.

**Note.** A corner pin takes the clip's box at t to its four points, so a clip whose box changes over time is stretched onto the same four points: a title whose `tracking`, `lineHeight` or background padding is keyframed, or whose text a template's argument gives, keeps its corners where the corner pin puts them, and letters spaced wider are squeezed back into its width. A plane whose content changes size is a compound clip of a fixed size with the corner pin, the title placed inside it.

### The homography

A **homography** of the plane maps a point (x, y) to (x', y'):

```
x' = (a x + c y + tx) / w
y' = (b x + d y + ty) / w          w = g x + h y + i
```

It is the matrix `[[a, c, tx], [b, d, ty], [g, h, i]]` acting on (x, y, 1), its first six numbers named as in the display list's `transform` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)). Two matrices that differ by a factor other than 0 give the same map. With g = h = 0 it is affine. The points where w is 0 make its **horizon**, the line it sends to infinity. A homography is the formula evaluation and the renderer use, computed from four point pairs, and no document or report writes one.

**Strict convexity.** A quadrilateral q₀, q₁, q₂, q₃ (top left, top right, bottom right, bottom left) is **strictly convex** when its four turns, with indices taken modulo 4,

```
z_k = (x_{k+1} - x_k) (y_{k+2} - y_{k+1}) - (y_{k+1} - y_k) (x_{k+2} - x_{k+1})        k = 0, 1, 2, 3
```

are all above 0 or all below 0. Each z_k is computed in IEEE 754 double precision as written, each product rounded before the subtraction and no fused multiply-add, and a z_k of 0 is neither above nor below 0: a quadrilateral with three corners on one line, or two on one point, is not strictly convex. Its **orientation** is the sign its four turns share. All above 0 runs clockwise in y-down pixels, and all below 0 is that quadrilateral mirrored, as a negative scale mirrors a clip ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). Every three of its four corners are three consecutive ones, so a strictly convex quadrilateral has no three corners on one line. The test decides alike in every implementation for points written in the document. Points that are computed, brought back through a homography, eased between keyframes or carried by a track, are computed in an order and a rounding this spec does not fix, so they may differ between implementations within rounding, and a test whose turns lie within rounding of 0 may decide one way in one implementation and the other way in another.

**The square to a quadrilateral.** S(q) is the homography that takes the corners of the unit square, (0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1), (0, 1), to q₀, q₁, q₂, q₃, with i = 1. For q strictly convex it is:

```
Δx₁ = x₁ - x₂      Δx₂ = x₃ - x₂      Σx = x₀ - x₁ + x₂ - x₃
Δy₁ = y₁ - y₂      Δy₂ = y₃ - y₂      Σy = y₀ - y₁ + y₂ - y₃
D   = Δx₁ Δy₂ - Δx₂ Δy₁

g  = (Σx Δy₂ - Δx₂ Σy) / D          h  = (Δx₁ Σy - Σx Δy₁) / D
a  = x₁ - x₀ + g x₁                 b  = y₁ - y₀ + g y₁
c  = x₃ - x₀ + h x₃                 d  = y₃ - y₀ + h y₃
tx = x₀                             ty = y₀                    i = 1
```

**Note.** The closed form solves the eight unknowns from the four point pairs, two equations a pair. (0, 0) to q₀ gives w = 1, tx = x₀ and ty = y₀. (1, 0) to q₁ gives w = 1 + g, so x₁ (1 + g) = a + x₀ and y₁ (1 + g) = b + y₀. (0, 1) to q₃ gives w = 1 + h, so x₃ (1 + h) = c + x₀ and y₃ (1 + h) = d + y₀. (1, 1) to q₂ gives w = 1 + g + h, so x₂ (1 + g + h) = a + c + x₀, which with a and c put in is g (x₁ - x₂) + h (x₃ - x₂) = x₀ - x₁ + x₂ - x₃, that is g Δx₁ + h Δx₂ = Σx, and from y the same, g Δy₁ + h Δy₂ = Σy. Cramer's rule on these two gives g and h, and they give a, b, c and d. D is 0 only where q₁, q₂ and q₃ lie on one line. Σx = Σy = 0 exactly where q is a parallelogram (q₀ + q₂ = q₁ + q₃), and then g = h = 0 and S(q) is the affine map of the parallelogram.

**From one quadrilateral to another.** For strictly convex p and q, **H(p → q)** is the one homography that takes p₀, p₁, p₂, p₃ to q₀, q₁, q₂, q₃: the matrix S(q) × S(p)⁻¹. A point lies **in front of** the horizon of H(p → q) when w there has the sign w has inside p, and a point where w is 0 does not. Its w has one sign over the inside of p exactly when q is strictly convex, so the inside of p lies in front of its horizon, and H(p → q) takes it onto the inside of q without passing through infinity. For a q that is not strictly convex, part of p would go through infinity and the picture would fold. No rule needs the matrix scaled: where a rule compares w, it compares w's sign at a point with its sign inside p, which no factor changes.

### What stops and what draws nothing

The **corner pin's map** at t, K(t), is H(B → Q(t)), from the corners of the clip's box B, `[0, 0, w, h]` with its origin at the box's top left, to the corner pin Q(t) as the clip's group draws it at t ([A tracked corner pin](#a-tracked-corner-pin)). **Whether a corner pin places a picture depends on its four points and a box with area**: on the four points by the test of [The homography](#the-homography), and on B having a width and a height above 0, never on what the clip draws around its box. Where B has a width or a height of 0 at t, as a keyframed size can reach ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)), no homography takes its corners to four points, and the clip draws nothing at t, as a scale of 0 draws nothing.

- A corner pin, written or found ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), at a keyframe's time or as a static one, that is not strictly convex stops at `resolve`, naming the place, and the time for a keyframe.
- **One rule decides where a corner pin draws.** At t it draws when the clip's box has area and its corner pin as written at t, Q'(t) ([A tracked corner pin](#a-tracked-corner-pin)), lies wholly in front of the horizon of H(t), and the corner pin it carries there, H(t) applied to each corner of Q'(t), is strictly convex. Otherwise it draws nothing at that instant, as a scale of 0 draws nothing, and on either side of such an instant the clip draws as its values say. An untracked corner pin has H the identity, so it draws where its box has area and Q(t) is strictly convex. This is the test a keyframe is brought back by, and it draws nothing where all four corners lie beyond the horizon, though the carried corner pin is strictly convex there again, seen from behind its plane.
- **What the clip draws outside B**, a stroke, an outset, a shadow, or a unit or the whole picture moved past the box by its animation, is carried by the same K where K's w has the sign it has inside B, in front of its horizon, and is not drawn at or beyond the horizon ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)). The picture itself is never cut there: B goes onto a strictly convex corner pin, so it lies wholly in front, and only what reaches past B can reach the horizon, where a plane seen in a shot ends too. Near the horizon K stretches lengths without bound, and what bounds the raster of that part is the renderer's pixel limit for a nested list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)).
- A tracked frame whose quadrilateral is not strictly convex, or is strictly convex with the other orientation than the target's, is not followed ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), for every tracked clip.

**Note.** A corner pin's corners interpolate one by one ([FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md)). Between two keyframes of opposite orientation, a card flipped by keyframing its corner pin mirrored, the four turns change sign on the way, so the interpolated corner pin passes through corner pins that are not strictly convex and draws nothing there, and once it is strictly convex again the card shows its picture mirrored, as a negative scale mirrors it. A clip has no other face: the other orientation is the mirror and nothing else, and a card whose back shows something else is two clips, its front and its back. A corner pin keyframed turned half way round, each corner going to the opposite one, passes through a point in the middle of the span and draws nothing there. The middle of a turn by an angle φ is the corner pin turned by φ / 2 and scaled by cos(φ / 2), so a turn keyframed in steps of less than half a turn stays strictly convex, and a parent turns a corner pin whole ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)).

### A tracked corner pin

A clip with a corner pin is placed by points written in its parent's space, so it follows its parent's media ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#what-is-tracked)), and `true` and `{"time": t}` follow its corner pin. Its own crop and mask are not followed: they lie flat on its plane and ride its corner pin, as a title's mask rides its transform. A storyline clip with a corner pin has no parent's media, and `track` on it stops. On each frame followed, the track has its motion M, the similarity of [FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md), and its homography H, which takes the target's quadrilateral to that frame's, H(target → quad). At an instant τ of the clip, M(τ) and H(τ) are those of the media frame the tracked media shows at τ, as for every tracked value.

- **A corner pin is where it is at its own time**, as every tracked value is. Each keyframe's corner pin Q_k, at its time τ_k, is brought back through H(τ_k)⁻¹, corner by corner. A keyframe whose corner pin does not lie wholly in front of the horizon of H(τ_k)⁻¹, or whose corner pin brought back is not strictly convex, stops at `resolve`, naming the keyframe: a corner at or beyond that horizon would come back through infinity. It is the test of where a corner pin draws ([What stops and what draws nothing](#what-stops-and-what-draws-nothing)), made through the inverse. A keyframe at a time whose media frame was not followed is brought back through the nearest frame followed, as every tracked value is ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#where-the-target-is-lost)). The brought-back corner pins interpolate between keyframes as [FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md) says, giving Q'(t), and the corner pin at t is H(t) applied to each corner of Q'(t), in the clip's coordinate space. A static corner pin is where it is on the track's frame, the frame the `track` object's `time` names, or for `true` and an area the one the clip's instant shows ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#the-track)), so it is Q'(t) itself. So a corner pin written on one frame with its corners on a screen's corners stays on them on every frame followed, whichever tracker or track data gave the quadrilaterals, and where `track` names that frame by `time` a trim of the clip's head moves nothing.
- **The motion stays the moving parent.** The clip's transform holds M(t) and nothing else, its animation acting on its plane ([The key](#the-key)), so its children follow M as they follow a tracked shape ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)). The corner pin is drawn inside that transform as Q(t) = M(t)⁻¹ H(t) applied to each corner of Q'(t), the part of the homography the motion does not carry. An untracked corner pin is drawn as written, Q(t) its keyframes interpolated at t, and its clip's transform is the identity, so its children are placed as under the clip's own parent.
- Where every quadrilateral of the track is a similarity image of the target, as the `point` tracker reports, H is M, the corner pin is drawn as Q'(t), and a tracked corner pin moves as a tracked shape placed by its area moves.

```json
{ "id": "phone", "media": "/abs/broll/phone.mov", "in": 0, "out": 6,
  "connected": [
    { "id": "screen", "at": [0, 6], "parent": "phone", "media": "/abs/ui/app-demo.mov",
      "cornerPin": [ [812, 304], [1131, 352], [1102, 918], [779, 861] ],
      "track": { "time": 0,
                 "frames": [ { "time": 0, "quad": [ [812, 304], [1131, 352], [1102, 918], [779, 861] ] },
                             { "time": 0.04, "quad": [ [815, 302], [1133, 355], [1103, 921], [781, 862] ] } ] } } ] }
```

A recording of an app is placed on a phone's screen in the shot, its corner pin written on the shot's first frame in the shot's media pixels, the frame the data's `time` names, and the track data of a planar tracker run outside frames, cut short here, keeps it on the screen as the phone turns. With `"track": {"time": 0}` in place of the data, the tracker provider follows the corner pin itself from that frame.

### What a renderer holds

A renderer ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) carries a picture onto its `cornerPin` by W = H(`dest` → `cornerPin`), the homography from the corners of its `dest` to its corner pin ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)). For a clip's group W is the corner pin's map K(t), since its `dest` is B and its corner pin Q(t). The renderer holds three things:

- **Exact corners.** The corners of `dest`, not the centres of its corner pixels, land on the four points, and every point of `dest` lands where W takes it.
- **Values as they are.** Values stay premultiplied working values in extended range ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#working-values)), with no clamp, no colour conversion, and no unpremultiply and premultiply between, so a pixel at alpha 0 keeps its colour and no colour reaches past alpha at the edges.
- **Area sampling.** Each output pixel of the carried picture is the mean of the source over that pixel's image under W⁻¹: the mean, over the pixel's square, of the source read at W⁻¹ of each point, transparent outside the source region. The source is the picture the item shows: for a picture of pixels, the bilinear reconstruction of its pixels, each pixel's value at its centre, and for a nested list, what the list draws, at any scale, so the raster a renderer draws the list to is part of how it approximates the mean. A renderer approximates that mean, and [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) tests how closely, on a named set of stills, whose own pixels give the mean. One sample at each output pixel's centre does not approximate it where W shrinks the picture: its edges are stair-stepped and a fine pattern aliases.

It does not promise a particular filter, equality with another engine's resampler, the same bytes on another GPU family or OS version (the same machine gives the same bytes, [FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)), or a hard edge sharper than one source pixel where W enlarges the picture: the mean is taken of the bilinear reconstruction, so such an edge ramps over one source pixel, half of it outside `dest`.

## Interactions

Placement and a clip's box are [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), points and clip areas [FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md), keyframes [FRAMES-4](../FRAMES-4/SPEC.md), keys [FRAMES-7](../FRAMES-7/SPEC.md). A clip with a corner pin gives its children the space its corner pin is written in, moved by its tracked motion and not by its plane ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)), and its masks stay in its own space, carried with its picture ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)). Its animation, which acts on its plane, is [FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md). The track, its homography, track data and lost frames are [FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md). The resolved document writes the corner pin and the track's quadrilaterals, from which evaluation computes each frame's homography ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)). Evaluation draws a clip with a corner pin as a group whose `picture` carries a `cornerPin` ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)), and the renderer draws it ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) as [What a renderer holds](#what-a-renderer-holds) says. `evaluate` and `locate` report the items of a clip with a corner pin ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)).

## Alternatives considered

A projective `transform` on every display list item, nine numbers in place of six: every renderer would draw strokes, glyphs, masks and feathers under a projective map, which Core Graphics and SVG draw under affine maps only, when only a picture needs to be carried onto a quadrilateral. `cornerPin` is a field of a `picture`, and a clip with a corner pin is drawn as a group, so its strokes, glyphs and masks are drawn flat in its box and carried with its picture.

Corner pins in the clip's box coordinates with its `position`, `scale` and `rotation` applied after them: two ways to place one clip, and a picture placed on a moving screen is written where the screen is, in its parent's space, not where a transform will take it.

Lengths, strokes and shadows drawn after the corner pin, in output pixels: a stroke around a card with a corner pin would keep one width while the card's far edge shrinks, and a shadow would fall off the plane it belongs to. Drawn in the box and carried with the picture, they lie on the plane as the picture does.

The homography for every tracked value (points, areas, masks, crops), or for a path's points alone: a homography takes a polygon to a polygon, so a path would stay a path, and a clip with a corner pin moves apart from a sibling rect on the same track already, so neither stops it. What does is that every followed value of a clip other than a corner pin is carried by one fit, the similarity, so values written in one space keep the relations they were written with, a path mask's point laid on a rect mask's corner staying on it, where a path carried by the homography beside a rect carried by the similarity would part them. A clip with a corner pin follows its corner pin alone ([A tracked corner pin](#a-tracked-corner-pin)). A shape that must follow perspective is put in a compound clip with a corner pin, and a filter that must follow a plane is an adjustment clip whose track matte is a shape with a corner pin.

A bilinear map of the four corners, as Fusion's Corner Positioner offers beside its perspective mapping: it bends straight lines, so a screen's straight edges and the lines inside it would curve, and it is not the map a plane seen through a camera takes.

A corner pin of three points with the fourth computed so that the sides stay parallel: that is an affine map, which a compound clip already gives ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), and four points hold it as the case Σx = Σy = 0.

Corner pins that are not strictly convex drawn anyway, folded or cut at the horizon: the picture would pass through infinity, which no plane in a shot does, so such a corner pin is not a placement of a picture and stops, or draws nothing between keyframes. A strictly convex corner pin keeps its whole picture in front of the horizon, so what is cut there is only what the clip draws past its box, and that is cut as a real plane ends.

A corner pin drawn wherever the corner pin it carries is strictly convex: with all four corners beyond the horizon it is strictly convex again, with the other orientation, and the plane would be drawn mirrored where the camera cannot see it. Checking the orientation as well would be a second rule beside the one carry-back needs, where being in front of the horizon is one.

Corner pin bounds taken after the tracked motion, the rectangle that bounds the corner pin as drawn: a sibling would follow the track where a tracked title's sibling does not, and a sibling and a child naming the same clip would get different rectangles, since a bounding box does not turn with what it bounds.

A corner pin that places a plane on which the clip's box may grow and shrink: the plane would need a rectangle of its own that the four points are the corners of, a key no clip has, where a compound clip of a fixed size already is that rectangle.

A point form that gives another clip's position after its tracked motion, so that four tracked shapes give a corner pin its points: a clip area gives a clip's box before its own transform, as corner pin bounds do ([The key](#the-key)), and a form after it would be a second rule for one name. Four point tracks written as track data by an extension give the same corner pin.

A corner pin judged by the corners of everything the clip draws, its shadow and strokes included: a theme length, a shadow's radius, would then decide whether a placement stops or draws, and the same corner pin would stop under one style and not under another.

## Notes

**Note.** Four point tracks drive a corner pin as an extension composes them ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md)): it runs `track` on four targets and writes track data whose quad on each frame is the four targets' centres there, in the corner pin's order.

**Note.** The pixel limit that bounds a nested list's raster is the renderer's own ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), and no rule fixes its value. The named test of [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) covers pictures carried inside their box. What a clip with a corner pin draws outside its box near the horizon, where K stretches lengths without bound, is drawn by the definition of [What a renderer holds](#what-a-renderer-holds), and lies outside that test.

**Note.** A renderer draws a picture with a corner pin by warping a raster of its nested list ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)). The area factor of a homography over a convex region is largest at a corner, since w is linear there, but the factor along one direction can be largest inside it, so a renderer bounds the raster's scale over the whole of what it carries, not at the corners of `dest` only. Near a horizon that scale has no bound, and the pixel limit holds it: the Quartz renderer draws a nested list's raster with at most twice the pixels of the area it is shown in, and at a lower scale where it would need more. Core Image's `CIPerspectiveTransform` takes the four corners a picture goes to, which is the form `cornerPin` gives.

**Note.** Measured on an Apple M1 Pro, with half float premultiplied pictures, on the set [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) names, against the mean of [What a renderer holds](#what-a-renderer-holds) taken over at least 16 × 16 samples per output pixel, more where neighbouring samples would land over a quarter of a source pixel apart. Core Image's `CIPerspectiveTransform` takes one bilinear sample at each output pixel's centre: it is 0.246 from the mean at worst on the checkerboard and the mild quadrilateral, 0.444 on the steep one, and on the grid carried onto the steep quadrilateral 0.769 at worst and 0.069 on average. Rastering the picture smaller first, or filtering it with Lanczos first, blurs the near part and still aliases the far part, 0.146 to 0.187 on average on that grid, since one scale cannot serve a map whose area factor changes a hundredfold. A Metal kernel that carries 4 × 4 subsamples of each output pixel back through W⁻¹, each a trilinear read from a 2 × 2 box pyramid of the raster at the level where neighbouring subsamples are at most one level pixel apart, was 0.075 at worst and 0.003 on average on that grid and 0.025 and 0.0002 on checkerboards, in 10 to 14 ms a frame, with the same bytes over 20 renders and three processes. Its levels are transparent past the source's edge, so under minification they mix that transparency into what lies just inside the edge and darken it, which the values rule of [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) does not allow. The Quartz renderer's kernel reads each level with its edge pixels repeated outward and weights each subsample by its coverage of the source, the ramp of the bilinear reconstruction at each edge averaged over the part of the pixel the subsample stands for, so a constant source keeps its value wherever those parts lie inside it. On the set as [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) writes it, in y-down pixels, that kernel was 0.077 at worst and 0.003 on average on the grid under the steep corner pin and 0.025 and 0.0002 on checkerboards, in 17 to 23 ms a frame, and it keeps the values [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) checks. An n × n kernel without a pyramid, n adapted to the local shrink, was 0.025 and 0.002 on the grid, at up to 48 ms a frame. Core Image's coordinates have y up, so a renderer that gives it corner pins in y-down pixels takes y' = height - y.
