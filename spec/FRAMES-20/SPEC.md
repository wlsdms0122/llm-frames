# FRAMES-20: Evaluation, display lists and the mix plan

Status: accepted

## Summary

`evaluate(resolved, t)`: the display list at a time, its four item kinds (`picture`, `path`, `glyphs`, `adjust`) with the fields every item shares (transform, opacity, blend, mask, track matte, reveal), a picture carried onto a quadrilateral by its corner pin, and the mix plan of a sequence's sound as a tree of buses.

## Motivation

One frame of an edit is many rules at once: keyframes and their eases, a parent's transform, a tracked motion, an animation, a transition moving two pictures, a feathered mask, a track matte, a retimed frame. If a renderer had to know those rules, each new renderer would implement the editing model again, and two renderers would disagree on where a title is in the middle of a dissolve. Evaluation does everything above pixels once and hands a role only what to draw, as a compositor's render graph hands a GPU only draw calls. The items are the primitives every 2D renderer already has (an image drawn into a rectangle, a path filled and stroked, glyphs, a filter over a backdrop), so Core Graphics, Skia or an SVG renderer can each draw them.

## Rules

`evaluate(resolved, t)` gives the display list at sequence time t: the frame size, its background, its `compositing`, and drawing items bottom first, every value already sampled at t. Where a clip with motion blur owns a value the frame at t reads, the outermost list holds N sample lists of items in place of its items, each a whole list of the frame at one instant of the frame's exposure, and the frame is their mean ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). Only the outermost list of a frame holds samples, and a nested list never does. Every list, nested ones included, carries the `compositing` its items are composited in: the sequence's, or in the list of a compound clip or a template instance that one's own ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). The outermost list also carries the sequence's `colorSpace`, which the encoder writes its frames in, and its `peak`, the `format.peak` an `rec2100-pq` output is clipped to or `null` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)). A nested list carries none, since a picture's source carries what the decoder needs: its clip's `colorSpace` and the peak an HDR picture of it is tone mapped to ([JSON form](#json-form)). Frame k of a sequence is `evaluate(resolved, k / fps)`, and a PNG is the display list at the time it is rendered at ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)).

### Fields every item has

| Field | Value | Meaning | Moves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `transform` | `[a, b, c, d, tx, ty]` | the affine map from the item's own coordinates to output pixels: a point (x, y) goes to (a x + c y + tx, b x + d y + ty), as Core Graphics and SVG write a matrix | yes |
| `opacity` | 0 to 1 | multiplies the item's alpha | yes |
| `blend` | a mode name | how the item composites with what is below it ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)) | no |
| `mask` | a list of masks, or `null` | each `{geometry, transform, feather, invert, mode}` (below) | each mask's `geometry`, `transform` and `feather` |
| `trackMatte` | `{source, mode, invert}` or `null` | `source` a nested display list, `mode` `alpha` or `luma` (below) | no |
| `reveal` | `[x, y, w, h]` or `null` | the part of the item that shows, a rectangle in its own coordinates before its transform | its rectangle's numbers |

- An item is drawn in this order: its content in its own coordinates, with its alpha cut to its `reveal` rectangle (a hard straight edge) and, for a `picture`, multiplied by its `matte`; then, for a `picture` with a `cornerPin`, carried by the homography H(`dest` → `cornerPin`) that takes the corners of `dest` to the corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-homography)); then carried by `transform` to output pixels; then its alpha multiplied by its mask, its track matte and its `opacity`; then composited onto what is below by its `blend`, with the formulas of W3C Compositing and Blending Level 1 (source-over with the mode's blend function), each in the form [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing) gives under the list's `compositing`. `reveal` turns with the item, so a wipe on a rotated title follows the title.
- An item's `reveal` is `null` where no animation or transition sets a reveal on it, and also where its wipe is complete in a list without samples: the receiver's part the wipe shows otherwise ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)). In the sample lists of a frame an item a reveal is set on, by an animation of its clip with a span that writes `reveal`, whichever span plays at the frame's instant, or by a transition of its storyline picture whose span writes `reveal` and that the picture takes part in at that instant, writes its rectangle in each, the receiver's whole box where the wipe is complete, and an item no reveal is set on writes `null` in each, since whether it is there is decided at the frame's instant ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). A reveal whose rectangle holds everything the item draws cuts nothing, so the renderer draws it as it draws `null`.
- An item's transform may scale its axes differently, mirror and skew ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)). It is affine for every item, and a picture's `cornerPin` is the one projective map in a display list. Its **length factor** k is √|ad - bc|, the geometric mean of the scales it applies.
- **The widths of the lines an item draws are in output pixels, drawn the same in every direction on its outline as the transform carries it**, as SVG draws a stroke under `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`: a stroke's `width`, a picture's `border` width, a run's stroke `width`, a `shadow`'s `radius`, and a mask's `feather`. The other lengths are in the item's own coordinates and are carried by the transform with its geometry ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)): the lengths of the outline (a corner `radius`, a highlight's or background's `radius`, an outset already applied to the geometry, a stroke's `dash` and `dashOffset`), and, as an effect applied before a layer's transform is, a `shadow`'s `offset` and a `picture`'s filter lengths.
- Evaluation writes lengths so that a clip's lengths come out as those of [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) multiplied by the clip's scale factor and its animation's scale ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), and not by its parent's ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)): a length drawn in output pixels is written at that size, and a carried length at that size over k. A clip with a corner pin draws its lengths in its box, its animation's scale applied there, and its group carries them with its picture ([Pictures](#pictures)), so its corner pin and its parent's map reach them as they reach the picture.
- Inside a nested display list, output pixels are the pixels of that list's own frame, at whatever size the list is drawn.
- Corners given by `radius` are circular arcs of that radius in the item's own coordinates, each tangent to the two sides it joins, as Canvas `arcTo` rounds a corner, and the transform carries them with the outline, so a transform that scales its axes differently draws them as elliptical arcs. A radius whose arcs would reach past the middle of a side is cut to the largest that does not: for a rectangle, half its shorter side, as CSS and Core Graphics take it.
- A `shadow` is `{radius, offset, color}`: the item's alpha after its reveal, matte, mask and track matte, blurred by a Gaussian whose standard deviation is `radius`, moved by `offset` in the item's own coordinates, filled with `color` and drawn below the item. The item and its shadow take `opacity` and `blend` together.

### Items

| Item | Fields | Draws | Moves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `picture` | `source` (a frame of a media file or image sequence, by index, or a page of a PDF, with the colour space its clip or transition cover reads it in and the peak an HDR picture of it is tone mapped to, or a nested display list), `rect` (the source region), `dest` (the rectangle it fills, in its own coordinates), `filter` (entries `{ops, mix}`, each its ops in order mixed with its input by `mix` as [FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md) says, written for `dest` and applied at the scale the picture is shown at ([Pictures](#pictures))), `matte` (`{source, frame, colorSpace, progress, softness, invert}` or `null`), `radius`, `border` `{width, color}` or `null`, `shadow` or `null`, `cornerPin` (`null`, or a strictly convex quadrilateral in its own coordinates, the points the top left, top right, bottom right and bottom left corners of `dest` go to, or, in a sample list only, four equal points at `[0, 0]`, which draw nothing) | a region of a picture scaled into a rectangle, filtered, cut by its transition matte, then carried onto its corner pin | `rect`, `dest`, `filter`, the `matte`'s `progress`, `radius`, the `border`'s `width` and `color`, `shadow`, `cornerPin` |
| `path` | `geometry`, `fill` (a paint or `null`), `stroke` (`{width, paint, join, cap, start, end, dash, dashOffset}` or `null`), `trim` `[a, b]`, `shadow` or `null` | a rectangle, an ellipse or a path of cubic Bézier segments, filled and stroked | `geometry`, `fill`, the `stroke`'s `width`, `paint`, the lengths of its `dash` and its `dashOffset`, `trim`, `shadow` |
| `glyphs` | `writingMode` (its title's, [FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#writing-mode)), `runs` (each: font file, face, size, `sideways`, whether its glyphs are drawn turned 90 degrees clockwise, `horizontalScale`, the scale they are drawn at along their own horizontal axis, colour, `stroke` `{width, color}` or `null`, `highlight` `{rects, radius, color, bands}` or `null`, `underline` `{rects, color, bands}` or `null`, glyph ids and positions), `layer` (`null`, or one of `highlights`, `underlines`, `strokes` and `fills` for a unit's item), `unit` (`null`, or the index of its unit among its title's units in reading order for a unit's item), `unitBox` (`null`, or the unit's box `[x, y, w, h]` in the text box for a unit's item), `extent` (the drawn extent of its glyphs ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) `[x, y, w, h]` in the text box: its unit's for a unit's item, its title's for every other), `background` `{rect, radius, color}` or `null`, `size` | glyphs, with their outlines, highlights, underlines and background | each run's colour, `stroke`, `highlight`, `underline` and glyph positions, `unitBox`, `extent`, `background`, `size` |
| `adjust` | `filter` | the filter applied to everything drawn below it in the same list | `filter` |

- **The Moves columns mark the fields evaluation computes from parameters that take time**, the only fields in which the sample lists of a frame may differ ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). Every other field is the same in each sample, and a nested list in a `source` is held to the same rule item by item, its own `size`, `background` and `compositing` the same in each sample. A `source` of media and a `matte`'s `source` and `frame` are content: the pinned media, a page's `page` included, the frame index or the `null` of a page, the `colorSpace` and the `targetPeak` are the same in each sample. A `filter` moves in its entries' `mix` and its ops' numeric parameters, the numbers and the `bloom` `color` that a preset's `param` may stand for ([FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md)), and in nothing that names content or form: an op's name, a `lut` op's `file`, `sha256` and `space`, a `displace` op's `file` and `sha256`, and a `grain` op's `seed` ([What evaluation has done](#what-evaluation-has-done)) are the same in each sample. A field added to an item takes the mark in its row or does not.

### Pictures

- Evaluation picks a media source's frame by [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md) from the pinned timestamps at the media time the clip's time map gives, its `timeRemap` included ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md)), and the renderer draws that frame. Part of `rect` that lies outside the source is transparent.
- A picture's `border` is `null` where the picture has no border, and also where its width is 0 in a list without samples. In the sample lists of a frame a border the picture has is written in each, its `width` 0 included, since whether it is there is decided at the frame's instant ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). It lies inside `dest`, its outer edge following the rounded corners.
- A nested display list stands for a compound clip or a template instance at its mapped time, for a storyline picture inside its transition motion ([below](#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)), or for a title animated by unit as its group ([Glyphs and adjustments](#glyphs-and-adjustments)). A nested list has no background, so what its items leave uncovered is transparent, except the list of a compound clip or a template instance, whose background is its own `format.background` ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)). It is drawn at the size it is shown at in the output, held to the pixel limit (below), as its items would be drawn in place, so a list shown larger is as sharp as its items drawn larger and every length inside it (a shadow, a filter length, a feather) keeps its size against the list's own frame. The picture's `rect` and `dest` apply to it as to any source of the list's size, and its `filter`, `radius`, `border` and `shadow` as to every picture (below).
- A source that is a page of a PDF ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)) is drawn at the size it is shown at in the output, as a nested display list is, so a page shown larger is drawn at a larger scale. With [a, b, c, d, tx, ty] the map from the page's media pixels to the pixels of the outermost output (the picture's `rect` to its `dest`, then its `transform`, then for each nested list that holds it the map that carries that list into the one that holds it: its picture's `rect` to `dest` and `transform`, and the identity for a track matte's list), its scale s is the larger of √(a² + b²) and √(c² + d²), the output pixels per media pixel along the axis the map stretches most. The renderer draws it from one picture the decoder gives of the page, a region of the page's grid at a scale of at least s, or where the decoder does not hold that region at that scale or the renderer cannot state its pixel positions exactly at it, at the largest scale at which both hold, resampled from there ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)). **The region and the scale are a function of the frame alone**: of its display list and of how far whatever reads the picture reaches, never of what the renderer drew before, so a frame asks for the same picture whenever and in whatever order it is drawn. In a frame given as sample lists ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)) the page is that of t in every sample and the rule holds for each sample list: samples whose region and scale agree read one picture, and where a blurred zoom makes the scale differ between samples, each sample asks for its own. So it is as sharp as the scale it is drawn at allows, and the rest of the map, a rotation, an offset by a fraction of a pixel and unequal scales of the two axes, resamples that picture, as it does any raster source. Which region, and how far above s the scale lies, is the renderer's choice under that bound, so a renderer may round s up by a rule of its own as long as the rule reads only the frame, and the region covers everything of the page that is read: the part of `rect` that reaches the output, and as far past it as whatever reads the picture reaches, its own filter and shadow and those of every picture and list that holds it. The region may reach past `rect`, but what of it lies outside `rect`, the conformed crop, is discarded before the filter, which reads past that edge as [FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md) gives, so no filter reads the page outside its crop. So a page inside a compound clip shown at twice its size is drawn at least at twice the scale, and a crop on a page asks only for what is cropped. The picture's `rect` and `dest` apply to it as to an image of the page's size in media pixels, and its `filter`, `radius`, `border` and `shadow` as to every picture (below). A media cover of a page is such a picture, and a page as a transition matte is read the same way as an analysis picture, at the scale the whole map shows it at, its stretch over `dest` and then the maps of the lists that hold it, by the rules above for a picture of a page ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)).
- **A picture is filtered at the scale it is shown at.** Its `filter`, `radius`, `border` and `shadow` are written for `dest`, and the renderer applies them at the resolution the picture is shown at, whatever its source, k pixels for each unit of `dest` with k the larger axis stretch of the map from `dest` to the outermost output (as s is for a page), held to the pixel limit (below), and multiplies every length by that factor from the unit it is written in, a carried length (a filter op's `radius` and `size`, so a blur radius, a pixelate block and a grain cell, a `displace` op's `amount` and `offset`, a corner `radius`, a `shadow`'s `offset`) by k, and a width in output pixels (a `border` width, a `shadow`'s `radius`) by the resolution's pixels per output pixel of the list that holds the picture. The grids of `pixelate` and `grain` lie from the top left of `dest` at that resolution too. So a picture is drawn as sharp as its source whatever its parents and animation scale it by: a picture of pixels is resampled once, onto that resolution, as nested lists and pages are drawn at the size they are shown at, and a page shown at ten times its size with a blur or a pixelation is blurred or pixelated as at `dest` size, ten times larger, its edges, under a grade alike, drawn at the larger size and not enlarged. A nonlinear op, a `lut` for one, sees the picture at its shown scale, so its effect on detail finer than a shown pixel follows the scale: a checker of single pixels shown at half its size reaches it as its mean grey, and shown at its size as black and white.
- **A renderer holds every bitmap it draws at the scale a picture is shown at to its pixel limit**, a constant of the renderer: a nested list's raster and the resolution a picture is filtered at. Such a bitmap covers the part of the picture that lands in the list that holds it, grown by how far what reads it reaches, and holds at most the pixel limit times the pixels of the area it lands in, grown the same way. Where the shown scale needs more, as a turn, a skew or unequal scales of the two axes can, it is drawn at the largest scale the limit allows, so a picture is drawn at its shown scale wherever its axes are carried upright at one scale. The picture a decoder gives of a page is asked for by the rule of a page above, not by this limit.
- **Only a clip's group carries a `cornerPin`** (below), and a picture of a media frame never does. On it `rect` is the clip's box in the nested list's coordinates, the part of the list fitted to `dest`, and `dest` is that box in the item's coordinates, whose corners go to the corner pin; neither is a crop. A picture with a `cornerPin` is carried by W = H(`dest` → `cornerPin`), the homography that takes the corners of `dest` to the corner pin, followed by its `transform`. Its source is a nested list, and the whole list is shown, its part outside `rect` included: each point is taken to the item's coordinates by the map of `rect` to `dest` and then carried by W where W's w has the sign it has inside `dest`, and nothing at or beyond the line where w is 0, the horizon, is drawn ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-stops-and-what-draws-nothing)). The list is drawn at the largest factor by which that map stretches a length, in any direction, at any point it carries inside the frame, where that factor is above 1, so no part of the list is shown larger than it was drawn, and at its own size where it is not, so a picture of pixels the list holds is resampled once, in the warp. Near the horizon that factor grows without bound, and a renderer holds the list's raster to its pixel limit (above): a list that would need more is drawn at its own size when it holds a picture of pixels, for the same reason, and at the largest scale the limit allows when it does not, and a list whose own size needs more is drawn at a lower scale. A page of a PDF is not a picture of pixels: it is drawn at the size it is shown at, as a nested list is (above), so a list that holds a page and no picture of pixels is drawn at the largest scale the limit allows, and the page is asked for at the scale the list is drawn at.
- **A clip with a corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)) is drawn as a group**: one `picture` item whose source is a nested display list holding what the clip draws flat in its box, on its plane ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#the-key)). With B the clip's box `[0, 0, w, h]`, origin at its top left, D(t) is the bounds of what the clip draws, in B's coordinates at t: the box an animation's reveal wipes ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), for a storyline clip its box with its border and shadow as for a media clip, where a shadow reaches 3 × its radius past its picture moved by its offset, carried by the transform part of its animation. The nested list is D's size, and the clip's items are drawn in it as they are drawn without a corner pin, with B's top left corner at (-x, -y) for D = `[x, y, w', h']` and the transform part of its animation, about B's centre, as their transform: its picture, path or glyphs item with its reveal, filter, radius, border and shadow, its masks in its own space ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)), and a title's group of units with their own animations, at opacity 1, `normal` blend and no track matte. The group's `rect` is B in the list's coordinates, `[-x, -y, w, h]`, its `dest` is B, and its `cornerPin` is the corner pin Q(t) as [FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#a-tracked-corner-pin) gives it, so the map that carries the list is the corner pin's map K(t) = H(B → Q(t)), what the list holds outside B included. Its transform is the clip's composed with its parent's ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)): its tracked motion, then P(t). It carries the clip's opacity, its animation's included, its blend and its track matte, except a storyline clip's group, whose opacity and blend its outer item carries ([Storyline pictures and transitions](#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)), and no filter, matte, radius, border, shadow, mask or reveal. So the clip's animation, lengths, strokes and shadows are drawn on its plane and foreshorten with its picture. A clip whose corner pin draws nothing at t ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-stops-and-what-draws-nothing)) draws no item in a list without samples. In the sample lists of a frame a clip with a corner pin that draws at the frame's instant gives its group in each, since whether an item is there is decided at that instant and never by a value ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)), and in a sample where its corner pin draws nothing, because its box has no area, its corner pin lies at or beyond the horizon of H, or the corner pin it carries is not strictly convex, the group's `cornerPin` is four equal points at `[0, 0]`, which no homography from `dest` reaches, so the group draws nothing there.
- A `matte` is a transition matte ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), stretched over `dest`. The picture shows where the matte's luminance in `frame` (picked from its media time) is below `progress`, with an edge `softness` wide, inverted when `invert` is true. With L the matte's luma ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)) clamped to 0 to 1 (1 - L when inverted), p the `progress` and s the `softness`, the picture's alpha is multiplied by clamp((p × (1 + s) - L) / s, 0, 1), and when s is 0 by its limit (1 where L < p, p where L = p, 0 where L > p), so progress 0 shows none of the picture and progress 1 all of it. `matte` and `trackMatte` are different things on purpose: the first is a wipe between two storyline pictures, the second a clip that cuts another.

### Paths

`geometry` is one of:

| Form | Outline |
| --- | --- |
| `{"rect": {"rect": [x, y, w, h], "radius": r}}` | a rectangle with circular corners of radius `r` (0 for square corners) |
| `{"ellipse": {"rect": [x, y, w, h]}}` | the ellipse inscribed in the rectangle |
| `{"path": {"vertices": [[x, y], ...], "inTangents": [[dx, dy], ...], "outTangents": [[dx, dy], ...], "closed": false}}` | cubic Bézier segments through the vertices in order, at least two, and from the last back to the first when `closed`: the segment from vertex i to the next has its control points at `vertices[i] + outTangents[i]` and at the next vertex plus its `inTangents` entry ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#shapes)), as After Effects' Shape object writes `vertices`, `inTangents` and `outTangents`. The three lists have one entry for every vertex, `[0, 0]` for a tangent of 0, so a segment whose two tangents are 0 is straight |

- **Direction.** Every outline runs clockwise in the item's own coordinates, y down, which is clockwise on the output unless the transform mirrors, from a fixed start: a rect from the top left corner (the start of its top edge, just after the top left corner's arc), an ellipse from its top, a path from its first vertex.
- **Direction at a point.** The outline's direction at a point is the direction it travels there, as SVG takes a path's direction: inside a segment its tangent's, and at a segment's end the limit of it, which is the direction from the segment's start to the first of its later control points that differs from it, and into its end from the last of its earlier control points that differs from it. So leaving a vertex whose `outTangent` is 0 the outline heads for the next vertex's control point, or for the next vertex itself where that tangent is 0 too. A segment whose four control points are one point has no length and no direction, and the outline passes over it. A **corner** is a point where the direction arriving and the direction leaving differ: a path's vertex where they differ, a cusp inside a segment, where its direction reverses, and the corner of a rect without `radius`. A vertex where they agree is smooth and has none.
- **Outset.** Evaluation writes a rect's or an ellipse's geometry grown on every side by its `outset` in the item's own coordinates, written as a carried length, so a per-axis scale stretches it with the outline.
- **Fill.** `fill` paints the inside of the geometry by the nonzero winding rule, an open path closed by a straight segment from its last vertex to its first, as SVG fills it. `trim` does not cut the fill.
- **Stroke.** `stroke` is `null` where the shape has no stroke, and also where its width is 0 in a list without samples. In the sample lists of a frame a stroke the shape has is written in each, its `width` 0 included, since whether it is there is decided at the frame's instant ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). The stroke is `width` wide, centred on the outline. `join` (`miter`, `round` or `bevel`) shapes each corner of the outline ([Direction at a point](#paths)), and a miter longer than 4 × `width` is drawn as a bevel, the SVG default miter limit, so a cusp, a turn of half a circle, takes a bevel under `miter`. A smooth vertex takes no join, as SVG draws none between segments whose directions agree. `cap` (`butt`, `round` or `square`) shapes the ends of an open outline and of a trimmed piece. A whole closed outline has no ends.
- **Trim.** `trim` is `[a, b]`, each from 0 to 1. With L the length of the outline in the item's own coordinates, its arc length along every segment's curve, the stroke is drawn along it from min(a, b) × L to max(a, b) × L, measured from its start in its direction, as After Effects trims a path in its layer's space, so a scale does not move where a trim falls. Its width, caps, joins and arrowheads are then drawn in output pixels, and its dashes are laid along it as lengths carried with the outline, as every dash length is ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). `[0, 1]` draws all of it, and `a = b` draws no stroke. A trimmed piece of a closed outline is open, with its caps at both ends.
- **Arrowheads.** `start` and `end` are `none` or `arrow`. An `arrow` is drawn at the start or the end of the stroke as drawn (after `trim`): a filled isosceles triangle in the stroke's paint, its tip at that end point and the middle of its base at the point of the stroke ℓ = 4 × `width` back along it from the tip, measured along the outline as it is drawn on the output, and 4 × `width` wide at its base. Its axis runs from the middle of its base to its tip, so on a straight end it lies along the outline and is ℓ long, and on a curved one it is the chord of the stroke's last ℓ, its tip and its base both on the curve. Where the two points are one, the axis lies along the outline's direction at the tip. The line stops at the middle of the base, with its cap there, so nothing of the line shows past the tip. Where the stroke as drawn is shorter than its heads, each head's ℓ and width are scaled down together, keeping its shape, so that their ℓ add up to the stroke's length. A whole closed outline has no ends and draws no arrowhead.
- **Dashes.** `dash` is `null` for a whole stroke, or a list of an even number of lengths, the dashes and gaps in turn (evaluation writes an odd list of [FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md) twice over). It is also `null` where its lengths are all 0 in a list without samples. In the sample lists of a frame a dash the stroke has is written in each, its lengths all 0 included, since whether it is there is decided at the frame's instant ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)), and a dash whose lengths are all 0 draws the stroke whole. The pattern is laid along the stroke as drawn, from its start after `trim`, moved back along it by `dashOffset` and repeated to its end, as SVG `stroke-dasharray` and `stroke-dashoffset` lay it. Each dash takes `cap` at its ends and `join` at its corners. Arrowheads are not dashed.
- **Paint.** A `fill` and a stroke's `paint` are a paint, written from a shape's `fill` and from its stroke's `color` or `gradient`: a colour, or a gradient `{"linear": {"from", "to", "stops"}}` or `{"radial": {"center", "radius", "stops"}}` whose points and radius evaluation has written in the item's own coordinates from the shape's box at t, and whose `stops` are `[offset, colour]` with their offsets already clamped and in order. The colour at each point is the one [FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md) gives, and the gradient is carried by the transform with the geometry. A stroke's paint colours its arrowheads too.
- The stroke and its arrowheads are one shape: where they overlap, nothing is drawn twice. The fill is drawn first and the stroke over it.

{ "item": "path", "origin": { "place": "clips[2]", "id": "pointer", "kind": "shape" },
  "transform": [1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0], "opacity": 1, "blend": "normal", "mask": null, "trackMatte": null, "reveal": null,
  "geometry": { "path": { "vertices": [[420, 300], [760, 520]], "inTangents": [[0, 0], [-120, -60]], "outTangents": [[200, 0], [0, 0]],
                         "closed": false } },
  "fill": null, "stroke": { "width": 8, "paint": "color(srgb 1 1 1 / 1)", "join": "round", "cap": "round", "start": "none", "end": "arrow",
              "dash": null, "dashOffset": 0 },
  "trim": [0, 0.6], "shadow": null }

### Glyphs and adjustments

- A `glyphs` item draws its background, then every run's highlight rectangles in run order, each with corners of its `radius`, then every run's underline rectangles, then the outline of every glyph of every run with a stroke, each run's glyphs as one stroke in its width and colour, centred on the outline with round joins and caps, then every glyph's fill, so no stroke covers a fill. Glyph positions are in the item's own coordinates, its title's text box at that instant, of its `size` with its origin at the top left, not the title's box the background's padding grows it to ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)). So its glyphs, its unit boxes, its `extent` and its background's `rect` lie in the text box, the background reaching past its edges by its padding, and a padding that takes keyframes moves no glyph.
- Evaluation writes a title's runs at t from the resolved runs ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)): each glyph at its pen position with its placement offset added, where its origin is drawn, narrowed about it along its own horizontal axis by its run's `horizontalScale` and turned 90 degrees clockwise about it in a `sideways` run, its stroke drawn at its width on the outline so narrowed, each run's glyphs and pieces raised by its `baselineShift` at t, each highlight rectangle its piece grown by its `padding` at t with corners of its `radius` at t, and each underline rectangle a band across its piece `width` thick at t with its top edge `offset` below its run's alphabetic baseline at t, on the axes of the title's writing mode ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#writing-mode)). It moves the glyphs, the pieces, the units' boxes and their parts along their lines by the tracking at t and with their lines by the line height at t, places each line again by `align`, and writes the text box `size` and the title's drawn extent as `extent` at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)). Its `background` takes as `rect` the text box at t grown by the background's `padding` at t, and the background's `radius` and `color` at t.
- **What a `glyphs` item draws is its `extent` grown by half its widest stroke and joined with its background's `rect` and the highlight and underline rectangles it draws**, each cut to its band where it has bands, and for a title's background drawn alone, which holds no runs, its background's `rect` only, or nothing when the title has none. It is what `bounds` and `locate` take ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#machine-output)) and what the item's `reveal` wipes ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), but for a title's background drawn alone, whose `reveal` is its title's (below), so a raised run, a space with ink and a mark set over its base are inside it. A stroke is drawn in output pixels on outlines the item's transform carries, so half its stroke reaches, in the text box, as far as the transform's inverse carries a disc of that radius along each axis, and nothing where the transform flattens the plane.
- **A title animated by unit ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)) is drawn as a group**, as After Effects composites a precomposition: one `picture` item whose source is a nested display list the size of the list that holds it, drawn in that list's output pixels, its `rect` and `dest` that whole list and its transform the identity, which carries the title's opacity, blend, mask and track matte as the one `glyphs` item of a title without `by` carries them, and no filter, radius, border or shadow. Inside it the title draws one `glyphs` item for what is not a unit, its background, with no runs, then four passes over the units, each in reading order: every unit's item of `layer` `highlights`, then of `underlines`, then of `strokes`, then of `fills`. A unit's item holds that unit's glyphs in their runs with the parts of their highlights and underlines it carries ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), its box as `unitBox`, its drawn extent ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)) at that instant as `extent`, and no background. In a unit's item a run's `highlight` and `underline` hold the whole pieces the unit has parts of, and `bands`, one `[x0, x1]` for each of those pieces in the text box, a span in y in a vertical `writingMode`: the item draws each piece as it is drawn whole, its padding and corners included, and cut to its band, the part the unit carries. A band's end inside its piece, where two units' parts meet, cuts whole pixels, each going to one of the two parts, so no seam shows between them. Outside a unit's item `bands` is `null`. The items inside the group are all in the text box, of its `size`, with `normal` blend and no mask or track matte. The background's transform and `reveal` are the title's and its opacity 1. A unit's transform is the title's composed with the unit's motion about the centre of its box, its opacity the unit's, and its `reveal` the part of what the unit draws that the unit's reveal shows, cut to the title's `reveal`, the loop's, when both are given, and either one alone otherwise ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)). Each is written as [Fields every item has](#fields-every-item-has) says: `null` at reveal 1 in a list without samples, and in a sample list where the unit's entrance or exit, or the title's loop, sets it, the whole of what the unit or the title draws at reveal 1. An item of a `layer` draws only that step of the order above for its glyphs, so the passes keep every highlight under every underline, stroke and fill of the title. A unit's opacity applies to each of its items on its own, so while a unit enters or leaves at an opacity below 1 its highlight and stroke show through its own fill. Once every unit has entered and before any leaves, the group holds the picture the title draws without `by`, and the title's opacity, blend, mask and track matte apply to it once, so the title draws as it does without `by` at any of them. A title not animated by unit, and one animated by unit that has no unit ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#units)), draws one item with `layer` `null`, every step.
- **Each item a unit draws names its unit as `unit`**, by the unit's index among its title's units in reading order, since which characters an item draws is a fact of what it holds and not of the clip that drew it, and reports tell a unit's item by it ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#text-placement)). Each pass of a title animated by unit draws one item for each unit in reading order, so the order of its `fills` items follows from their units and agrees with them: its k-th `fills` item is the one whose `unit` is k.
- An `adjust` item covers the whole frame of its list: the filter is applied to everything drawn below it in that list, and the filtered picture is composited onto the unfiltered one with the item's `blend`, its alpha multiplied by the item's reveal, mask, track matte and `opacity`. Its `transform` carries its `reveal` only.

### Masks

A mask is `{geometry, transform, feather, invert, mode}`: `geometry` in one of the path forms, `transform` the affine map from its coordinates to output pixels, `feather` a length in output pixels, `invert` a boolean and `mode` `add`, `subtract` or `intersect`.

- A mask's value m at a point of the output is the coverage of its geometry carried by its transform (1 inside, 0 outside, a path always closed, by the nonzero rule), blurred by a Gaussian whose standard deviation is `feather` (none at 0), and 1 - m when `invert` is true.
- The masks of an item combine in list order, as After Effects combines a layer's masks. The result starts at 0 when the first mask's mode is `add` and at 1 when it is `subtract` or `intersect`, and each mask then changes it: `add` gives r + m - r × m, `subtract` gives r × (1 - m), `intersect` gives r × m. The item's alpha is multiplied by the result.
- A mask's transform carries it to the output pixels of the list that holds its item: through the clip's coordinate space, its own transform, its parent's, and the crop of a clip that follows its own media ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), so a mask stays on what it cuts. A storyline picture's transition motion is carried by its outer item ([Storyline pictures and transitions](#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)), not by its mask.

### Track mattes

- A `trackMatte`'s `source` is a nested display list the size of the list that holds the item, drawn in that list's output pixels: the items the matte clip draws at t ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)). A clip used as a matte draws no item of its own. Outside the matte clip's visible window its source holds no item.
- At each point of the output the matte's value v is the source's alpha for `alpha`, and for `luma` the source's luma ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)) clamped to 0 to 1, multiplied by its alpha, so a transparent part of a luma matte counts as black. With `invert` it is 1 - v. The item's alpha is multiplied by v.

### Storyline pictures and transitions

- A storyline clip's picture is two `picture` items, one inside the other, so its own transform and the transition motion each act in their own coordinates, in the order [FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md) gives. The inner item of a storyline clip with a corner pin is its group ([Pictures](#pictures)), at opacity 1 with `blend` `normal`, and its outer item carries the clip's opacity, multiplied by the transition motion's, and the clip's blend. Any other inner item is its media: its conformed crop as `rect`, its box ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)) as `dest`, its filter, `radius`, `border` and `shadow`, and its own transform, opacity, mask and track matte, with `blend` `normal`. It is the one item of a nested display list the size of the frame. The outer item is a `picture` whose `source` is that list and whose `dest` is the frame, carrying the clip's `blend` and, during a transition, the transition's motion for its side ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)): the motion's scale, rotation and `x`, `y` (multiples of the frame) as its transform about the centre of the frame, the motion's opacity as its opacity, the motion's reveal as its reveal rectangle, and on the incoming picture the transition's `matte`. Out of a transition the outer item's transform is the identity and its opacity 1. **A storyline picture's transition motion is carried by its outer item and by nothing inside it.** The two storyline pictures stack as `over` says. A solid is drawn the same way, its inner item a `path` item, a rect the size of the frame filled with its colour, so a matte transition cuts a solid as it cuts media.
- A transition cover is an item above both storyline pictures and below the clips on positive lanes: a colour cover a `path` item, a rect the size of the frame filled with the cover's colour, and a media cover a `picture` item whose `dest` is the frame, each with the cover's opacity at t.
- Items come bottom first in the stacking order of [FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md). The background is the list's own `background`, not an item.
- An item's transform is its clip's own (position, anchor, scale, rotation, the tracked motion of a clip that follows its parent's media, and its animation's transform part; a clip that follows its own media moves its crop and mask instead, [FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)) composed with its parent's as [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md) gives it. A parent's transition motion moves its children, which are items outside its nested list; its opacity, reveal, mask and matte do not.

### What evaluation has done

Evaluation has done everything above pixels: keyframes and eases (springs included), clip areas at t ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), animation spans and how they combine, transition motion, cover and matte progress, reveal, parent transforms, masks, track mattes, tracked motion and tracked corner pins, corner pins as quadrilaterals, time remapping, and the visible windows. A role is left with the items as written.

- Evaluation writes `seed` into every `grain` op: the number of the output frame being drawn, k for frame k of the outermost sequence (evaluated at k / fps), and the frame whose interval holds t for `still`, the `evaluate` command and a PNG. Nested lists take the outer frame's seed, so grain belongs to the output frame and two pictures in a transition share it.
- Evaluation has also exposed every frame in which a clip with `motionBlur` owns a value the frame reads, into the sample lists of [FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md#the-samples), each read as [FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md#what-a-sample-moves-and-what-it-holds) says.

### JSON form

- In the JSON form ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) an item names what it draws under `item` (`picture`, `path`, `glyphs` or `adjust`), and carries `origin`, the clip or op that drew it as `{place, id, kind}` as the `evaluate` command reports it ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)), or `null` for a transition cover. Every item carries every field of its kind and the common fields, `null` where it has none.
- A `picture`'s `source` is `{media, frame, colorSpace, targetPeak}`, the pinned media, the frame's index, the `colorSpace` its clip or transition cover writes or `null` where the file's tags apply, and `targetPeak`, BT.2390's target peak L_max in cd/m² ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#tone-mapping)): the outermost sequence's peak, as the Peak column of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces) gives it, its `format.peak` for `rec2100-pq`, where its clip's `toneMap` is `true`, as a transition cover's always is, and `null` where it is `false`. The decoder tone maps an HDR picture of it where the file's source peak L_W is above that number, and takes it as display light where L_W is at or below it or `toneMap` is `null` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)). Or it is `{list}`, a nested list without the version. Its `rect` is `null` where it shows the whole source. Its `cornerPin` is `null` or four points `[[x, y], [x, y], [x, y], [x, y]]`, and every picture writes it. A list read back stops on a `cornerPin` that is not strictly convex, except four equal points at `[0, 0]` in a sample list. A transition matte's `source` is the pinned media and its `colorSpace` the one the matte writes, or `null` where the file's tags apply, which the renderer reads as an analysis picture whatever the sequence ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), so it carries no target peak. A track matte's `source` is `{list}`. A run's font is pinned as `{file, sha256}`. Colours are `color(srgb r g b / a)` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). A list's `compositing` follows its `size` and `background`, and the outermost list's `colorSpace` and `peak` come before its `compositing`.
- A source of a page of a PDF is the pinned media with its `page` ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) and `frame` `null`, since the decoder is asked for a page by region and scale and not by a frame index ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)), and so is a transition matte's `source` of a page. The decoder refuses a `page` its file does not hold before it starts ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). Its `targetPeak` is `null`, a transition cover's of a page included, since a page is not HDR ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)).
- An outermost list with samples carries `samples`, a list of its sample lists in order, each a list of items, in place of `items`, and every other key as a list without samples does. A list read back stops on an outermost list that carries both `items` and `samples` or neither, on `samples` with fewer than two lists, on a nested list that carries `samples`, and on sample lists that differ in what they hold: each holds the same items in the same order as the first, equal in every field the Moves columns do not mark ([Items](#items)), since content and membership are those of t in every sample.
- A list read back stops on a `glyphs` item that carries some but not all of `layer`, `unit` and `unitBox`, since a unit's item carries all three and a title's own item none, and when its `fills` items do not name the units 0, 1, 2 and on in order.
- The model keeps one seed per list, its nested lists included, so a list read back takes the seed of the first `grain` written, stops on a `grain` with another, and takes 0 when it has no `grain`. A frame index outside its media's frames stops.

### The mix plan

The mix plan is the sound of a whole sequence as a tree of buses ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)): its length, its segments, each with a media file, its media start and speed, the sequence times it starts and ends, its audio filter, a gain envelope and a pan envelope, its buses, and its filter, the chain the mixer runs over their sum. Each bus holds its own segments, buses and filter, its span and a level envelope.

- A storyline clip's segment runs over its drawn range with its edges moved by its `split` ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), and its gain envelope's crossfade at an edge is moved with that edge. A visual media clip's segment, connected or in `clips`, runs over its visible window with its edges moved by its `split`.
- The gain envelope is a list of points `[t, gain]` joined in straight lines. It already holds volume keyframes and their eases, fades, and transition crossfades on the transition's `audio` curve ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)): with u the progress through the transition, `constant-power` gives the outgoing sound cos(uπ/2) and the incoming sin(uπ/2), and `constant-gain` gives them 1 - u and u. Eased keyframes and the crossfade curve are sampled at the frame rate.
- The pan envelope is a list of points `[t, pan]` joined in straight lines, from -1 (left) to 1 (right), the pan keyframes and their eases sampled at the frame rate. The mixer pans by the pan law of [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md).
- An envelope has a point at both ends of its segment, at every keyframe and every end of a fade or transition, a nested sound's mapped to the outermost sequence's time through the time maps of the compound clips that hold it, and at every frame time k / fps of the outermost sequence inside a span where its value moves: between two keyframes of different values, or over a fade or transition. A value that holds has no points between its bends.
- The audio filter is the segment's entries `{ops, mix}` ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), `[]` when it has none. A number in it, a `mix` included, that holds over the segment is written as a number, and one that moves as an envelope of `[t, value]` points joined in straight lines, with its points where a gain envelope has them, a `frequency` sampled in log space. A `convolve` op is `{op, file, sha256, gain}`: its impulse response pinned as media is, the same over the whole segment, and its `gain` a number or an envelope as any other value. The mixer runs each entry's ops on the segment's sound and mixes the result with the entry's input by its `mix`, before the gain and pan envelopes, takes each moving value from its envelope at the sample, and computes an op's coefficients again at least every 32 samples while one of its values moves. Each entry reads its input as the sound's samples, in floating point of 32 bits or more ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)), and gives its output to the next entry as those samples, so the sound is rounded to them after each entry of a filter or a chain.
- The mixer mixes each segment's channels into the output's as its role decides ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). A loop is one segment per repeat, and a looped compound clip one bus per repeat. A compound clip's sounds are segments of their own, in its bus. A clip with `timeRemap` has no segment.
- The length is N, the samples per channel at 48 kHz the output's frames last. With F the number of output frames ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)) and r their rate as frames presents them, a rate that differs from a whole number n by at most a hundred-thousandth of itself read as n and one that differs as little from n × 1000 / 1001 read as that rate, N = F × 48000 / r rounded to the nearest whole number, halves rounded up. At a whole rate that divides 48000 it is exact, at 30000 / 1001 it is F × 1601.6 rounded, and at 24000 / 1001 it is F × 2002. The mixer mixes exactly the N samples from sequence time 0, the sample k at sequence time k / 48000.
- A span, a segment's or a bus's, from sequence time s to e is half-open: it holds the samples k from round(48000 s) to round(48000 e) - 1, each rounded to the nearest whole number, halves rounded up. Its first sample is round(48000 s): a segment's media plays its sample at `mediaStart` there, and the ops of a segment's filter or a bus's chain start at rest there. A span that ends at e and one that starts at e share no sample and leave none between them. An envelope or a moving value is read at sample k at time k / 48000.
- A segment's pan envelope holds its own pan with the `pan` of every compound clip and template instance that holds it added, cut to -1 and 1, since pan comes before a bus's sum ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)).
- The plan's filter is the sequence's `audioFilter` as entries `{ops, mix}`, `[]` when it has none, each value written as a segment's audio filter writes it, a number that holds over the N samples or an envelope over them.
- Every compound clip and template instance is a bus, its filter `[]` where its chain is empty. Its span is its `start` and `end` in sequence time, as [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses) cuts it. Its filter is its chain, the nested sequence's entries and then the compound clip's own, each value written as a segment's audio filter writes it, a number that holds over the span or an envelope over it, the nested sequence's values mapped through the compound clip's time map as its sounds' envelopes are ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)). Its level envelope holds the compound clip's `volume`, fades and crossfades, with its points as a gain envelope has them.
- The plan's segments and buses are in audio entry order, and so are each bus's.
- The mixer sums a bus's segments, each after its filter, gain and pan, and its buses, each after its own chain and level, over the samples of its span, runs its filter's entries over the stereo sum with their ops at rest at the span's first sample, taking each moving value from its envelope and computing coefficients again as for a segment's audio filter, mixes each entry's result with its input by its `mix`, multiplies the result by its level envelope and adds it to the plan or the bus that holds it. It does the same for the plan over the N samples from sequence time 0, with no level envelope, and hands the result, the mix, to the encoder.
- In the JSON form the mix plan is `{mixPlan, length, segments, buses, filter}`, `mixPlan` its version ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) and `length` N, a whole number. A segment is `{media, mediaStart, speed, start, end, filter, envelope, pan}`, `media` the pinned media, `filter` its entries, each envelope point `[t, gain]` and each pan point `[t, pan]`. A plan of one medium, the mix of the sequence that plays one clip of it, which only `loudness` makes ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#measuring-loudness)), holds the file's path alone in its segment's `media`, as `{file}`, not pinned, and is not read back. A bus is `{start, end, segments, buses, filter, envelope}`, each envelope point `[t, gain]`. The plan writes no bus that holds no segment and no bus, since its sum is silence and it adds nothing to the bus that holds it, so a compound clip none of whose clips has sound has no bus in the plan. This shape changed with buses: a compound clip's or a template instance's sounds are segments under `buses`, and its level is on its bus's `envelope` and in no segment's gain envelope, where the plan before buses held them among the plan's `segments` with that level multiplied into their gain envelopes. The frames that writes this shape writes a version in `mixPlan` raised from the one that wrote the shape before it ([FRAMES-18](../FRAMES-18/SPEC.md)).

## Alternatives considered

Roles that read the resolved document, as an NLE's playback engine reads its project: every role would implement keyframes, parenting and transitions again, and two of them would disagree.

One item kind per shape (a box, a fill, an arrow): every new shape would need a new item in every renderer. One `path` item with three geometry forms and one stroke model is what Core Graphics, Skia and SVG all draw, so a renderer maps it to its own path, a cubic segment to Core Graphics' `addCurve`, Skia's `cubicTo` and SVG's `C`.

A polyline form kept beside the path form for paths whose tangents are all 0: one outline would have two forms, every reader would handle both, and a path whose tangents keyframe to 0 would change form between instants. A path's vertices with their control points written as points, as PostScript's `curveto` takes them: the display list keeps the vertex and its tangents as the document writes them, so what `evaluate` reports is read against the document vertex by vertex, and a renderer adds each tangent to its vertex.

A flattening tolerance fixed by the specification, a curve drawn as straight pieces no further than a set distance from it: the outline is the curve, as an ellipse and a rounded corner are the curves they name, and how a renderer rasters a curve is its own, as two renderers give the same geometry and not the same pixels ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). A tolerance in the item's coordinates would show facets under a zoom, and one in output pixels is what every renderer's own flattening already keeps.

An arrowhead along the outline's direction at its tip, as SVG orients a marker with `orient="auto"`: its base would lie off a curved stroke, which would show past one side of it and leave a gap at the other ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#alternatives-considered)).

Masks as hard clip paths, as SVG `clipPath` is: an editor's mask is feathered and combined by mode, and a clip path is only the case of feather 0 and one mask.

Feather in the mask's own coordinates: a feather would then soften with a zoom on its parent, where an editor sets it to look the same on the output.

A storyline picture and the clips grouped with it drawn as one nested list, moved together by a transition: a parent's transform already carries a clip with its picture, and a compound clip gives a group that must leave as one picture. The storyline picture alone is nested, inside its transition motion, because its own transform and the motion are two coordinate stages that one item's transform cannot hold. A transition field on the picture item: the order of the common fields would then differ for one kind of item. Both items keep the clip as their origin, as a compound clip's do, so `evaluate` and `locate` still see one place.

A decoder that takes a page's whole map to the output, and not a region at a scale: a page would be drawn as its paths are in place, but every frame of a pan would draw it again, 1 to 2 s for a dense page ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#notes)), and its thin lines would shimmer as the offset moves.

A compound clip with an empty chain folded into the bus that holds it, its level multiplied into its sounds' gain envelopes: a compound clip's level would then reach its sounds by two paths, and their samples differ where an inner gain and the level both move, so adding an entry that changes nothing to a chain would change the sound. Every compound clip is a bus, and its level is applied to its sum.

## Notes

**Note.** The arc length of a cubic Bézier, and the point at a given length along it, have no closed form, as an ellipse's have none, so trim, dashes and arrowheads are placed by a numerical integration of the curve's speed. The rules name the exact lengths, which an implementation reaches as closely as its integration does, as it does for an ellipse. The bounding box of a curve, its extremes where a quadratic is 0, is exact ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md#shapes)).

**Note.** Two regions of one page of a PDF at one scale need not agree to the last value where they overlap, since a rasteriser's coverage of a thin stroke depends on the region it draws ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#notes)). So a renderer that changes the region it asks for from frame to frame while the scale holds still, as a pan over a page shown at a constant size does, can make thin lines shimmer, and one that asks for the whole page whenever its decoder can hold the page's picture at that scale ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md#rules)) does not. A renderer that reuses a larger region it drew for an earlier frame, as a cache kept across a pan would, is what the rule that the region and the scale are a function of the frame alone rules out: `render --at` draws a frame with no frame before it, so its picture and the master's frame at the same time would differ, and frames drawn in parallel would differ by the order they were drawn in. A renderer that rounds s up by a rule of the frame, to the next power of two for one, draws a page under a scale animation again only when that rounding changes.
