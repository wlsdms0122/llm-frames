# FRAMES-20: Evaluation, display lists and the mix plan

Status: accepted

## Summary

`evaluate(resolved, t)`: the display list at a time, its four item kinds (`picture`, `path`, `glyphs`, `adjust`) with the fields every item shares (transform, opacity, blend, mask, track matte, reveal), and the mix plan of a sequence's sound.

## Motivation

One frame of an edit is many rules at once: keyframes and their eases, a parent's transform, a tracked motion, an animation, a transition moving two pictures, a feathered mask, a track matte, a retimed frame. If a renderer had to know those rules, each new renderer would implement the editing model again, and two renderers would disagree on where a title is in the middle of a dissolve. Evaluation does everything above pixels once and hands a role only what to draw, as a compositor's render graph hands a GPU only draw calls. The items are the primitives every 2D renderer already has (an image drawn into a rectangle, a path filled and stroked, glyphs, a filter over a backdrop), so Core Graphics, Skia or an SVG renderer can each draw them.

## Rules

`evaluate(resolved, t)` gives the display list at sequence time t: the frame size, its background, its `compositing`, and drawing items bottom first, every value already sampled at t. Where a clip with motion blur owns a value the frame at t reads, the outermost list holds N sample lists of items in place of its items, each a whole list of the frame at one instant of the frame's exposure, and the frame is their mean ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). Only the outermost list of a frame holds samples, and a nested list never does. Every list, nested ones included, carries the `compositing` its items are composited in: the sequence's, or in the list of a compound clip or a template instance that one's own ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). The outermost list also carries the sequence's `colorSpace`, which the encoder writes its frames in, and its `peak`, the `format.peak` an `rec2100-pq` output is clipped to or `null` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)). A nested list carries none, since a picture's source carries what the decoder needs: its clip's `colorSpace` and the peak an HDR picture of it is tone mapped to ([JSON form](#json-form)). Frame k of a sequence is `evaluate(resolved, k / fps)`, and a PNG is the display list at the time it is rendered at ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)).

### Fields every item has

| Field | Value | Meaning | Moves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `transform` | `[a, b, c, d, tx, ty]` | the affine map from the item's own coordinates to output pixels: a point (x, y) goes to (a x + c y + tx, b x + d y + ty), as Core Graphics and SVG write a matrix | yes |
| `opacity` | 0 to 1 | multiplies the item's alpha | yes |
| `blend` | a mode name | how the item composites with what is below it ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)) | no |
| `mask` | a list of masks, or `null` | each `{geometry, transform, feather, invert, mode}` (below) | each mask's `geometry`, `transform` and `feather` |
| `trackMatte` | `{source, mode, invert}` or `null` | `source` a nested display list, `mode` `alpha` or `luma` (below) | no |
| `reveal` | `[x, y, w, h]` or `null` | the part of the item that shows, a rectangle in its own coordinates before its transform | yes |

- An item is drawn in this order: its content in its own coordinates, with its alpha cut to its `reveal` rectangle (a hard straight edge) and, for a `picture`, multiplied by its `matte`; then carried by `transform` to output pixels; then its alpha multiplied by its mask, its track matte and its `opacity`; then composited onto what is below by its `blend`, with the formulas of W3C Compositing and Blending Level 1 (source-over with the mode's blend function), each in the form [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing) gives under the list's `compositing`. `reveal` turns with the item, so a wipe on a rotated title follows the title.
- An item's transform may scale its axes differently, mirror and skew ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md), [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)). Its **length factor** k is √|ad - bc|, the geometric mean of the scales it applies.
- **The widths of the lines an item draws are in output pixels, drawn the same in every direction on its outline as the transform carries it**, as SVG draws a stroke under `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`: a stroke's `width`, a picture's `border` width, a run's stroke `width`, a `shadow`'s `radius`, and a mask's `feather`. The other lengths are in the item's own coordinates and are carried by the transform with its geometry ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)): the lengths of the outline (a corner `radius`, a highlight's or background's `radius`, an outset already applied to the geometry, a stroke's `dash` and `dashOffset`), and, as an effect applied before a layer's transform is, a `shadow`'s `offset` and a `picture`'s filter lengths.
- Evaluation writes lengths so that a clip's lengths come out as those of [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) multiplied by the clip's scale factor and its animation's scale ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)), and not by its parent's ([FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md)): a length drawn in output pixels is written at that size, and a carried length at that size over k.
- Inside a nested display list, output pixels are the pixels of that list's own frame, at whatever size the list is drawn.
- Corners given by `radius` are circular arcs of that radius in the item's own coordinates, each tangent to the two sides it joins, as Canvas `arcTo` rounds a corner, and the transform carries them with the outline, so a transform that scales its axes differently draws them as elliptical arcs. A radius whose arcs would reach past the middle of a side is cut to the largest that does not: for a rectangle, half its shorter side, as CSS and Core Graphics take it.
- A `shadow` is `{radius, offset, color}`: the item's alpha after its reveal, matte, mask and track matte, blurred by a Gaussian whose standard deviation is `radius`, moved by `offset` in the item's own coordinates, filled with `color` and drawn below the item. The item and its shadow take `opacity` and `blend` together.

### Items

| Item | Fields | Draws | Moves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `picture` | `source` (a frame of a media file or image sequence, by index, with the colour space its clip or transition cover reads it in and the peak an HDR picture of it is tone mapped to, or a nested display list), `rect` (the source region), `dest` (the rectangle it fills, in its own coordinates), `filter` (entries `{ops, mix}`, each its ops in order mixed with its input by `mix` as [FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md) says, applied at `dest` size), `matte` (`{source, frame, colorSpace, progress, softness, invert}` or `null`), `radius`, `border` `{width, color}` or `null`, `shadow` or `null` | a region of a picture scaled into a rectangle, filtered, then cut by its transition matte | `rect`, `dest`, `filter`, the `matte`'s `progress`, `radius`, `border`, `shadow` |
| `path` | `geometry`, `fill` (a paint or `null`), `stroke` (`{width, paint, join, cap, start, end, dash, dashOffset}` or `null`), `trim` `[a, b]`, `shadow` or `null` | a rectangle, an ellipse or a polyline, filled and stroked | `geometry`, `fill`, the `stroke`'s `width`, `paint`, `dash` and `dashOffset`, `trim`, `shadow` |
| `glyphs` | `runs` (each: font file, face, size, colour, `stroke` `{width, color}` or `null`, `highlight` `{rects, radius, color, bands}` or `null`, `underline` `{rects, color, bands}` or `null`, glyph ids and positions), `layer` (`null`, or one of `highlights`, `underlines`, `strokes` and `fills` for a unit's item), `unitBox` (`null`, or the unit's box `[x, y, w, h]` in the title's box for a unit's item), `unitGlyphBox` (`null`, or the unit's glyph box `[x, y, w, h]` in the title's box for a unit's item), `background` `{rect, radius, color}` or `null`, `size` | glyphs, with their outlines, highlights, underlines and background | each run's colour, `stroke`, `highlight`, `underline` and glyph positions, `unitBox`, `unitGlyphBox`, `background`, `size` |
| `adjust` | `filter` | the filter applied to everything drawn below it in the same list | `filter` |

- **The Moves columns mark the fields evaluation computes from parameters that take time**, the only fields in which the sample lists of a frame may differ ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). Every other field is the same in each sample, and a nested list in a `source` is held to the same rule item by item. A `filter` moves in its entries' `mix` and its ops' parameters, and never in a `grain` op's `seed` ([What evaluation has done](#what-evaluation-has-done)). A field added to an item takes the mark in its row or does not.

### Pictures

- Evaluation picks a media source's frame by [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md) from the pinned timestamps at the media time the clip's time map gives, its `timeRemap` included ([FRAMES-8](../FRAMES-8/SPEC.md)), and the renderer draws that frame. Part of `rect` that lies outside the source is transparent.
- A picture's `border` lies inside `dest`, its outer edge following the rounded corners.
- A nested display list stands for a compound clip or a template instance at its mapped time, for a storyline picture inside its transition motion ([below](#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)), or for a title animated by unit as its group ([Glyphs and adjustments](#glyphs-and-adjustments)). A nested list has no background, so what its items leave uncovered is transparent, except the list of a compound clip or a template instance, whose background is its own `format.background` ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)). It is drawn at the size it is shown at in the output, as its items would be drawn in place, so a list shown larger is as sharp as its items drawn larger and every length inside it (a shadow, a filter length, a feather) keeps its size against the list's own frame. The picture's `rect`, `dest`, `filter`, `radius`, `border` and `shadow` apply to it as to any source of the list's size.
- A `matte` is a transition matte ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)), stretched over `dest`. The picture shows where the matte's luminance in `frame` (picked from its media time) is below `progress`, with an edge `softness` wide, inverted when `invert` is true. With L the matte's luma ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)) clamped to 0 to 1 (1 - L when inverted), p the `progress` and s the `softness`, the picture's alpha is multiplied by clamp((p × (1 + s) - L) / s, 0, 1), and when s is 0 by its limit (1 where L < p, p where L = p, 0 where L > p), so progress 0 shows none of the picture and progress 1 all of it. `matte` and `trackMatte` are different things on purpose: the first is a wipe between two storyline pictures, the second a clip that cuts another.

### Paths

`geometry` is one of:

| Form | Outline |
| --- | --- |
| `{"rect": {"rect": [x, y, w, h], "radius": r}}` | a rectangle with circular corners of radius `r` (0 for square corners) |
| `{"ellipse": {"rect": [x, y, w, h]}}` | the ellipse inscribed in the rectangle |
| `{"polyline": {"points": [[x, y], ...], "closed": false}}` | straight segments through the points in order, at least two, and from the last back to the first when `closed` |

- **Direction.** Every outline runs clockwise in the item's own coordinates, y down, which is clockwise on the output unless the transform mirrors, from a fixed start: a rect from the top left corner (the start of its top edge, just after the top left corner's arc), an ellipse from its top, a polyline from its first point.
- **Outset.** Evaluation writes a rect's or an ellipse's geometry grown on every side by its `outset` in the item's own coordinates, written as a carried length, so a per-axis scale stretches it with the outline.
- **Fill.** `fill` paints the inside of the geometry by the nonzero winding rule, an open polyline closed by a straight segment from its last point to its first, as SVG fills it. `trim` does not cut the fill.
- **Stroke.** The stroke is `width` wide, centred on the outline. `join` (`miter`, `round` or `bevel`) shapes each corner of a polyline, and a miter longer than 4 × `width` is drawn as a bevel, the SVG default miter limit. `cap` (`butt`, `round` or `square`) shapes the ends of an open outline and of a trimmed piece. A whole closed outline has no ends.
- **Trim.** `trim` is `[a, b]`, each from 0 to 1. With L the length of the outline in the item's own coordinates, the stroke is drawn along it from min(a, b) × L to max(a, b) × L, measured from its start in its direction, as After Effects trims a path in its layer's space, so a scale does not move where a trim falls. Its width, caps, joins and arrowheads are then drawn in output pixels, and its dashes are laid along it as lengths carried with the outline, as every dash length is ([FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md)). `[0, 1]` draws all of it, and `a = b` draws no stroke. A trimmed piece of a closed outline is open, with its caps at both ends.
- **Arrowheads.** `start` and `end` are `none` or `arrow`. An `arrow` is drawn at the start or the end of the stroke as drawn (after `trim`): a filled isosceles triangle in the stroke's paint, its tip at that end point, 4 × `width` long along the outline's direction there and 4 × `width` wide at its base. The line stops at the middle of the base, with its cap there, so nothing of the line shows past the tip. Where the stroke as drawn is shorter than its heads, each head is scaled down about its tip, keeping its shape, so that their lengths add up to the stroke's length. A whole closed outline has no ends and draws no arrowhead.
- **Dashes.** `dash` is `null` for a whole stroke, or a list of an even number of lengths, the dashes and gaps in turn (evaluation writes an odd list of [FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md) twice over). The pattern is laid along the stroke as drawn, from its start after `trim`, moved back along it by `dashOffset` and repeated to its end, as SVG `stroke-dasharray` and `stroke-dashoffset` lay it. Each dash takes `cap` at its ends and `join` at its corners. Arrowheads are not dashed.
- **Paint.** A `fill` and a stroke's `paint` are a paint, written from a shape's `fill` and from its stroke's `color` or `gradient`: a colour, or a gradient `{"linear": {"from", "to", "stops"}}` or `{"radial": {"center", "radius", "stops"}}` whose points and radius evaluation has written in the item's own coordinates from the shape's box at t, and whose `stops` are `[offset, colour]` with their offsets already clamped and in order. The colour at each point is the one [FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md) gives, and the gradient is carried by the transform with the geometry. A stroke's paint colours its arrowheads too.
- The stroke and its arrowheads are one shape: where they overlap, nothing is drawn twice. The fill is drawn first and the stroke over it.

```json
{ "item": "path", "origin": { "place": "clips[2]", "id": "pointer", "kind": "shape" },
  "transform": [1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0], "opacity": 1, "blend": "normal", "mask": null, "trackMatte": null, "reveal": null,
  "geometry": { "polyline": { "points": [[420, 300], [760, 520]], "closed": false } },
  "fill": null, "stroke": { "width": 8, "paint": "color(srgb 1 1 1 / 1)", "join": "round", "cap": "round", "start": "none", "end": "arrow",
              "dash": null, "dashOffset": 0 },
  "trim": [0, 0.6], "shadow": null }
```

### Glyphs and adjustments

- A `glyphs` item draws its background, then every run's highlight rectangles in run order, each with corners of its `radius`, then every run's underline rectangles, then the outline of every glyph of every run with a stroke, each run's glyphs as one stroke in its width and colour, centred on the outline with round joins and caps, then every glyph's fill, so no stroke covers a fill. Glyph positions are in the item's own coordinates, its text box `size` with its origin at the top left.
- Evaluation writes a title's runs at t from the resolved runs ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)): each run's glyphs and pieces raised by its `baselineShift` at t, each highlight rectangle its piece grown by its `padding` at t with corners of its `radius` at t, and each underline rectangle a band across its piece `width` thick at t with its top edge `offset` below the baseline at t. For a title with `lines`, it moves the glyphs, the pieces, the units' boxes and their parts along their lines by the tracking at t and with their lines by the line height at t, places each line again by `align`, and writes the text box `size` at t ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)). Its `background` takes as `rect` the text box at t grown by the background's `padding` at t, and the background's `radius` and `color` at t.
- **A title animated by unit ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)) is drawn as a group**, as After Effects composites a precomposition: one `picture` item whose source is a nested display list the size of the list that holds it, drawn in that list's output pixels, its `rect` and `dest` that whole list and its transform the identity, which carries the title's opacity, blend, mask and track matte as the one `glyphs` item of a title without `by` carries them, and no filter, radius, border or shadow. Inside it the title draws one `glyphs` item for what is not a unit, its background, with no runs, then four passes over the units, each in reading order: every unit's item of `layer` `highlights`, then of `underlines`, then of `strokes`, then of `fills`. A unit's item holds that unit's glyphs in their runs with the parts of their highlights and underlines it carries ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), its box as `unitBox`, its glyph box ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)) at that instant as `unitGlyphBox`, and no background. In a unit's item a run's `highlight` and `underline` hold the whole pieces the unit has parts of, and `bands`, one `[x0, x1]` for each of those pieces in the title's box: the item draws each piece as it is drawn whole, its padding and corners included, and cut to its band, the part the unit carries. A band's end inside its piece, where two units' parts meet, cuts whole pixels, each going to one of the two parts, so no seam shows between them. Outside a unit's item `bands` is `null`. The items inside the group are all in the title's box, of its `size`, with `normal` blend and no mask or track matte. The background's transform and `reveal` are the title's and its opacity 1. A unit's transform is the title's composed with the unit's motion about the centre of its box, its opacity the unit's, and its `reveal` the part of what the unit draws that the unit's reveal shows (`null` at reveal 1), cut to the title's `reveal`, the loop's, when both are given, and either one alone otherwise ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)). What a unit's item draws is its `unitGlyphBox` grown by half its stroke and joined with the parts it carries, as what a title draws is its text box grown by half its stroke and joined with its highlights and underlines ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)). A stroke is drawn in output pixels on outlines the item's transform carries, so half its stroke reaches, in the title's box, as far as the transform's inverse carries a disc of that radius along each axis, and nothing where the transform flattens the plane. An item of a `layer` draws only that step of the order above for its glyphs, so the passes keep every highlight under every underline, stroke and fill of the title. A unit's opacity applies to each of its items on its own, so while a unit enters or leaves at an opacity below 1 its highlight and stroke show through its own fill. Once every unit has entered and before any leaves, the group holds the picture the title draws without `by`, and the title's opacity, blend, mask and track matte apply to it once, so the title draws as it does without `by` at any of them. A title not animated by unit draws one item with `layer` `null`, every step.
- An `adjust` item covers the whole frame of its list: the filter is applied to everything drawn below it in that list, and the filtered picture is composited onto the unfiltered one with the item's `blend`, its alpha multiplied by the item's reveal, mask, track matte and `opacity`. Its `transform` carries its `reveal` only.

### Masks

A mask is `{geometry, transform, feather, invert, mode}`: `geometry` in one of the path forms, `transform` the affine map from its coordinates to output pixels, `feather` a length in output pixels, `invert` a boolean and `mode` `add`, `subtract` or `intersect`.

- A mask's value m at a point of the output is the coverage of its geometry carried by its transform (1 inside, 0 outside, a polyline always closed, by the nonzero rule), blurred by a Gaussian whose standard deviation is `feather` (none at 0), and 1 - m when `invert` is true.
- The masks of an item combine in list order, as After Effects combines a layer's masks. The result starts at 0 when the first mask's mode is `add` and at 1 when it is `subtract` or `intersect`, and each mask then changes it: `add` gives r + m - r × m, `subtract` gives r × (1 - m), `intersect` gives r × m. The item's alpha is multiplied by the result.
- A mask's transform carries it to the output pixels of the list that holds its item: through the clip's coordinate space, its own transform, its parent's, and the crop of a clip that follows its own media ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), so a mask stays on what it cuts. A storyline picture's transition motion is carried by its outer item ([Storyline pictures and transitions](#storyline-pictures-and-transitions)), not by its mask.

### Track mattes

- A `trackMatte`'s `source` is a nested display list the size of the list that holds the item, drawn in that list's output pixels: the items the matte clip draws at t ([FRAMES-11](../FRAMES-11/SPEC.md)). A clip used as a matte draws no item of its own. Outside the matte clip's visible window its source holds no item.
- At each point of the output the matte's value v is the source's alpha for `alpha`, and for `luma` the source's luma ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)) clamped to 0 to 1, multiplied by its alpha, so a transparent part of a luma matte counts as black. With `invert` it is 1 - v. The item's alpha is multiplied by v.

### Storyline pictures and transitions

- A storyline clip's picture is two `picture` items, one inside the other, so its own transform and the transition motion each act in their own coordinates, in the order [FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md) gives. The inner item is its media: its conformed crop as `rect`, its box ([FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md)) as `dest`, its filter, `radius`, `border` and `shadow`, and its own transform, opacity, mask and track matte, with `blend` `normal`. It is the one item of a nested display list the size of the frame. The outer item is a `picture` whose `source` is that list and whose `dest` is the frame, carrying the clip's `blend` and, during a transition, the transition's motion for its side ([FRAMES-14](../FRAMES-14/SPEC.md)): the motion's scale, rotation and `x`, `y` (multiples of the frame) as its transform about the centre of the frame, the motion's opacity as its opacity, the motion's reveal as its reveal rectangle, and on the incoming picture the transition's `matte`. Out of a transition the outer item's transform is the identity and its opacity 1. **A storyline picture's transition motion is carried by its outer item and by nothing inside it.** The two storyline pictures stack as `over` says. A solid is drawn the same way, its inner item a `path` item, a rect the size of the frame filled with its colour, so a matte transition cuts a solid as it cuts media.
- A transition cover is an item above both storyline pictures and below the clips on positive lanes: a colour cover a `path` item, a rect the size of the frame filled with the cover's colour, and a media cover a `picture` item whose `dest` is the frame, each with the cover's opacity at t.
- Items come bottom first in the stacking order of [FRAMES-9](../FRAMES-9/SPEC.md). The background is the list's own `background`, not an item.
- An item's transform is its clip's own (position, anchor, scale, rotation, the tracked motion of a clip that follows its parent's media, and its animation's transform part; a clip that follows its own media moves its crop and mask instead, [FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)) composed with its parent's as [FRAMES-10](../FRAMES-10/SPEC.md) gives it. A parent's transition motion moves its children, which are items outside its nested list; its opacity, reveal, mask and matte do not.

### What evaluation has done

Evaluation has done everything above pixels: keyframes and eases (springs included), clip areas at t ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), animation spans and how they combine, transition motion, cover and matte progress, reveal, parent transforms, masks, track mattes, tracked motion, time remapping, and the visible windows. A role is left with the items as written.

- Evaluation writes `seed` into every `grain` op: the number of the output frame being drawn, k for frame k of the outermost sequence (evaluated at k / fps), and the frame whose interval holds t for `still`, the `evaluate` command and a PNG. Nested lists take the outer frame's seed, so grain belongs to the output frame and two pictures in a transition share it.
- Evaluation has also exposed every frame in which a clip with `motionBlur` owns a value the frame reads ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)): sample list i is the frame at the i-th instant of the outermost sequence's exposure, the same for every clip, at which every parameter that takes time and that a clip with `motionBlur` owns is read, clamped to t's side of every jump written on it, and every other at t, while media frames, tracked motion, the seed and which clips draw are those of t. It writes the samples whether or not they differ.

### JSON form

- In the JSON form ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)) an item names what it draws under `item` (`picture`, `path`, `glyphs` or `adjust`), and carries `origin`, the clip or op that drew it as `{place, id, kind}` as the `evaluate` command reports it ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)), or `null` for a transition cover. Every item carries every field of its kind and the common fields, `null` where it has none.
- A `picture`'s `source` is `{media, frame, colorSpace, targetPeak}`, the pinned media, the frame's index, the `colorSpace` its clip or transition cover writes or `null` where the file's tags apply, and `targetPeak`, BT.2390's target peak L_max in cd/m² ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#tone-mapping)): the outermost sequence's peak, as the Peak column of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#colour-spaces) gives it, its `format.peak` for `rec2100-pq`, where its clip's `toneMap` is `true`, as a transition cover's always is, and `null` where it is `false`. The decoder tone maps an HDR picture of it where the file's source peak L_W is above that number, and takes it as display light where L_W is at or below it or `toneMap` is `null` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)). Or it is `{list}`, a nested list without the version. Its `rect` is `null` where it shows the whole source. A transition matte's `source` is the pinned media and its `colorSpace` the one the matte writes, or `null` where the file's tags apply, which the renderer reads as an analysis picture whatever the sequence ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), so it carries no target peak. A track matte's `source` is `{list}`. A run's font is pinned as `{file, sha256}`. Colours are `color(srgb r g b / a)` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). A list's `compositing` follows its `size` and `background`, and the outermost list's `colorSpace` and `peak` come before its `compositing`.
- An outermost list with samples carries `samples`, a list of its sample lists in order, each a list of items, in place of `items`, and every other key as a list without samples does. A list read back stops on an outermost list that carries both `items` and `samples` or neither, on `samples` with fewer than two lists, on a nested list that carries `samples`, and on sample lists that differ in what they hold: each holds the same items in the same order as the first, equal in every field the Moves columns do not mark ([Items](#items)), since content and membership are those of t in every sample.
- The model keeps one seed per list, its nested lists included, so a list read back takes the seed of the first `grain` written, stops on a `grain` with another, and takes 0 when it has no `grain`. A frame index outside its media's frames stops.

### The mix plan

The mix plan is the sound of a whole sequence: a list of segments, each with a media file, its media start and speed, the sequence times it starts and ends, its audio filter, a gain envelope and a pan envelope.

- A storyline clip's segment runs over its drawn range with its edges moved by its `split` ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), and its gain envelope's crossfade at an edge is moved with that edge. A visual media clip's segment, connected or in `clips`, runs over its visible window with its edges moved by its `split`.
- The gain envelope is a list of points `[t, gain]` joined in straight lines. It already holds volume keyframes and their eases, fades, and transition crossfades on the transition's `audio` curve ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)): with u the progress through the transition, `constant-power` gives the outgoing sound cos(uπ/2) and the incoming sin(uπ/2), and `constant-gain` gives them 1 - u and u. Eased keyframes and the crossfade curve are sampled at the frame rate.
- The pan envelope is a list of points `[t, pan]` joined in straight lines, from -1 (left) to 1 (right), the pan keyframes and their eases sampled at the frame rate. The mixer pans by the pan law of [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md).
- An envelope has a point at both ends of its segment, at every keyframe and every end of a fade or transition, and at every frame time k / fps inside a span where its value moves: between two keyframes of different values, or over a fade or transition. A value that holds has no points between its bends.
- The audio filter is the segment's entries `{ops, mix}` ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), `[]` when it has none. A number in it, a `mix` included, that holds over the segment is written as a number, and one that moves as an envelope of `[t, value]` points joined in straight lines, with its points where a gain envelope has them, a `frequency` sampled in log space. The mixer runs each entry's ops on the segment's sound and mixes the result with the entry's input by its `mix`, before the gain and pan envelopes, takes each moving value from its envelope at the sample, and computes an op's coefficients again at least every 32 samples while one of its values moves.
- The mixer mixes each segment's channels into the output's as its role decides ([FRAMES-21](../FRAMES-21/SPEC.md)). A loop is one segment per repeat, and a compound clip's sounds are segments of their own. A clip with `timeRemap` has no segment.
- In the JSON form a segment is `{media, mediaStart, speed, start, end, filter, envelope, pan}`, `media` the pinned media, `filter` its entries, each envelope point `[t, gain]` and each pan point `[t, pan]`.

## Alternatives considered

Roles that read the resolved document, as an NLE's playback engine reads its project: every role would implement keyframes, parenting and transitions again, and two of them would disagree.

One item kind per shape (a box, a fill, an arrow): every new shape would need a new item in every renderer. One `path` item with three geometry forms and one stroke model is what Core Graphics, Skia and SVG all draw, so a renderer maps it to its own path.

Masks as hard clip paths, as SVG `clipPath` is: an editor's mask is feathered and combined by mode, and a clip path is only the case of feather 0 and one mask.

Feather in the mask's own coordinates: a feather would then soften with a zoom on its parent, where an editor sets it to look the same on the output.

A storyline picture and the clips grouped with it drawn as one nested list, moved together by a transition: a parent's transform already carries a clip with its picture, and a compound clip gives a group that must leave as one picture. The storyline picture alone is nested, inside its transition motion, because its own transform and the motion are two coordinate stages that one item's transform cannot hold. A transition field on the picture item: the order of the common fields would then differ for one kind of item. Both items keep the clip as their origin, as a compound clip's do, so `evaluate` and `locate` still see one place.
