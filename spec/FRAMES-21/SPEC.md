# FRAMES-21: Roles and providers

Status: accepted

## Summary

The four roles that read media and make pixels, sound and files, the providers resolve calls (shaping) and the analysis commands call (tracking, text recognition), the working space and the files frames writes.

## Motivation

The engine is meant to be swapped, as an NLE swaps its playback engine or a compositing tool its GPU back end: Apple's frameworks first, Skia or ffmpeg later. Without a fixed contract for what each role takes and gives, and for what two of them must agree on (which frame a time names, what colour space blending happens in, how a mono source is spread over two speakers), two implementations would each be right and give different results. Fixing those is what lets one replace another, and keeping text shaping as a provider resolve calls, and tracking and text recognition as providers whose results the document writes as values, keeps every value a role sees fixed in the resolved document.

## Rules

Rendering is four roles. The renderer and the mixer take the two outputs of evaluation, the decoder reads media files for all of frames, and the encoder writes files.

| Role | Takes | Gives | Also |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| decoder | a request of one of two kinds, a frame of raster media (a video, an image sequence, an image) by its index, or a page of a PDF ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)) by a region of its pixel grid at a scale ([below](#rules)), each with the colour space the place that names it reads it in when it writes one, and the target peak to tone map an HDR picture to, or none, or a request for an analysis picture; or for sound a media time range of the file's sound ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#rules)) | for a frame, a picture of working values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)), RGBA with premultiplied alpha in floating point, at the media's bit depth or more, an HDR picture tone mapped to that peak where its source peak is above it and display light otherwise; for a page, the region as a picture of working values, RGBA with premultiplied alpha in floating point; for an analysis picture, that picture ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)); or sound | the providers, the analysis commands and `transcode` read through it too |
| renderer | display lists | frames of working values, as pixels | reads pictures through the decoder, a transition matte's as an analysis picture, a `displace` op's map as working values read by its file's tags ([FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md#displacement)), a page of a PDF as the part it draws at the scale it shows it at ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), applies filter ops, LUTs, displacement maps, blend modes and transition mattes, draws paths, masks, track mattes and glyphs, and draws a frame given as sample lists as their mean ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)) |
| mixer | the mix plan | audio, as samples | reads sound through the decoder, a `convolve` op's impulse response as it reads any sound ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md#audio-filters)), stretches time with the pitch kept, runs each segment's audio filter, convolving where a `convolve` op says, applies each segment's gain and pan envelopes, sums each bus over its span, runs its chain, applies its level and adds it to the bus that holds it, the outermost over the mix plan's length ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)) |
| encoder | frames and audio, and the colour space to write them in | a file, encoded and tagged as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md) gives | `transcode` uses it with the decoder |

- **A page is asked for by region and scale, and a region is part of one picture.** A decoder draws a page of a PDF at a scale s on the page's pixel grid at that scale, which is anchored at the page's media pixel (0, 0): its pixel (i, j) covers the media pixels from (i / s, j / s) to ((i + 1) / s, (j + 1) / s). It is asked for a region of that grid, [i, j, n, m] in whole pixels, and gives that region as one draw, a picture of n by m pixels, so a region's edges fall on whole pixels, and what of it lies off the page is transparent. A region whose n or m is under 1, or a scale of 0 or less, is refused. A picture asks for one region, which its frame alone decides ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), and nothing stitches regions: two draws of different regions need not agree to the last value where they overlap, since a rasteriser's coverage of a thin stroke depends on the region it draws ([Notes](#notes)). The same page, scale and region give the same picture. The whole page of w by h media pixels at scale s is the region [0, 0, max(1, round(w × s)), max(1, round(h × s))]. A provider reads the whole page at scale 4, or at the largest scale at which the decoder holds the whole page where that is less, and what it gives is divided by that scale, so it is in the page's points ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)). `transcode` asks for the whole page at its `--scale` ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#transcode)).
- Each role supports everything its input names (items, geometry forms, joins, caps, arrowheads, mask modes, track matte modes, filter ops, audio ops, blend modes, LUTs, displacement maps, impulse responses, mattes, media formats, codecs) or refuses before it starts, naming the first thing it lacks and its place. It never drops one silently. A decoder states the largest picture it holds, as the most pixels on a side, a constant of the decoder and the same on every machine. It refuses so a request whose picture's size the request fixes and that is larger (a frame of raster media, a page at a `--scale` written to `transcode`), naming the picture's size in pixels. The largest scale at which a decoder holds the whole page, or a region of it, is L / x, with L the decoder's largest picture side and x the longer side in pixels of the page, or of the region, at scale 1. A provider's reading of a page and `transcode`'s default scale for one fall to the largest scale at which the decoder holds the whole page, so neither is refused for its size. The renderer's request for a page fixes no size: it asks for the region at a scale of at least s that its frame alone decides, or at the largest scale at which the decoder holds that region where that is less, and resamples from there ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), so no frame fails for its size and no pass over every frame is needed. A renderer may also have a largest scale of its own, the largest at which it states the region's pixel positions exactly; past it, it asks at that scale and resamples from there the same way, so that limit is never a refusal either ([Notes](#notes)).
- A renderer draws each frame's items as written ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)): pictures with their rounded corners, borders, shadows and transition mattes, carried onto their `cornerPin`; paths with their fill, their stroke's joins and caps, their arrowheads and their trim; feathered masks combined in order by their modes; track mattes by alpha or luma; and glyphs from the font files and faces named, at the positions written, narrowed and turned as their runs say. It shapes no text and lays nothing out. An engine that only runs a filter graph over time fills the decoder, the mixer or the encoder, not the renderer.
- **A renderer resamples every picture of pixels by one definition.** A picture of pixels (a frame of raster media), a nested list's raster and a page's picture are each a source of pixels shown through one affine map M from the source's pixels to the output's: the picture's `rect` to its `dest`, then its `transform`, then for each nested list that holds it the map that carries that list into the one that holds it. Each source pixel stands for a square of its value, the pixels at the source's edge extended outward past it, and each output pixel of the picture is the mean of that source over a box in the source's pixels centred on the image of the pixel's centre under M⁻¹, as wide along each of the source's axes as the image of the pixel reaches along that axis, and at least one source pixel wide. That mean is multiplied by the geometric coverage of the output pixel by what the picture shows: the area of the pixel inside the image under M of `dest` and of the source's own bounds, with the corners of `dest` rounded by `radius`. So a source point lands exactly where M takes it, a picture at its own size on whole pixels gives its own pixels, a picture moved by part of a pixel or enlarged is the bilinear interpolation of its pixels, each value at its pixel's centre, read at the image of each output pixel's centre, and a picture shrunk is the mean of the source pixels each output pixel's image covers, an area filter, so a pattern finer than an output pixel reaches the output as its mean and does not alias. A picture turned by an angle that is not a multiple of 90 degrees is averaged over the box that bounds its output pixel's image, which is wider than that image. Coverage is the picture's edge, counted once: no other cut at the same edge multiplies it again. A filtered picture ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)) is resampled by the same definition onto the resolution it is filtered at, covered there by the source's own bounds where they lie inside `dest` and extended past `dest`, cut by `dest` with its rounded corners once after its filter and its transition matte, and that resolution is resampled onto the output by the same definition, covered by its own bounds. A renderer computes the mean and the coverage of straight edges within the bounds [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md#tolerances) tests on a named set, and the coverage of a rounded corner by its own rasteriser within the edge bound there. A picture with a corner pin is carried as the next rule gives instead.
- A renderer carries a picture onto its `cornerPin` by the one homography from the corners of its `dest` to the four points ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-a-renderer-holds)). The corners of `dest` land exactly on the points. Values stay premultiplied working values in extended range, with no clamp and no colour conversion. Each output pixel is the mean of the source over that pixel's image under the inverse map ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-a-renderer-holds)), which the renderer approximates within the bounds [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md) tests on a named set, and which one sample per output pixel does not approximate where the picture is shrunk. It promises no particular filter, and the same bytes only on the same machine (below), not across GPU families or OS versions.
- A renderer draws a frame given as sample lists by drawing each of them as it draws a frame without them, background included, summing their premultiplied colours and alphas in the order of the samples, from 0, in floating point of 32 bits or more, and dividing the sum by their number once, so the mean of equal pictures is that picture ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)).
- Every decoder keeps [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) and [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md), and frames are named by index. Frame i is the frame at `frames[i]`: a decoder checks a file's timestamps against the pinned ones and refuses before it starts when it counts them differently. So what a provider read from a frame (a tracked target, recognised text) and the pixels a renderer draws come from the same frame, whichever decoder renders. A decoder reads a video's nominal frame rate that differs from a whole number n, or from n × 1000 / 1001, by at most a hundred-thousandth of itself as exactly that rate, so a file's 29.999998 is 30 and probe, resolve and transcode see the rate it was written at.
- A sequence output lasts `duration` to within one frame. Frame k is drawn at sequence time k / fps for every k whose time, measured to the microsecond ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md)), is before `duration`, and frame 0 always, so the last frame is inside the sequence and floating point noise in `duration` adds no frame.
- Every file is encoded in one colour space and tagged with it as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md) gives: a sequence's output in the sequence's `colorSpace`, an analysis picture in `srgb`, and a `transcode` output in the colour space [FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md) gives it. A video carries the H.273 code points of its space, a JPEG, a HEIC and a PNG an ICC profile of it, and an `srgb` PNG an sRGB chunk, so every encoder's output looks the same in a player.
- `render` writes, in the sequence's colour space, a master or a delivery file. A master is a `.mov` (ProRes 422 HQ, or ProRes 4444 when the format's `background` is not opaque, so the frames carry alpha, with 24-bit PCM sound at 48 kHz in stereo), whose 10 and 12 bits carry every colour space, HDR included, or a `.png` of 16 bits per channel, one frame of a sequence ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)), with alpha where the frame is not opaque. A delivery file is an `.mp4` at the encoder's highest quality with AAC at 256 kbps or more, with transparent parts over black: H.264 for a sequence of an SDR colour space, and HEVC Main 10 for one of an HDR colour space, since H.264 at 8 bits cannot carry HDR. `render` to an `.mp4` writes the delivery file alone, and `--deliver` writes the same file from the same frames as a `.mov` master, in the same step. A sequence without sound mixes to silence ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)), and its `.mov` master and its delivery file each carry a silent track as long as its frames, since a file's tracks do not depend on what the sequence holds: every `.mov` master and every delivery file carries one picture track and one sound track.
- A ProRes 4444 master stores premultiplied alpha and says so in its file. Frame k of a video is presented at exactly k / fps, where a rate that differs from a whole number n by at most a hundred-thousandth of itself is n and one that differs as little from n × 1000 / 1001 is that NTSC rate, so 29.97 is 30000 / 1001 and frame times never drift.
- Every picture from the decoder to the encoder holds working values, one extended-range sRGB for every sequence ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). Every renderer takes each form-free formula in the form the list's `compositing` names and every other formula in its home, as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing) gives them, the filter ops included ([FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md)). Mask coverage and feather are not colours and are computed as written.
- No role leaves temporary files.
- **Each role reports what only it knows of a run** ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#runs)), through its contract, and the render loop times the stages it runs itself. The decoder gives, for each medium whose pictures it read and each conversion it read them by, the medium, the colour space, the conversion to working values and the processor it ran on, how it is given the stored values, the pictures it gave and decoded and the times it opened a reader on the medium again, its time in `decode` and `workingConversion`, the part of that time it spent reading ahead on threads of its own, and the time its caller waited on that reading. The encoder gives, for each file it wrote, the codec its pictures are written as, whether a hardware encoder wrote them, the processor it took the frames to the output's values on, and its time in `outputConversion` and `encode`. The decoder's conversion of a medium by its tags is what `probe` reports ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)).
- **Each role is deterministic, and two of a role agree within a tolerance.** The same display lists on the same decoder and renderer, of the same version of frames, give the same bytes before encoding on every run on the same machine, and on another one the same frames to within the rounding of its GPU. File bytes are not promised, since hardware encoders differ. Two renderers give the same geometry, text, timing and operations in the same working space, and a renderer's frames lie within the tolerances [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md#tolerances) gives of those `quartz` draws, the reference renderer, not on the same bits. Two decoders convert colour by the fixed formulas of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media) and give the same working values to within the tolerance [FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md#tolerances) gives a conversion on the GPU against one on the CPU, except where they upsample the chroma of 4:2:0 and 4:2:2 video, which is each decoder's own filter, so two decoders may differ at edges of colour there. The same mix plan on the same decoder and mixer gives the same samples. Two mixers run the audio ops by their formulas at 48 kHz in floating point of 32 bits or more ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), on each segment and in each bus's chain ([FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md#buses)), so their samples differ only by rounding and, while a value moves, by how often each computes its coefficients again. Every mixer mixes at 48 kHz in stereo: a mono source plays at the same level on both channels before its pan, and a source with more channels is downmixed by ITU-R BS.775, so two mixers give the same levels and channels. Each segment is then panned by its pan envelope with the pan law of [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md).
- Two decoders agree on what [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages) fixes of a page of a PDF: the page, its size and boxes, that its content streams are drawn and not its annotations, that nothing outside its file is read, its pixel grid, and the colour space of its untagged colour ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)). They may differ anywhere in how its content is drawn, not only at edges: which optional content is shown, how a tagged colour space is converted, anti-aliasing, transparency groups and blend modes, soft masks, how an image inside the page is resampled and gradients are each decoder's PDF renderer, as chroma upsampling is each decoder's own filter.
- The BS.775 downmix gives Lo = L + 0.7071 C + 0.7071 Ls and Ro = R + 0.7071 C + 0.7071 Rs, drops LFE, adds a single centre surround (3/1, the 4.0 layout) at 0.5 into both sides, and in 7.1 adds each side's side and rear surround at 0.7071 each. A source of more than two channels is downmixed by the channel layout its file names. A mixer refuses one whose file names no channel layout, naming the file and saying its channels have no layout, and one with a channel BS.775 names no speaker for. A source of one channel whose file names no layout is mono, and one of two channels is left and right.

`--decoder`, `--renderer`, `--mixer` and `--encoder` pick each role by the name of its implementation. The renderer defaults to `quartz` (Core Graphics and Core Image), and the others to `avfoundation`. The decoder `avfoundation-cpu` is `avfoundation` converting every picture on the CPU ([Pictures](#pictures)). The renderer `metal` composites each frame on the GPU ([Pictures](#pictures)).

The `avfoundation` decoder holds a picture of at most 16384 pixels on a side. It is given a video's frames by AVAssetReader in the Y'CbCr its stream stores, a plane of luma and a plane of interleaved chroma at 8 or 10 bits, and takes them to R'G'B' itself, as the next paragraph gives, where the stream is H.264 or HEVC with no alpha, of 8 or 10 bits a sample in luma and chroma alike, at 4:2:0, 4:2:2 or 4:4:4, as the first sequence parameter set of H.264 and the `hvcC` box of HEVC give them, or ProRes 422 of any of its four rates, its matrix is one H.273 gives by its two constants KR and KB (1, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9, a matrix left out or 2 being 1) and its chroma location is not DV 4:2:0. It is given every other video's frames as `16-bit float RGBA`, taken to R'G'B' by VideoToolbox's conversion: a video with alpha, ProRes 4444 and 4444 XQ, H.264 and HEVC of another depth or of monochrome, another codec, and a stream of another matrix or of the DV 4:2:0 chroma location. A still's as ImageIO decodes its file, at the file's depth and channels, and a page as Core Graphics draws it, `32-bit float RGBA`. It keeps one reader open on a video after the last frame it read and reads a frame up to a second after that one by decoding forward from it, and opens a reader again at any other frame. It keeps a reader open on at most 48 videos, since AVFoundation decodes no frame for a reader opened while 64 others decode in the same process, and on videos whose readers hold at most a quarter of the machine's memory together, a reader holding in VideoToolbox's decoding service 28 frames of its video's stored size in the Y'CbCr it is given, or 19 in `16-bit float RGBA`, the most measured on the reference machine ([Notes](#notes)). A video read with no reader open where that bound leaves no room closes the readers of others first, one at a time: the one read longest ago of those holding frames of a turn, else the one read longest ago where it was not read since the asking video's read before this one, else the one read last, so videos read in a cycle past the bound keep the readers of all but a few. A video whose reader the bound closed takes a turn when it is read again: the reader it opens reads 8 frames, the one asked and those after it by the step from the frame asked before, within a second of it, and the decoder keeps the other 7 until a later frame of the video is asked, giving each when it is asked with no reader. One reader is open whatever its cost. It reads a video's next frame ahead on a thread of its own: once two frames of a file are read in one reading, the second after the first, it reads the frame as far on again from the second, where the video has it, by the same read, so it gives the same values as a frame read where it is asked. It gives that frame when it is asked for next, waiting for its reading where it is still going. It reads any other frame where it is asked, once the frames of the same file being read ahead are read, so the reader it is read from is where those readings left it, and a frame whose reading ahead failed is read again where it is asked and fails there. It makes a thread for each frame it reads ahead, and reads ahead fewer frames at once than the machine has processors, reading a frame it would read ahead past that where it is asked. The `avfoundation` decoder, mixer and encoder wait on AVFoundation nowhere else but on the thread that calls them and on the serial queue of each file being written. AVFoundation finishes the work of a read and of a write on Swift's shared pool and on the 64 threads Dispatch runs its concurrent queues on, and itself holds one of those 64 waiting for each read whose sample data it makes ready, so waits on threads of either pool, or near 64 reads at once on any threads, hold every thread that work needs, and no read ends. A still's file, and an image sequence's frame's, is read again for each picture asked of it, which the renderer keeps for the next frame. It draws a page of a PDF with Core Graphics, which converts each tagged colour space the page draws in into the space the decoder draws in by its own colour management. Core Graphics draws DeviceRGB and DeviceGray as `srgb` into every destination space, and no call changes the space they stand for, so the decoder reads a written `colorSpace` other than `srgb` by taking the numbers it draws in that space, which are the untagged colour's only on a page that draws in no other colour space. It refuses such a `colorSpace` on a page that also draws in another colour space, naming the reason: one whose content sets it, or draws an image, a shading or a transparency group in it, a device space under a default colour space included. It counts a page's whole draw as `decode`, any colour conversion included, that of Core Graphics and that of a written `colorSpace` alike, so a page takes no time in `workingConversion`. The `avfoundation` encoder writes a video with AVAssetWriter, which names no encoder to VideoToolbox, and gives `hardware` as whether the compression session VideoToolbox makes for the file's codec, picture size and pixel format, with no encoder named, runs on a hardware encoder. It writes a PNG with ImageIO, on the processor.

**The `avfoundation` decoder takes a video's Y'CbCr to R'G'B' by the stream's tags and a bilinear chroma filter.** A sample's code value D at n bits is taken to Y' and to Cb and Cr by the range the stream's tags name, as BT.709 and BT.2100 quantise them: in the narrow range Y' = (D − 16 × 2ⁿ⁻⁸) / (219 × 2ⁿ⁻⁸) and Cb = (D − 128 × 2ⁿ⁻⁸) / (224 × 2ⁿ⁻⁸), in the full range Y' = D / (2ⁿ − 1) and Cb = (D − 2ⁿ⁻¹) / (2ⁿ − 1), Cr as Cb, and a value beyond the nominal range is kept. R' = Y' + 2 (1 − KR) Cr, B' = Y' + 2 (1 − KB) Cb and G' = (Y' − KR R' − KB B') / (1 − KR − KB), by the stream's matrix. Each pixel takes the Cb and Cr at its centre by straight lines between the two chroma samples nearest it across and the two nearest it down, a chroma sample past the picture's edge being the edge's. A chroma sample stands where the stream's chroma location tag puts it in the block of luma samples it covers, two across and two down at 4:2:0 and two across at 4:2:2: across, on the left luma sample's centre for left, top left and bottom left, and midway between the two for centre, top and bottom; down, at 4:2:0, midway between the two rows for left and centre, on the top row's centre for top left and top, and on the bottom row's for bottom left and bottom. A stream with no chroma location tag is left, the default of H.273. At 4:4:4 each pixel has its own chroma sample. The GPU runs these formulas in single precision and the CPU in double precision, the reference ([Pictures](#pictures)).

### Pictures

**A picture one role hands another is a bitmap or a surface.** A bitmap is pixels in memory. A surface is an IOSurface of working values, RGBA half floats with premultiplied alpha in the machine's byte order, rows top first, laid out as a bitmap of half floats is, so a role that reads pixels reads a surface's where they lie, with no copy and no conversion. Whoever makes a surface has finished writing it before it hands it over, and nothing writes it after. A decoder gives a surface only for working values it converted on the GPU, and a bitmap for every other picture: an analysis picture, a page, and working values it converted on the CPU. A renderer gives each frame in either form, `quartz` as a bitmap and `metal` as a surface. Every role that takes a picture takes either form, the encoder a frame among them. A renderer keeps each media picture a frame reads until the next frame is drawn and lets go of every one that frame did not use, so it holds the pictures of at most two frames, however many files a sequence shows one after another.

**The `metal` renderer composites on the GPU.** It holds each frame as a Core Image image on the GPU, from the list's background up, and lays each item over it as a layer, by source-over or by the kernel of its blend mode, which are the kernels `quartz` blends with. A layer it makes on the GPU reads a picture the decoder gave as a surface where it lies, and uploads one given as a bitmap. It resamples a picture on the GPU by the kernel `quartz` resamples with ([Rules](#rules)), at any scale, turn and position: a picture of a media frame with no filter, transition matte, corner pin, mask, track matte or reveal is a layer made on the GPU from the decoder's pixels, resampled and covered as the rule gives, its rounded corners covered by the rasteriser of `quartz`, under its border, which that rasteriser draws, and over its shadow, cast from the two on the GPU by the operations `quartz` casts one with, times its opacity. An item it has no GPU operation for is drawn by the Core Graphics drawing of `quartz` into a layer of the part of the frame the item covers, an adjustment reading the frame below it, and a frame given as sample lists it has no GPU operation for is drawn whole by that drawing and handed over as a surface. So `metal` draws every list `quartz` draws and refuses what `quartz` refuses, by the same check. It renders each frame once into a surface it has finished writing before it hands it over, and it uses a surface again once no picture holds it.

**The `avfoundation` decoder converts a video's frames on the GPU where it can.** It takes a frame read as working values to a surface on the GPU, by a Metal compute function, once for each frame it decodes, from its Y'CbCr where it is given Y'CbCr and from its R'G'B' half floats otherwise, where the machine has a Metal device, the frame's conversion ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#runs)) is `none`, `space` in an SDR space or `transfer`, and its displayed picture maps its stored pixels one to one, by a turn of a multiple of 90 degrees or a flip. The function takes Y'CbCr to R'G'B' as [Rules](#rules) gives, decodes each channel by the table of its transfer's value for each half float, and a value between two half floats by the straight line between theirs, takes the linear values by the conversion's matrix and encodes them by the sRGB transfer, in single precision. Every other picture, a still, an image sequence's frame, a page, an HDR frame, an analysis picture and a `platform` reading ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#transcode)), it converts on the CPU by the formulas in double precision, which are the reference the GPU's conversion is held to ([FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md#tolerances)). Which processor converts a picture is fixed by the decoder, the medium and the reading, so it is the same for every frame of a medium in a run, and the run's report names it ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#runs)). It writes each frame into a surface no picture holds, one written before where there is one, through a texture made once with that surface, since the GPU maps a surface's memory anew for each texture made on it. `avfoundation-cpu` converts every picture on the CPU.

**The `avfoundation` encoder converts a frame given as a surface on the GPU where it can.** It takes the frame's working values to the output's values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#output)) on the GPU, by a Metal compute function that reads the surface where it lies and writes the pixel buffer it encodes, where the machine has a Metal device, the file's colour space is SDR and the file's picture is the frames' size. The function clips alpha to 0 to 1, takes each pixel's colour unpremultiplied, decodes it by the sRGB transfer, takes the linear values to the space's primaries by the space's matrix, clips them to 0 to 1, encodes them by the space's transfer and premultiplies them again where the file keeps alpha, in single precision, and writes each component as its nearest code value, a half way value rounded away from zero. A frame given as a bitmap, a file of an HDR space and a file whose picture is scaled from the frames it converts on the CPU by the formulas in double precision, which are the reference the GPU's conversion is held to ([FRAMES-24](../FRAMES-24/SPEC.md#tolerances)). A renderer gives every frame in one form, so the processor is the same for every frame of a file, and the run's report names it ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#runs)).

and each glyph with the place in the text it was set from and the face it was set in: the first of the faces given that has its grapheme cluster, its default ignorable scalars left out ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md#faces)), else a face the provider finds on the machine for the weight and italic. | tracker | analysis pictures from the decoder, read in the colour space `track --color-space` names where it is given one ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), around one frame, and a quadrilateral on that frame | the target's quadrilateral in media pixels on each frame before and after it, out to where it reports the target lost in each direction |
| OCR | an analysis picture from the decoder, read in the colour space `ocr --color-space` names where it is given ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), and the recognition languages | recognised lines: each a text and a rectangle in media pixels |

- A quadrilateral is four points in media pixels: the target's top left, top right, bottom right and bottom left corners, in that order. A rectangle is given to a tracker as its four corners. A tracker that follows only position and size gives rectangles as quadrilaterals. `track` prints them as track data, to which resolve fits the similarity motion and from which evaluation computes the homography ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#the-track)), so a tracker that follows perspective drives a corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#a-tracked-corner-pin)).
- The recognition languages are a list of BCP 47 tags in order of preference, from the `ocr` command's `--languages <tag>[,<tag>...]` (for example `en-US,ko-KR`, [FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)). Without it, the provider detects the language of each picture. A provider refuses before it starts on a tag it cannot recognise, naming it. `vision` passes the list as Vision's recognition languages, and detects the language when there is none.
- The OCR provider is called by the `ocr` command alone. Resolve calls none, since a document names a place by a rectangle or a clip ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), and a place found by its text is written as the rectangle `ocr` gives ([FRAMES-25](../FRAMES-25/SPEC.md#tools)).
- A provider's result is written in the document as a value: a rect area for a line `ocr` recognised ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), and track data for a target `track` followed ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#track-data)). Resolve calls neither provider, so what a tracker or recognizer run outside frames finds comes in through the document the same way.
- An OCR line's rectangle hugs its text from its highest to its lowest ink, not the font's line box, and `vision` gets it by cutting Vision's line rectangle, width kept, to the rows inside it that hold ink: a pixel farther from the median colour of the rectangle's edge than half the farthest pixel there.
- A provider that fails (it cannot run, or it gives an error for a picture) stops the command, naming what it was asked for: the place in the document or the command's option that asked, the media file and its media time, then the provider's own message.
- What makes one line is the OCR provider's, so two providers can split or join a line differently. What a tracker follows is the tracker's, and two trackers can lose a target on different frames, so the track data of one is not the other's.
- `--shaper`, `--tracker` and `--ocr` pick each provider by the name of its implementation. Shaping defaults to `coretext`, the tracker to `point` and OCR to `vision`. The `point` tracker reports similarity images of its target, and the `planar` tracker reports its image under a homography on each frame, so it follows perspective ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#a-tracked-corner-pin)).

## Interactions

Roles take what [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md) gives, and a renderer draws a corner pin as [FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-a-renderer-holds) holds. Working values, colour spaces, tone mapping, output encoding and analysis pictures are [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md). Resolve calls the shaping provider for text layout ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), and the analysis commands ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)) call the tracker, `track`, and OCR, `ocr`, whose results a document writes as track data ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md#track-data)) and rect areas ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)). The pan law is [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md). The buses the mixer sums, and the loudness meter that measures the mix, are [FRAMES-28](../FRAMES-28/SPEC.md).

The exposure a renderer averages and the samples it is given are [FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md).

## Alternatives considered

Roles and providers loaded at run time as plugins, or run as processes outside frames: implementations are built into frames and named by option. A provider's result can be written in the document as a value, a rect area or track data, so a tracker or recognizer outside frames gives its result through the document and needs no place inside frames.

A renderer that draws shapes as primitives of its own (a box, an arrow): paths with joins, caps, trim and arrowheads, and masks as feathered paths, are what Core Graphics, Skia and SVG all draw, so any renderer maps them without a frames-specific shape.

A tracker that reports a rectangle: a rectangle carries neither rotation nor perspective. A quadrilateral carries both, and a corner pin reads perspective from it ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md)).

One chroma upsampling filter fixed for every decoder: a platform's video decoder upsamples chroma inside its own conversion to R'G'B', and a fixed filter would leave every decoder to take Y'CbCr planes and convert them itself. The difference lies at edges of colour, so the filter is left to the decoder and the difference is named.

One PDF renderer fixed for every decoder: how a page's content becomes a picture beyond what [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages) fixes is each 2D library's own (Core Graphics, Skia, pdfium), and fixing it would leave a decoder to write a PDF renderer itself. So it is left to the decoder and the difference is named, as chroma upsampling is.

Every renderer, and a decoder's conversion on the GPU, held to the bits of `quartz` and of the conversion on the CPU: a GPU's single precision arithmetic and its power function differ from double precision in the last bit of a half float on a small part of the pixels, so the same bits would keep every picture's conversion on the CPU, which was 84 % of a render's time measured on a Display P3 screen recording. A role that gives the same bytes on every run keeps a render repeatable, and a tolerance against the reference is what a GPU path is checked by, as other editors let a user choose a GPU or a software renderer.

A second renderer built as an AVFoundation custom video compositor: AVFoundation picks the frames it hands a compositor by time and not by index ([Rules](#rules)), puts its own colour management between the decoder and the compositor, and would join the decoder, the renderer and the encoder into one object, so `metal` takes display lists and pictures from the decoder as `quartz` does.

A `metal` renderer that refuses the items it has no GPU operation for: a renderer refuses only what it cannot draw ([Rules](#rules)), and a list that renders on `quartz` and not on `metal` would make the choice of renderer change which documents render. Drawing those items by the shared Core Graphics drawing into layers keeps every list drawable on both, and makes moving an item to the GPU a change of speed alone, held to the same tolerances.

Resampling left to each renderer's 2D library, Core Graphics drawing at `medium` interpolation for `quartz`: measured on the reference machine, Core Graphics filters a picture it shrinks by a filter of its own, places a picture it enlarges about a tenth of a source pixel from where M takes it, and where a picture's own antialiased edge and its cut to `dest` meet, as they do on every conformed picture, multiplies the two coverages, so a pixel covered 0.624 came out 0.388 and the edge lay a quarter of a pixel inward. Core Image's bilinear sampling differed from it by up to 0.44 in a component on a detailed still scaled by 0.625 and 1.375. A reference made of one library's accidents is met by no other renderer and is not what the document says, so the definition is fixed and both renderers resample by one kernel held to it.

The definition of the corner pin ([FRAMES-27](../FRAMES-27/SPEC.md#what-a-renderer-holds)) for every picture, the mean of the bilinear reconstruction over each output pixel's image: at a picture's own size on whole pixels it is not the picture's pixels but a blur of 1/8, 3/4 and 1/8 along each axis, its edge ramps over one source pixel where a picture's edge is the geometric edge of its `dest`, and its warp's sixteen trilinear reads a pixel cost 17 to 23 ms a frame at 1024 by 1024. An affine map gives every pixel of a picture the same footprint, so the box of this definition is computed exactly from the source pixels it covers, which a homography's footprint, changing from pixel to pixel, does not allow.

Bicubic or Lanczos reconstruction of an enlarged picture, as compositors offer for quality: it is sharper, but it rings at hard edges and gives values outside the source's range on flat colour, as on a screen recording, and reads sixteen or more pixels where the bilinear one reads four. A box pyramid read trilinearly for a shrunk picture, as a GPU's mipmaps are: its levels halve, so a picture shrunk by 0.625 blends two levels whose pixels lie off the output's, a blur that depends on the scale's distance from a power of two, where the exact box is a few more reads. Bilinear enlargement and the area mean for shrinking are what image libraries call area resampling, and they keep a picture at its own size its own pixels.

A decoder that gives a picture converted on the GPU as a bitmap, its values copied out to memory: measured on the reference machine, the copy cost a 1206 by 2622 frame 2.3 ms against 0.7 ms for its conversion, and a surface is read in place by every role that reads pixels.

Taking every video's frames from AVAssetReader as `16-bit float RGBA`, so that VideoToolbox's conversion takes Y'CbCr to R'G'B' with its own chroma filter: VideoToolbox converts each frame on the GPU itself, on the GPU the decoder's conversion runs on, before the decoder converts it again, and its decoding service holds a frame of four half floats a pixel, 8 bytes against 1.5 at 8-bit 4:2:0, for each frame it keeps, measured on the reference machine as about 335 MB for each reader decoding a video of 1206 by 2622 pixels against about 127 MB for the same reader given 8-bit 4:2:0 ([Notes](#notes)).

Learning an H.264 stream's depth and chroma sampling from what the platform states, in place of its first sequence parameter set: measured on the reference machine, the format description of an H.264 track carries no depth and no chroma sampling, where that of HEVC carries its depth, and the decompression session VideoToolbox makes for it lists no pixel format, where that of ProRes lists those it gives. A reader given no pixel format gives the stream's own, `420v` for 8-bit 4:2:0 and `p420` for HEVC of 10 bits, a packed form the conversion does not read, but only once it has decoded a frame, so the reader that reads the video would be the second opened on it. Core Media gives the parameter set, and the decoder reads its first fields.

Closing the reader of the video read longest ago, alone, with no turns: videos read in a cycle past the bound then each close the reader the next one needs, so each of their frames opens a reader again. Measured on the reference machine, a render of 64 videos of 320 by 180 pixels shown at once, 240 frames, took 73.1 s that way, 31 s with a reader open on each and 25.0 s with turns, which opened 515 readers again and decoded each of the 15360 frames it read once.

A page's region cut from a larger draw, or stitched from tiles: a cut costs the larger draw, and tiles drawn alone differ from one draw at their seams and inside them, where thin strokes are covered differently, so a picture is one draw of the one region its frame decides.

Reading and writing through AVFoundation's awaited calls, so no thread waits: every role is called synchronously by the render's thread, so each awaited call would be waited on by a thread all the same, and waits on threads the process makes, fewer at once than the machine has processors, leave free every thread AVFoundation's work needs.

Reading ahead up to 32 frames at once, half the 64 reads at which AVFoundation's reads stop finishing, in place of fewer than the machine has processors: measured on the reference machine, renders of 16 and 24 videos of 1206 by 2622 pixels shown at once took 31.3 and 70.7 s reading up to 10 frames ahead and 30.8 and 70.0 s reading up to 32, since the GPU converts the frames one after another however many are read at once, and with up to 32 a render of 64 small videos shown at once had a reader give no frame.

Converting frames on several Metal command queues in place of one: measured on the reference machine, a render of 24 such videos took 131 s with four queues against 121 s with one, and 129 s of system time against 90 s.

## Notes

**Note.** A renderer may draw sample lists that are equal once, draw the items that are the same in every sample from the bottom of the list up once and go on from that picture in each sample, and raster a nested list that is the same in several samples once, since each gives the same picture ([FRAMES-29](../FRAMES-29/SPEC.md)). Evaluation writes every sample either way.

**Note.** An HDR file frames writes carries no HDR10 static metadata (the SMPTE ST 2086 mastering display colour volume, MaxCLL and MaxFALL): frames knows a PQ master's mastering peak, its `format.peak` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#sequence-colour-space)), but not the mastering display's primaries, white point and minimum luminance that ST 2086 also names, and MaxCLL and MaxFALL need a second pass over every frame before the file is written. A player takes its own defaults where they are missing, and a delivery that must carry them is made from the master with another tool.

**Note.** Two renders of one document to an `.mp4` on the reference machine, by the same decoder, renderer and conversion, gave files of the same length that differed in the creation and modification times of the movie, track and media headers, which AVAssetWriter sets to the time it writes them and takes no value for, and in two bytes of the user data message the hardware H.264 encoder writes into the first frame, which it sets itself. So a file's bytes differ between runs whatever frames writes into its headers, and only its frames before encoding are the same.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine: AVFoundation's H.264 encoder accepts PQ tags but writes 8 bits, which is why HDR is never written as H.264, and it writes HEVC Main 10 in PQ and HLG with the right tags from half-float buffers. ImageIO writes 16-bit PQ, HLG and P3 PNGs with the right ICC profile.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine with the `avfoundation` decoder: a page of 400 by 300 points drawn at scale 3.3 as four tiles at whole pixel origins of the same grid, compared with one draw of the whole page, gives the same pixels for text set from an embedded font, differs by up to 3.2e-3 on an axial gradient and 3.9e-3 (1/255) on filled curves, and by up to 0.208 on strokes 0.1 and 0.35 points wide, on 7 % of the pixels and mostly away from the seams (965 of 94740 within 1 pixel of one). A region whose origin is half a pixel off the grid differs by up to 0.996, since it is resampled. Three processes drawing the same page, scale and region gave the same hash.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine with the `avfoundation` decoder: a dense page (1920 by 1080 points, 50,000 paths about 30 points across and 2,000 runs of text in 4 fonts) costs on the order of a second per draw, 1.9 s whole at scale 2 (3840 by 2160 pixels), 1.1 s for a region at a 10 times zoom and 1.0 s at scale 1, most of it a fixed cost of about 20 microseconds per object, and paths that span the page cost up to 58 s. A page at a scale or region not drawn before is a new draw, so every frame of a scale animation over a dense page draws it again, far slower than a 4K ProRes 4444 frame decodes, and cannot play live. A decoder may draw frames in parallel from one document: 16 draws in parallel from one shared document gave the serial draw's pixels, at about 4 times its throughput on 10 cores.

**Note.** The `quartz` renderer places a region on a page's grid in double-precision numbers, which hold every whole number up to 2⁵³, so it asks for a page at a scale of at most 2⁵³ divided by the longer side of the page in media pixels. A page shown at a larger scale is drawn from a picture at that scale, resampled ([Rules](#rules)).

**Note.** The `planar` tracker reports its target lost on the first frame where too little of it matches the target's own frame: where something covers above about a third of it, where it has no texture to align, and where it moves between two frames farther than its alignment reaches. Measured on 640 by 360 H.264 video of a textured poster in perspective with a bar growing over the target from its left or its top edge, the target was followed to 30 % covered and lost at 32 %, within 0.3 pixels on every frame followed, and a target of one flat grey was lost on the first frame after its own.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine (10 processors, macOS 27.2): with every thread of Swift's shared pool blocked on a semaphore, one AVAssetReader read of a small video on a thread of its own did not finish in six minutes, and with one of the ten free it finished in 0.02 s. Sixty reads at once on a concurrent Dispatch queue held 60 of its 64 threads, AVFoundation's waits to make sample data ready the other four, and none finished, and with 64 threads of such a queue blocked one read on a thread of its own waited until they were freed. Eighty reads on eighty serial queues finished in 0.3 s. A hundred reads on threads of their own finished in 0.5 s once, and once held 64 threads in those waits and none finished. VideoToolbox made forty H.264 or HEVC compression sessions of 1920 by 1080 at once and encoded a frame with each, 31 of them on a hardware encoder.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine (10 processors, 16 GB of memory, macOS 27.2) with H.264 videos of 1206 by 2622 pixels and 258 MB each, read as `16-bit float RGBA`: while 64 AVAssetReaders decoded in one process, a 65th failed its first frame with AVFoundation's error -11821, Cannot Decode, and each reader decoding held about 35 MB in the process and about 335 MB in VideoToolbox's decoding service, which held about 127 MB for a reader of the same video read as 8-bit 4:2:0 YUV. Reading a file in chunks through FileHandle keeps each chunk until the reading thread drains its pool of objects let go of later, so a resolve that hashes its media on a thread that drains none holds every file it hashes whole: renders of 8, 16, 24 and 32 such videos shown at once peaked at 5.8, 11.3, 16.7 and 22.2 GB of memory that way, and at 1.6, 3.0, 4.3 and 5.6 GB with each chunk's pool drained. **Note.** Measured on the reference machine (10 processors, 16 GB of memory, macOS 27.2), VideoToolbox's decoding service held for each reader decoding a video, with 30 frames read and the last kept, in frames of the stored size in the form the reader gives: 26.8 for H.264 of 1206 by 2622 pixels and 17.5 for H.264 of 1920 by 1080 given as `420v`, 13.1 for HEVC of 10 bits given as `x420` and 6.4 for ProRes 422 given as `x422`, at 1920 by 1080, and, given as `16-bit float RGBA`, 13.2 and 12.5 for those H.264 videos, 13.0 for the HEVC, 15.0 for the ProRes 422 and 18.4 for ProRes 4444. **Note.** Measured on the reference machine with the H.264 videos of 1206 by 2622 pixels and 258 MB each, High 4:4:4 Predictive profile streams of 8-bit 4:2:0, read as `8-bit integer Y'CbCr 4:2:0`, 180 frames by `quartz`: renders of 8, 16 and 24 such videos shown at once took 14.9, 28.7 and 44.3 s, the process's footprint peaked at 1.1, 2.0 and 2.8 GB, the decoding service's at 1.06, 1.91 and 2.78 GB, wired memory at 4.0, 5.4 and 6.7 GB, and no page was swapped out, against 15.5, 29.0 and 59.9 s, 2.9, 6.0 and 8.7 GB in the service and 8.9 GB swapped out during the render of 24, read as `16-bit float RGBA`.
