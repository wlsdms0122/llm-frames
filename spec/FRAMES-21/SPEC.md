# FRAMES-21: Roles and providers

Status: accepted

## Summary

The four roles that read media and make pixels, sound and files, the providers resolve and the analysis commands call (shaping, tracking, text recognition), the working space and the files frames writes.

## Motivation

The engine is meant to be swapped, as an NLE swaps its playback engine or a compositing tool its GPU back end: Apple's frameworks first, Skia or ffmpeg later. Without a fixed contract for what each role takes and gives, and for what two of them must agree on (which frame a time names, what colour space blending happens in, how a mono source is spread over two speakers), two implementations would each be right and give different results. Fixing those is what lets one replace another, and keeping text shaping, tracking and text recognition as providers called before evaluation keeps every value a role sees fixed in the resolved document.

## Rules

Rendering is four roles. The renderer and the mixer take the two outputs of evaluation, the decoder reads media files for all of frames, and the encoder writes files.

| Role | Takes | Gives | Also |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| decoder | a request of one of two kinds, a frame of raster media (a video, an image sequence, an image) by its index, or a page of a PDF ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages)) by a region of its pixel grid at a scale ([below](#rules)), each with the colour space the place that names it reads it in when it writes one, and the target peak to tone map an HDR picture to, or none, or a request for an analysis picture; or for sound a media time range | for a frame, a picture of working values ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)), RGBA with premultiplied alpha in floating point, at the media's bit depth or more, an HDR picture tone mapped to that peak where its source peak is above it and display light otherwise; for a page, the region as a picture of working values, RGBA with premultiplied alpha in floating point; for an analysis picture, that picture ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)); or sound | the providers, the analysis commands and `transcode` read through it too |
| renderer | display lists | frames of working values, as pixels | reads pictures through the decoder, a transition matte's as an analysis picture, a page of a PDF as the part it draws at the scale it shows it at ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), applies filter ops, LUTs, blend modes and transition mattes, draws paths, masks, track mattes and glyphs |
| mixer | the mix plan | audio, as samples | reads sound through the decoder, stretches time with the pitch kept, runs each segment's audio filter, applies each segment's gain and pan envelopes |
| encoder | frames and audio, and the colour space to write them in | a file, encoded and tagged as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md) gives | `transcode` uses it with the decoder |

- **A page is asked for by region and scale, and a region is part of one picture.** A decoder draws a page of a PDF at a scale s on the page's pixel grid at that scale, which is anchored at the page's media pixel (0, 0): its pixel (i, j) covers the media pixels from (i / s, j / s) to ((i + 1) / s, (j + 1) / s). It is asked for a region of that grid, [i, j, n, m] in whole pixels, and gives that region as one draw, a picture of n by m pixels, so a region's edges fall on whole pixels, and what of it lies off the page is transparent. A region whose n or m is under 1, or a scale of 0 or less, is refused. A picture asks for one region, which its frame alone decides ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), and nothing stitches regions: two draws of different regions need not agree to the last value where they overlap, since a rasteriser's coverage of a thin stroke depends on the region it draws ([Notes](#notes)). The same page, scale and region give the same picture. The whole page of w by h media pixels at scale s is the region [0, 0, max(1, round(w × s)), max(1, round(h × s))]. A provider reads the whole page at scale 4, or at the largest scale at which the decoder holds the whole page where that is less, and what it gives is divided by that scale, so it is in the page's points ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)). `transcode` asks for the whole page at its `--scale` ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md#transcode)).
- Each role supports everything its input names (items, geometry forms, joins, caps, arrowheads, mask modes, track matte modes, filter ops, audio ops, blend modes, LUTs, mattes, media formats, codecs) or refuses before it starts, naming the first thing it lacks and its place. It never drops one silently. A decoder states the largest picture it holds, as the most pixels on a side, a constant of the decoder and the same on every machine. It refuses so a request whose picture's size the request fixes and that is larger (a frame of raster media, a page at a `--scale` written to `transcode`), naming the picture's size in pixels. The largest scale at which a decoder holds the whole page, or a region of it, is L / x, with L the decoder's largest picture side and x the longer side in pixels of the page, or of the region, at scale 1. A provider's reading of a page and `transcode`'s default scale for one fall to the largest scale at which the decoder holds the whole page, so neither is refused for its size. The renderer's request for a page fixes no size: it asks for the region at a scale of at least s that its frame alone decides, or at the largest scale at which the decoder holds that region where that is less, and resamples from there ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md#pictures)), so no frame fails for its size and no pass over every frame is needed.
- A renderer draws each frame's items as written ([FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md)): pictures with their rounded corners, borders, shadows and transition mattes; paths with their fill, their stroke's joins and caps, their arrowheads and their trim; feathered masks combined in order by their modes; track mattes by alpha or luma; and glyphs from the font files and faces named, at the positions written. It shapes no text and lays nothing out. An engine that only runs a filter graph over time fills the decoder, the mixer or the encoder, not the renderer.
- Every decoder keeps [FRAMES-1](../FRAMES-1/SPEC.md) and [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md), and frames are named by index. Frame i is the frame at `frames[i]`: a decoder checks a file's timestamps against the pinned ones and refuses before it starts when it counts them differently. So what a provider read from a frame (a tracked target, recognised text) and the pixels a renderer draws come from the same frame, whichever decoder renders. A decoder reads a video's nominal frame rate within a hundred-thousandth of a whole number n, or of n × 1000 / 1001, as exactly that rate, so a file's 29.999998 is 30 and probe, resolve and transcode see the rate it was written at.
- A sequence output lasts `duration` to within one frame. Frame k is drawn at sequence time k / fps for every k whose time, measured to the microsecond ([FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md)), is before `duration`, and frame 0 always, so the last frame is inside the sequence and floating point noise in `duration` adds no frame.
- Every file is encoded in one colour space and tagged with it as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md) gives: a sequence's output in the sequence's `colorSpace`, an analysis picture in `srgb`, and a `transcode` output in the colour space [FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md) gives it. A video carries the H.273 code points of its space, a JPEG, a HEIC and a PNG an ICC profile of it, and an `srgb` PNG an sRGB chunk, so every encoder's output looks the same in a player.
- `render` writes a master in the sequence's colour space: a `.mov` (ProRes 422 HQ, or ProRes 4444 when the format's `background` is not opaque, so the frames carry alpha, with 24-bit PCM sound at 48 kHz in stereo), whose 10 and 12 bits carry every colour space, HDR included, or a `.png` of 16 bits per channel, one frame of a sequence ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)), with alpha where the frame is not opaque. `--deliver` also writes a delivery file from the same frames in the same step, at the encoder's highest quality with AAC at 256 kbps or more, with transparent parts over black: H.264 for a sequence of an SDR colour space, and HEVC Main 10 for one of an HDR colour space, since H.264 at 8 bits cannot carry HDR. A sequence without sound gets a silent track as long as its frames, so every master and delivery carries the same tracks.
- A ProRes 4444 master stores premultiplied alpha and says so in its file. Frame k of a video is presented at exactly k / fps, where a rate within a hundred-thousandth of a whole number n is n and one within a hundred-thousandth of n × 1000 / 1001 is that NTSC rate, so 29.97 is 30000 / 1001 and frame times never drift.
- Every picture from the decoder to the encoder holds working values, one extended-range sRGB for every sequence ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md)). Every renderer takes each form-free formula in the form the list's `compositing` names and every other formula in its home, as [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#compositing) gives them, the filter ops included ([FRAMES-15](../FRAMES-15/SPEC.md)). Mask coverage and feather are not colours and are computed as written.
- No role leaves temporary files.
- The same display lists on the same decoder and renderer, of the same version of frames, give the same frames before encoding on the same machine, and on another one to within the rounding of its GPU. File bytes are not promised, since hardware encoders differ. Two renderers give the same geometry, text, timing and operations in the same working space, not the same pixels. Two decoders convert colour by the fixed formulas of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media) and give the same working values to their rounding, except where they upsample the chroma of 4:2:0 and 4:2:2 video, which is each decoder's own filter, so two decoders may differ at edges of colour there. The same mix plan on the same decoder and mixer gives the same samples. Two mixers run the audio ops by their formulas at 48 kHz in floating point of 32 bits or more ([FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md)), so their samples differ only by rounding and, while a value moves, by how often each computes its coefficients again. Every mixer mixes at 48 kHz in stereo: a mono source plays at the same level on both channels before its pan, and a source with more channels is downmixed by ITU-R BS.775, so two mixers give the same levels and channels. Each segment is then panned by its pan envelope with the pan law of [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md).
- Two decoders agree on what [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages) fixes of a page of a PDF: the page, its size and boxes, what of it is drawn, that nothing outside its file is read, its pixel grid, and the colour of what it draws by the formulas of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media), which shows on an opaque paint in Normal blend mode outside every transparency group, or inside groups each drawn opaque, in Normal blend mode and under no soft mask, whose colour spaces hold its colour, away from its edges ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media)). They may differ anywhere in how its content is drawn, not only at edges: anti-aliasing, transparency groups and blend modes, soft masks, how an image inside the page is resampled and gradients are each decoder's PDF renderer, as chroma upsampling is each decoder's own filter.
- The BS.775 downmix gives Lo = L + 0.7071 C + 0.7071 Ls and Ro = R + 0.7071 C + 0.7071 Rs, drops LFE, adds a single centre surround (3/1, the 4.0 layout) at 0.5 into both sides, and in 7.1 adds each side's side and rear surround at 0.7071 each. A source whose file names no channel layout is taken in SMPTE order for its channel count (3.0, 4.0, 5.0, 5.1, 6.1, 7.1), and a mixer refuses one with a channel BS.775 names no speaker for.

`--decoder`, `--renderer`, `--mixer` and `--encoder` pick each role by the name of its implementation. The renderer defaults to `quartz` (Core Graphics and Core Image), and the others to `avfoundation`.

The `avfoundation` decoder holds a picture of at most 16384 pixels on a side. It draws a page of a PDF with Core Graphics. It decides whether it converts a colour space of a page by the formulas of [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#media) by drawing a ramp of that space's values as it draws the page and comparing it with the formula, and it refuses a space whose ramp differs from the formula by more than 1e-4 of linear light ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#notes)). It refuses a written `colorSpace` other than `srgb` on a page that also draws in a tagged colour space: Core Graphics draws DeviceRGB and DeviceGray as `srgb` into every destination space and no call changes the space they stand for, and in one picture the untagged colour cannot be told from the tagged, so it reads a written `colorSpace` only on a page that draws in no other colour space, where the numbers it draws are the untagged colour's.

### Providers

| Provider | Takes | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shaping | a family, in a pack's font files or among the installed fonts; and a run of text, the faces it is tried in (the style's face, then those of the later families of its `font` list, each a font file and a face), a size, the style's weight and italic, and OpenType feature settings | the family's faces, each with the width, style and weight its OS/2 table declares, which resolve chooses among ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)); and the run's glyph ids and advances, each glyph with the place in the text it was set from and the face it was set in: the first of the faces given that has its character, else a face the provider finds on the machine for the weight and italic |
| tracker | analysis pictures from the decoder, read in the tracked clip's `colorSpace` where it writes one ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), around one frame, and a quadrilateral on that frame | the target's quadrilateral in media pixels on each frame before and after it, out to where it reports the target lost in each direction |
| OCR | an analysis picture from the decoder, read in its clip's `colorSpace` where it writes one ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#providers-and-analysis-pictures)), and the recognition languages | recognised lines: each a text and a rectangle in media pixels |

- A quadrilateral is four points in media pixels: the target's top left, top right, bottom right and bottom left corners, in that order. A rectangle is given to a tracker as its four corners. A tracker that follows only position and size gives rectangles as quadrilaterals. Resolve fits the similarity motion from them ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)), so a tracker that follows perspective gives a contract a corner pin can later read.
- The recognition languages are a list of BCP 47 tags in order of preference, from the global option `--ocr-languages <tag>[,<tag>...]` (for example `en-US,ko-KR`), or for the `ocr` command from its `--languages` ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)). Without either, the provider detects the language of each picture. A provider refuses before it starts on a tag it cannot recognise, naming it. `vision` passes the list as Vision's recognition languages, and detects the language when there is none.
- What was recognised is pinned in the resolved document as the areas it gave ([FRAMES-19](../FRAMES-19/SPEC.md)), so a resolved document needs neither the languages nor the provider again.
- An OCR line's rectangle hugs its text from its highest to its lowest ink, not the font's line box, and `vision` gets it by cutting Vision's line rectangle, width kept, to the rows inside it that hold ink: a pixel farther from the median colour of the rectangle's edge than half the farthest pixel there.
- A provider that fails (it cannot run, or it gives an error for a picture) stops the command, naming what it was asked for: the place in the document or the command's option that asked, the media file and its media time, then the provider's own message.
- What makes one line is the OCR provider's. A document can resolve on one provider and stop on another, where a line is split or joined differently, and the resolved document keeps what was found. The same holds for what a tracker followed.
- `--shaper`, `--tracker` and `--ocr` pick each provider by the name of its implementation. Shaping defaults to `coretext`, the tracker to `point` and OCR to `vision`.

## Interactions

Roles take what [FRAMES-20](../FRAMES-20/SPEC.md) gives. Working values, colour spaces, tone mapping, output encoding and analysis pictures are [FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md). Providers are called by resolve for text layout ([FRAMES-5](../FRAMES-5/SPEC.md)), tracking ([FRAMES-12](../FRAMES-12/SPEC.md)) and text areas ([FRAMES-2](../FRAMES-2/SPEC.md)), and by the analysis commands ([FRAMES-23](../FRAMES-23/SPEC.md)). The pan law is [FRAMES-13](../FRAMES-13/SPEC.md).

## Alternatives considered

Roles loaded at run time as plugins: implementations are built into frames and named by option.

A renderer that draws shapes as primitives of its own (a box, an arrow): paths with joins, caps, trim and arrowheads, and masks as feathered paths, are what Core Graphics, Skia and SVG all draw, so any renderer maps them without a frames-specific shape.

A tracker that reports a rectangle: a rectangle cannot carry rotation, and a later perspective track would need a new contract. A quadrilateral carries both.

Recognition languages in the document: they steer a provider, as `--ocr` picks one, and what they gave is already pinned in the resolved document.

One chroma upsampling filter fixed for every decoder: a platform's video decoder upsamples chroma inside its own conversion to R'G'B', and a fixed filter would leave every decoder to take Y'CbCr planes and convert them itself. The difference lies at edges of colour, so the filter is left to the decoder and the difference is named.

One PDF renderer fixed for every decoder: how a page's content becomes a picture beyond what [FRAMES-3](../FRAMES-3/SPEC.md#pdf-pages) fixes is each 2D library's own (Core Graphics, Skia, pdfium), and fixing it would leave a decoder to write a PDF renderer itself. So it is left to the decoder and the difference is named, as chroma upsampling is.

A page's region cut from a larger draw, or stitched from tiles: a cut costs the larger draw, and tiles drawn alone differ from one draw at their seams and inside them, where thin strokes are covered differently, so a picture is one draw of the one region its frame decides.

## Notes

**Note.** An HDR file frames writes carries no HDR10 static metadata (the SMPTE ST 2086 mastering display colour volume, MaxCLL and MaxFALL): frames knows a PQ master's mastering peak, its `format.peak` ([FRAMES-26](../FRAMES-26/SPEC.md#sequence-colour-space)), but not the mastering display's primaries, white point and minimum luminance that ST 2086 also names, and MaxCLL and MaxFALL need a second pass over every frame before the file is written. A player takes its own defaults where they are missing, and a delivery that must carry them is made from the master with another tool.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine: AVFoundation's H.264 encoder accepts PQ tags but writes 8 bits, which is why HDR is never written as H.264, and it writes HEVC Main 10 in PQ and HLG with the right tags from half-float buffers. ImageIO writes 16-bit PQ, HLG and P3 PNGs with the right ICC profile.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine with the `avfoundation` decoder: a page of 400 by 300 points drawn at scale 3.3 as four tiles at whole pixel origins of the same grid, compared with one draw of the whole page, gives the same pixels for text set from an embedded font, differs by up to 3.2e-3 on an axial gradient and 3.9e-3 (1/255) on filled curves, and by up to 0.208 on strokes 0.1 and 0.35 points wide, on 7 % of the pixels and mostly away from the seams (965 of 94740 within 1 pixel of one). A region whose origin is half a pixel off the grid differs by up to 0.996, since it is resampled. Three processes drawing the same page, scale and region gave the same hash.

**Note.** Measured on the reference machine with the `avfoundation` decoder: a dense page (1920 by 1080 points, 50,000 paths about 30 points across and 2,000 runs of text in 4 fonts) costs on the order of a second per draw, 1.9 s whole at scale 2 (3840 by 2160 pixels), 1.1 s for a region at a 10 times zoom and 1.0 s at scale 1, most of it a fixed cost of about 20 microseconds per object, and paths that span the page cost up to 58 s. A page at a scale or region not drawn before is a new draw, so every frame of a scale animation over a dense page draws it again, far slower than a 4K ProRes 4444 frame decodes, and cannot play live. A decoder may draw frames in parallel from one document: 16 draws in parallel from one shared document gave the serial draw's pixels, at about 4 times its throughput on 10 cores.
